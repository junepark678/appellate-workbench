## Rule 54(b) and appellate jurisdiction

1. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 54(b), current Judiciary rules PDF (rules effective through
   December 1, 2025):
   <https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf>

   Proposition used: in a multiple-claim or multiple-party action, a court may direct entry of
   final judgment as to fewer than all claims or parties only if it expressly determines that
   there is no just reason for delay. Otherwise the partial decision remains nonfinal and
   revisable.

2. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 58(a), same current Judiciary PDF:
   <https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/document/federal-rules-of-civil-procedure.pdf>

   Proposition used: a judgment ordinarily must be set out in a separate document. The authored
   record includes a separate partial judgment so the Rule 54(b) defect is not conflated with a
   separate-document defect.

3. 28 U.S.C. § 1291, United States Code 2023 edition, laws in effect January 3, 2024, GovInfo:
   <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title28/pdf/USCODE-2023-title28-partIV-chap83-sec1291.pdf>

   Proposition used: the regional courts of appeals have jurisdiction over final decisions of
   district courts, subject to statutory exceptions.

4. *Kinsale Insurance Co. v. JDBC Holdings, Inc.*, 31 F.4th 870, 873–76 (4th Cir. 2022),
   official published opinion:
   <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/211754.P.pdf>

   Propositions used: Rule 54(b) requires finality plus no just reason for delay; the no-just-
   reason inquiry is case-specific and tilted against fragmented appeals; the five *Braswell*
   considerations address claim relationship, possible mootness, repeat appellate review,
   setoff, and miscellaneous equities; findings should be stated; certification is reviewed for
   abuse of discretion. *Kinsale* dismissed and remanded without disturbing the partial merits
   order. The published opinion includes a separate concurrence disagreeing with part of the
   majority's finality analysis, so this set does not generalize the majority's claim-finality
   holding beyond its facts.

5. *McPherson v. Patton*, No. 23-1938, slip op. at 4–7 (4th Cir. Oct. 15, 2024), official
   unpublished opinion:
   <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/231938.U.pdf>

   Proposition used: a paperless grant of a Rule 54(b) motion and a partial judgment did not
   satisfy the express no-just-reason determination; the court dismissed for lack of jurisdiction
   and remanded. This disposition is expressly unpublished and nonbinding. It is used as highly
   fact-specific, official branch evidence, not as binding precedent. Binding support remains
   Rule 54(b), § 1291, and *Kinsale*'s published synthesis.

6. Fourth Circuit New Appeals & Petitions guide, page checked 2026-08-11, guide marked March
   2026:
   <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/appellateprocedureguide/Initial_Requirements/APG-newappealsandpetitions.html>

   Proposition used: the court describes a Rule 54(b) appeal as one from a partial judgment made
   final by an express no-just-reason determination and direction for entry of judgment. The same
   official page states that timely filing of a civil notice is mandatory and jurisdictional
   because the appeal period is set by 28 U.S.C. § 2107; that statement supports only the
   expressly assumed no-tolling/no-extension adverse branch.

## Initiation and initial requirements

7. 28 U.S.C. § 2107(a), United States Code 2023 edition, laws in effect January 3, 2024,
   GovInfo:
   <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title28/pdf/USCODE-2023-title28-partV-chap133-sec2107.pdf>

   Proposition used: absent an applicable exception, the civil notice must be filed within 30
   days after entry of the judgment, order, or decree. The authored appeal has no federal party.

8. FRAP 3 and Fourth Circuit Local Rule 3, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
   <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule03.html>

   Propositions used: an appeal as of right is initiated by filing the notice in the district
   court; the notice identifies the appellant, appealed judgment/order, and appellate court;
   Local Rule 3(b) requires the docketing statement within 14 days after docketing and attachment
   of any transcript order.

9. FRAP 4, March 23, 2026 official Fourth Circuit rule page:
   <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/Rules/Rule04.html>

   Proposition used: a private-party civil notice is due within 30 days after entry. The separate
   judgment was entered February 4, 2026; the March 3, 2026 notice is timely, with a March 6
   ordinary deadline.

10. FRAP 10 and Local Rule 10, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule10.html>

    Propositions used: the record includes original papers/exhibits, transcripts if any, and a
    certified docket; within 14 days after the notice, appellant orders necessary transcript or
    certifies none will be ordered. This record uses only written rulings, so no transcript is
    ordered.

11. FRAP 12, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule12.html>

    Proposition used: after receiving the notice and district docket entries, the circuit clerk
    dockets the appeal under the district-court title and identifies the appellant.

12. Fourth Circuit Appearance, Disclosure & Docketing Statements guide, official March 2026
    page checked 2026-08-11:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/appellateprocedureguide/Initial_Requirements/APG-appearancedisclosuredocketing.html>

    Propositions used: counsel appearance and corporate disclosure are ordinarily due within 14
    days after docketing; the appellant's docketing statement is due in the same period and must
    include the transcript order.

13. Fourth Circuit Transcript & Record on Appeal guide, official March 2026 page checked
    2026-08-11:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/appellateprocedureguide/Initial_Requirements/APG-transcriptandrecordonappeal.html>

    Propositions used: appellant orders necessary transcript within 14 days after notice; the
    district record contains the original papers, exhibits, transcripts, and certified docket.

## Briefing, disposition, rehearing, and mandate

14. FRAP 26 and Local Rule 26, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule26.html>

    Proposition used: exclude the trigger day, count intermediate weekends and legal holidays,
    and roll a weekend or legal-holiday last day forward. Three added service days do not apply
    to electronic service.

15. FRAP 30 and Local Rule 30, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule30.html>

    Proposition used: the appendix contains relevant docket entries, pleadings, rulings, the
    judgment/order under review, and other pertinent record parts.

16. Fourth Circuit Appendix Pagination & Brief Citation Guide, dated June 24, 2025:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/docs/pdfs/appendixpagination-briefcitationguide.pdf>

    Proposition used: the appendix uses continuous Bates pagination and brief citations in the
    `JA123` form. The intended appendix metadata starts at JA1 and has no inserted suffix pages.

17. FRAP 31 and Local Rule 31, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule31.html>

    Proposition used: ordinary civil timing is 40 days for the opening brief, 30 days for the
    response, and 21 days for the reply, subject to a court order. The synthetic opening brief is
    filed on the ordinary 40-day deadline measured from record filing; the response is early.

18. FRAP 34 and Local Rule 34, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule34.html>

    Proposition used: a unanimous three-judge panel may dispense with oral argument when the
    issue has been authoritatively decided or the briefs and record adequately present the issue
    and argument would not significantly aid decision. The authored disposition is on the briefs;
    the separate argument configuration is a counterfactual training configuration.

19. FRAP 36, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/rules/Rule36.html>

    Proposition used: judgment is entered when noted on the docket, and the clerk serves the
    opinion/judgment and notice of the entry date that day.

20. FRAP 40, March 23, 2026 official rule page:
    <https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/Rules/Rule40.html>

    Proposition used: with no federal party and no modifying order, a rehearing petition is due

    Propositions used: absent a shortening/extension, mandate issues seven days after rehearing

22. Fourth Circuit 2026 holiday calendar, live page checked 2026-08-11:

## Known legal uncertainty

- The latest GovInfo annual codification located for the two Title 28 provisions was the 2023
  edition (laws in effect January 3, 2024). The text is cross-checked against the March 2026
  Fourth Circuit rule and guide pages, but a release process should confirm the current positive-
  law text again at packaging time.
- *McPherson* is unpublished and nonprecedential. The authored defect tracks Rule 54(b)'s text
  exactly and is additionally grounded in published *Kinsale*, but the precise disposition of a
  future real appeal always depends on its record and controlling law at that time.
- Whether adjudicated and unadjudicated matters constitute separate "claims" and whether a
  district court abused its certification discretion are case-specific. The authored trace does
  not purport to establish a universal outcome for adequately reasoned certifications.
- The current workflow schema cannot encode the later-of calculation in Rule 41(b), motion-
  tolling under FRAP 4(a)(4), or the distinction between a legal holiday and a court-only closure.
  The trace file therefore records those as explicit expectations outside the executable resource.
- Rules and live local pages can change after the cutoff. Recheck the combined rulebook, official
  local rules, relevant standing orders, procedure guide, and forms before release.
