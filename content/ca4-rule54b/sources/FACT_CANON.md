## Contract and pre-suit chronology

- 2024-04-15: Asterglen and Copper Kestrel sign the Pilot License. It authorizes twelve identified
  terminals, selects Virginia substantive law, and requires a completed written change process
  for expanded licensed scope.
- Section 9.2 requires written notice describing a material breach and a fifteen-calendar-day
  cure opportunity before termination for breach or a demand for breach damages. Whether and how
  that condition applies to the disputed activity is a merits question.
- 2024-06-03: Copper proposes a written change order for twenty-nine additional terminals.
  Asterglen does not sign it.
- 2024-06-10: Asterglen sends written load-test conditions. Asterglen treats them as bounded
  testing permission that preserves the twelve-terminal license boundary pending a signed
  change; Copper disputes that characterization.
- 2024-06-14: Copper retains Meridian under a separate integration statement of work. That
  agreement does not amend the Asterglen-Copper license.
- 2024-07-01: the provisioning ledger lists forty-one BeaconRoute identifiers: twelve associated
  with the signed pilot and twenty-nine associated with the proposed expansion. Provisioning
  alone does not resolve whether operational use was contractually authorized.
- 2024-07-08: representatives of all three companies speak. Asterglen understands the discussion
  to permit only a bounded load test; Copper understands it as permission for current activity to
  continue; Meridian understands integration may proceed but does not claim authority to sign an
  amendment for Asterglen. No signed change order follows.
- 2024-07-18: Asterglen's compliance audit reports activity it regards as outside the limited
  pilot. The audit is not itself delivered as a Section 9.2 breach notice and states no cure
  deadline.
- 2024-07-22: Asterglen disables a shared staging credential, declares immediate termination, and
  demands $480,000 in contract damages. The writing gives zero cure days.
- Copper states it can return the active population to twelve terminals within three business
  days; later controlled test evidence supports feasibility within that period. Asterglen
  disputes whether the test reproduces live conditions and maintains that prospective correction
  cannot erase completed harm.
- Meridian continues technical work through 2024-07-25. Asterglen characterizes that activity as
  continued interference; Meridian characterizes it as orderly shutdown and preservation work.

## Fixed legal and evidentiary wedge

Rule 54(b) has two distinct requirements in this exercise: ultimate disposition of an individual
claim and an express judicial determination that there is no just reason for delay. Count I
satisfies the former. Neither the bare February 4 order nor the separate partial judgment
satisfies the latter. Asterglen's motion requested the right words, but a party's request cannot
substitute for the court's express determination.

The March 3 notice is conditionally timely if the February 4 papers are appealable, but timeliness
does not cure the missing final decision. Conversely, the counterfactual March 9 notice is late
when an otherwise valid certification and separate judgment start the thirty-day statutory
period. Those are separate jurisdictional questions and have separate disposition targets.

The Pilot License merits preserve competing evidence about authorization, notice, feasibility,
completed harm, and excuse. The supported-certification branch applies Virginia condition-
precedent law and affirms on its exact authored record. The actual jurisdictional disposition
expressly reaches none of those merits and leaves the January 12 order undisturbed in the district
case.
