Copper identifies no publicly held corporation with a direct financial interest in the outcome of the appeal. Copper's pending $120,000 amended counterclaim is its own asserted claim and does not create a separate corporate disclosure for another entity.

Meridian Silt Holdings, LLC states that it has no parent corporation and that no publicly held corporation owns ten percent or more of its membership or equity interests. Meridian identifies no publicly held corporation with a direct financial interest in the appellate disposition. Its status as a defendant on unresolved Count II is disclosed in the docketing papers, but that status does not alter the ownership statement made here.

Each party has reviewed the entities named in the Complaint, amended counterclaim, and Notice of Appeal. No additional affiliate requiring disclosure has been identified. These statements concern ownership and financial interest; they do not concede liability, jurisdiction, finality, or the merits of any claim.

The parties make these disclosures on the basis of their present organizational records and after inquiry of personnel responsible for ownership information. Any later corporate transaction that changes an answer will be reported by a supplemental filing so the Court can evaluate financial interests throughout the appeal.

## Counsel certifications and service

Counsel for each party certifies that the appearance information supplied to the Clerk is current, that electronic service is authorized, and that no known conflict requires recusal based on an undisclosed parent, owner, or financially interested public corporation. Each counsel will supplement the applicable disclosure without awaiting a court request if a relevant relationship arises.

The parties understand that an appearance does not waive a jurisdictional objection. Copper and Meridian preserve their position that the February 4 order and partial judgment lack the express no-just-reason determination required by Rule 54(b). Asterglen preserves its contention that appellate jurisdiction exists while acknowledging the Court's duty to examine finality independently. All parties also preserve their positions concerning the January 12 summary-judgment ruling if the Court reaches the merits.

This combined filing is intended to reduce duplicative administrative papers. Each party remains responsible for its own briefs, motions, and certifications. No counsel signing for one client purports to speak for another on a disputed issue. The parties agree only that the appearances and ownership statements above are accurately attributed.

We certify that this filing was submitted electronically on March 16, 2026 and served through the Court's system on every counsel listed for Asterglen, Copper, and Meridian. The filing contains no restricted material, requests no substantive relief, and does not change the scope of the Notice of Appeal.
