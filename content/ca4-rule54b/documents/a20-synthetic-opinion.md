PER CURIAM (fictional composite):

Asterglen appeals a separate partial judgment dismissing its contract claim against Copper
Kestrel. A tortious-interference claim against Meridian Silt and Copper Kestrel's contract
counterclaim remain pending. Before considering the merits, we must determine whether the
district court entered a final decision within 28 U.S.C. § 1291.

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 54(b) provides a limited path to final judgment on fewer than all
claims or parties. It permits the district court to direct entry "only if" the court expressly
determines that there is no just reason for delay. The district court must first determine that an
individual claim has been finally disposed and then make the no-just-reason determination.
*Kinsale Insurance Co. v. JDBC Holdings, Inc.*, 31 F.4th 870, 873–74 (4th Cir. 2022).

The January 12 order dismissed Count I with prejudice. Asterglen then moved for Rule 54(b)
entry, expressly requesting the no-just-reason determination and case-specific findings.
Appellees opposed and warned that a bare grant would be insufficient.

The February 4 order stated in full, as relevant: "Plaintiff's Motion for Entry of Partial Final
Judgment Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 54(b) ... is GRANTED. The Clerk is DIRECTED
to enter judgment ... on Count I." The separate judgment entered judgment on Count I and noted
that Count II and the counterclaim remained. Neither document expressly determined there was no
just reason for delay. No oral findings exist.

Asterglen contends the grant incorporated its requested determination. But the rule requires the
court to determine the matter expressly. The order neither incorporates the motion nor adopts its
contested assertions. A party's request cannot substitute for the court's determination.

The official unpublished disposition in *McPherson v. Patton*, No. 23-1938, slip op. at 4–7 (4th
Cir. Oct. 15, 2024), involved the same structural omission: a district court granted a Rule 54(b)
motion and entered a partial judgment without expressly finding no just reason for delay. The
court dismissed and remanded. We do not treat that disposition as binding precedent; its analysis
is persuasive because it follows Rule 54(b)'s text and *Kinsale*'s published synthesis.

Rule 54(b) otherwise provides that a decision resolving fewer than all claims or parties does not
end the action and may be revised before entry of a judgment adjudicating all claims and rights.
Because the express condition was not satisfied, the purported partial judgment did not create a
final decision under § 1291.

We therefore dismiss rather than retain jurisdiction for findings. Dismissal leaves the January
12 partial summary-judgment order in place; we do not vacate or review that order's contract
analysis. Count II and the counterclaim remain for the district court.

Because the dispositive issue is authoritatively addressed and the briefs and appendix adequately
present the facts and legal contentions, the fictional panel unanimously concludes oral argument
would not significantly aid the decisional process. See Fed. R. App. P. 34(a)(2). The simulation
argument configuration remains available as a counterfactual training mode, not as a docket fact.

The appeal is dismissed for lack of jurisdiction and the matter remanded for further proceedings.
