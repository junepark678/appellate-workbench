The district clerk certifies that the district court entered a supported Rule 54(b) order on February 4, 2026 but never entered the separate judgment required by Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 58(a). We address only the date on which the judgment is treated as entered for purposes of Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(a).

Rule 4(a)(7)(A)(ii) applies when Rule 58(a) requires a separate document but none is entered. In that circumstance, entry for appellate timing occurs when judgment is entered in the civil docket and either a separate document is later entered or 150 days have run from docket entry, whichever occurs first. No separate document appeared here.

The supported certification finally resolved Count I for this conditional calculation; the omitted item is the distinct judgment document, not a merits ruling or the required no-just-reason determination.

## Computation of Deemed Entry and Notice Period

The February 4 docket entry is the starting event. The day of the event is excluded. The 150-day period ordinarily reaches Saturday, July 4, 2026. Because the final day was both a Saturday and a legal holiday, the computation rule continues the period through the end of Monday, July 6, the next day that was not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday.

The judgment is therefore treated as entered for Rule 4(a) purposes on July 6, 2026. This result does not create a separate judgment on the district docket; it applies the timing backstop to prevent indefinite uncertainty when the required document is omitted.

Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 4(a)(1)(A) supplies thirty days after deemed entry because no federal party participates. Excluding July 6 and counting thirty calendar days yields Wednesday, August 5, 2026. That date is a business day and requires no further extension. A notice filed on or before August 5 would be timely under this conditional calculation. A later notice would require some independently authorized extension or reopening.

The district status certificate filed July 6 reports facts but does not itself restart or enlarge the period.
