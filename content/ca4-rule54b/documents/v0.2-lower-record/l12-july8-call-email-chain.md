## Conference Record

Date: July 8, 2024
Participants by role: Asterglen contract operations, Asterglen platform operations, Copper Kestrel pilot operations, Copper Kestrel routing operations, and Meridian Silt integration delivery.
Subject: status of twenty-nine proposed terminal connectors following initial provisioning.

The conference began with a review of the forty-one provisioned Identifiers: twelve associated with the signed pilot and twenty-nine associated with the proposed expansion. The participants discussed connector readiness, sample traffic, monitoring, and the next test group.

All participants agreed that the June 3 change order had not been signed. They did not agree on whether Asterglen's statements during the call allowed activity broader than the June 10 written conditions. Each organization prepared or retained its own account.

The notes that follow are arranged by source rather than merged into a single transcript. Quotation marks are avoided because no verbatim recording was made. Time references are approximate. Where the accounts differ, the difference is preserved rather than reconciled. Later emails show that Copper Kestrel sought confirmation and Asterglen maintained a narrower understanding.

## Asterglen Operations Note

The platform review showed that the X-series connectors were available for a broader measured load sample. Asterglen operations stated that the team could proceed with the next monitored group if Meridian Silt stayed within the traffic range and isolation method already supplied. The purpose was to obtain useful capacity data without committing to a commercial expansion.

Asterglen contract operations reminded the group that the twelve-terminal Pilot License remained in force and that the June 3 proposal still required signature. According to this note, the permission given on the call concerned continuation of testing, not ordinary routing at twenty-nine additional terminals.

Copper Kestrel asked whether the test could use current data rather than a replay. Asterglen responded that a bounded sample could be observed if its output remained isolated from dispatch decisions. The note does not record approval to rely on BeaconRoute output for general freight movement at an X-series environment.

Meridian Silt was asked to keep separate status and event records. Asterglen expected a results summary before any readiness decision. The note closes with three actions: continue monitored sampling, preserve written restrictions, and return the proposed change order to commercial review after technical results were available.

Asterglen's stated interpretation: oral permission for a controlled test only.

## Copper Kestrel Operations Note

Copper Kestrel recorded that Asterglen had reviewed early connector results and said the expanded group could move forward under monitoring. Copper understood “move forward” to include continued routing comparison across all proposed environments as they became ready, using current data where necessary to measure actual load.

The Copper note acknowledges that price and final paperwork remained open. It distinguishes commercial signature from operational permission and states that the technical group did not need to wait for the change order before continuing the approved evaluation. Copper did not understand Asterglen to require a new written approval for each connector.

Copper Kestrel further recorded that Asterglen could see platform events and would identify any unacceptable pattern. Copper took the absence of a direction to stop as confirmation that monitored activity could continue. The note does not say Asterglen used the words “permanent license” or “unrestricted production.”

Copper assigned Meridian Silt to continue configuration and to assemble a group-level status report. It also planned to keep the original twelve terminals as a comparison baseline.

Copper's stated interpretation: oral permission to continue the expanded evaluation using monitored current-data activity, with commercial terms to follow. Copper expected the permission to cover more activity than Asterglen later acknowledged.

## Meridian Silt Delivery Note

Meridian Silt recorded a direction to continue integration and measured traffic across the proposed connector set. Its note states that technical approval was given for the next stage, while contract signature and pricing remained unresolved.

The Integrator understood that Asterglen wanted event visibility, separate connector records, and the ability to pause activity. Meridian Silt did not understand that it had received authority to purchase or convey a BeaconRoute license. It treated Copper Kestrel as responsible for confirming the boundary between test activity and ordinary operations.

During discussion of current data, Meridian noted that some terminal feeds could not be evaluated realistically without contemporary inputs. Its account reflects permission for controlled ingestion and comparison, but it does not resolve whether resulting output could influence dispatch. That boundary was not restated in the same terms by every participant.

Meridian Silt listed four follow-up tasks: verify all X-series mappings; retain time-stamped event records; send Copper Kestrel a readiness list; and ask for written clarification if a requested step departed from the June 10 conditions.

The delivery note contains no reference to a signed change order. It characterizes the meeting as technical-stage approval and flags the written-condition document as still applicable. Meridian's later position was that it performed integration under a reasonable, bounded understanding rather than seeking to disrupt Asterglen's contract.
