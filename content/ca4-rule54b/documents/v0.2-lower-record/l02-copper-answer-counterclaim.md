## Affirmative Defenses

Without shifting Asterglen's burden, Copper asserts that Count I fails because Asterglen gave neither the written breach notice nor the fifteen-day cure opportunity required by Section 9.2. Copper also relies on Asterglen's provisioning, technical direction, June 10 conditions, and disputed July 8 statements as authorization or consent.

Asterglen rejected a proposed three-business-day reduction to twelve terminals. The $480,000 demand exceeds any executed expansion agreement and must account for avoided expense, mitigation, proper offsets, and duplication with claimed Meridian damages. Waiver, estoppel, and course of performance apply only as discovery establishes them. Copper preserves defenses supported by later evidence without pleading an unidentified defense in their place.

## Counterclaim — Parties and Common Facts

Copper Kestrel asserts this counterclaim against Asterglen:

1. Copper is the customer under the April 15, 2024 Pilot License, and Asterglen is the licensor. The agreement and its Section 9.2 are incorporated only as necessary to state this claim.

2. The original licensed scope covered twelve terminals. Copper later requested an evaluation for twenty-nine more and sent a proposed change order on June 3.

3. Asterglen responded on June 10 with written load-test conditions and then provisioned identifiers needed for the evaluation. By July 1, the system contained forty-one identifiers in total.

4. On July 8, Asterglen made statements Copper reasonably understood as permission to continue monitored testing. The parties dispute the precise words and effect of those statements.

5. On July 18, Asterglen completed an audit. Copper promptly explained that the disputed configuration could be reduced to the original twelve-terminal set within three business days.

6. On July 22, Asterglen terminated immediately and demanded $480,000. It afforded Copper no cure day at all.

7. The alleged condition was capable of cure. Asterglen's refusal to allow contractual correction disrupted Copper's routing transition and exposed Copper to service obligations.

8. Jurisdiction over this counterclaim exists because it arises from the same transaction as the Complaint and forms part of the same case or controversy.

## Counterclaim — Breach and Loss

9. Section 9.2 was a bargained-for limitation on termination and breach damages. It required a writing that identified the asserted remediable breach and allowed fifteen calendar days for correction.

10. Asterglen's July 22 notice did not satisfy that procedure. It combined termination with a present damages demand and declared both effective without an opportunity to respond through performance.

11. Copper immediately requested cure and described a three-business-day reduction plan. That period was substantially shorter than the contractual allowance.

12. Asterglen rejected or rendered impossible the proposed correction by maintaining immediate termination and restricting the access needed for an orderly reduction.

13. Asterglen thereby breached the Pilot License. Its conduct deprived Copper of the benefit of the notice provision regardless of the ultimate characterization of earlier testing.

14. As a direct result, Copper incurred emergency dispatch labor, temporary routing expense, customer service credits, and technical restoration and validation costs associated with the abrupt interruption.

15. Copper's total counterclaim is $120,000 across those four categories. The amount does not include the value of any permanent twenty-nine-terminal license and does not assume that the proposed change order was executed.

16. Copper took reasonable steps to reduce loss after July 22, including offering a prompt terminal reduction, coordinating status checks, and preserving information needed to separate test activity from other use.
