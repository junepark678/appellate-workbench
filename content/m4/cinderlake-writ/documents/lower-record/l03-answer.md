# Answer

**United States District Court for the Eastern District of North Carolina**\
**Rhea Calder v. Cinder Lake Health Network, Inc.**\
Civil Action No. SYN-EDNC-25-CV-0882\
Docket Entry 8 — Filed December 22, 2025

Defendant Cinder Lake Health Network, Inc. answers the First Amended Complaint. It admits only that Calder worked as a compliance analyst, raised concerns during June 2025, received a performance notice, and was discharged on September 12, 2025. It denies retaliation and denies that any adverse action occurred because of protected activity.

Cinder Lake states that its response to Calder's reports included an attorney-directed review and operational remediation. It does not plead advice of counsel as a defense and does not disclose the content of legal advice.

## Responses to Billing-Report Allegations

Cinder Lake admits Calder delivered written reports on June 4 and June 18, 2025, and participated in a June 27 compliance interview. ## Employment Decisions

Cinder Lake admits issuing a performance notice on July 18 and ending Calder's employment on September 12. It denies that either decision was retaliatory. It alleges the decisions followed documented concerns about collaboration, deadline management, and compliance with ordinary reporting practices that were independent of Calder's protected reports.

Cinder Lake denies changing its explanation and denies removing Calder from meetings as punishment. It admits that some responsibilities changed while the organization separated Calder from records under active review. Whether those actions were legitimate or pretextual is a merits issue for discovery and trial.

## Specific Denials and Reservation

Cinder Lake denies that Calder has established protected activity, knowledge, causation, damages, or entitlement to relief. It denies all allegations not expressly admitted. It reserves the right to amend its defenses as permitted after discovery.

The reservation is not a claim that privilege extends to underlying facts. Cinder Lake will identify responsive custodial records and witnesses, make document-specific claims where protection applies, and provide the information required for a privilege log. It opposes any inference that an entire subject becomes discoverable merely because nonprivileged facts are disclosed.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->

## Affirmative Defenses

Without assuming a burden it does not bear, Cinder Lake alleges legitimate nonretaliatory reasons for the challenged actions, failure to mitigate to the extent proven, and limits on remedies imposed by law. It also preserves objections based on relevance, proportionality, privilege, work product, and confidentiality.

These defenses do not rely on the substance of counsel's advice. Cinder Lake will not argue that it acted lawfully because counsel approved its conduct. It instead intends to prove its employment reasons through nonprivileged records and testimony.

## Requested Judgment

Cinder Lake requests that Calder take nothing, that judgment enter in its favor, and that costs be awarded as permitted. It requests no ruling at this stage on a particular discovery document and no categorical protective order beyond ordinary procedural rights.

The organization recognizes that the Court may later distinguish protected communications from discoverable facts. It asks that any such decision be based on the content and purpose of particular materials rather than a broad subject label.
