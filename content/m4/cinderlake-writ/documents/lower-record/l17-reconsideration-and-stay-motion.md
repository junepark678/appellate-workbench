Cinder Lake moves the Court to reconsider or vacate the categorical-waiver and seven-day-production provisions of its March 25 order and requests an interim stay. ## Reconsideration Standard and Narrow Request

Reconsideration is warranted to correct clear legal error and prevent manifest harm from irreversible disclosure. Cinder Lake identifies the premise that an announced corporate conclusion and remediation necessarily disclose counsel's advice.

The requested correction is limited. The Court may leave in place its denial of a blanket related-discovery bar, preserve public counterparts, and permit Calder to challenge particular logged entries. It should vacate categorical waiver and the command to produce all investigation files.

## No Privileged Communication Identified

The August 21 letter stated the fact of counsel's review, Cinder Lake's conclusion, and corporate measures. It did not attribute the conclusion to a communication from Rowan Vale, state what Vale recommended, reveal a witness communication, or expose legal analysis.

Rule 502(a)'s same-subject and fairness elements follow a disclosure that waives protection. The order instead inferred advice and then used the inference to open the files that might show whether the inference is true.

## Text and Context of the Letter

The public letter expressly says only that outside counsel reviewed Calder's reports and that Cinder Lake adopted identified remediation. Its conclusion is framed as the organization's determination. It promises cooperation through lawful requests and encloses no memorandum or interview record.

## Rule 502(a) and Fairness

Subject-matter waiver is reserved for circumstances in which an intentional disclosure of protected information would otherwise permit a selective and misleading presentation. Cinder Lake has presented no protected advice and will not use it to prove a defense.

Fairness can be protected by limiting evidence, requiring ordinary-source production, and revisiting waiver if Cinder Lake later relies on advice. Immediate categorical disclosure is not necessary to understand the public letter's complete text.

## Privilege, Work Product, and Document Review

Upjohn protects qualifying employee communications made so organizational counsel can give legal advice while leaving facts available. The complete memorandum and drafts contain counsel's legal analysis and mental impressions. Interview memoranda combine communications, questions, selection, and impressions.

The March 25 order does not determine whether factual portions can be obtained elsewhere or severed. It opens all twenty logged items and attachments based only on their general subject, bypassing Rule 26's item-specific distinctions.

## Fluor as Narrow Persuasive Analogy

In re Fluor Intercontinental, Inc. is unpublished and nonprecedential. Cinder Lake cites it only for its close factual analogy: an order requiring production within seven days after a company disclosed conclusions and general subject matter without revealing privileged communications.

The decision does not control this Court and Cinder Lake seeks no broader rule from it. Its reasoning nevertheless shows why irreversible production based on an inferred communication warrants reconsideration or a stay long enough for review.

## Interim Stay Factors

Cinder Lake has a substantial argument that the order mistakes corporate conclusions for protected communications. Production will destroy confidentiality before later review; a subsequent appeal cannot make Calder forget counsel's analysis. A short stay preserves the status quo.

Calder retains ordinary discovery, may depose fact witnesses, and can use public records. The public interest favors correct privilege adjudication while permitting legitimate fact discovery. No patient or public-safety need requires April 1 disclosure.
