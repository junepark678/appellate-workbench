## Rule 502(a) analysis

The public letter supplies no quoted advice, no paraphrase of counsel's conclusion, and no selective use of a privileged communication. The same-subject and fairness inquiries therefore lack the necessary initial disclosure.

Rule 502(a) requires an intentional disclosure of privileged or protected information before related undisclosed material can be swept into subject-matter waiver. The public words disclose that a review occurred and changes followed; they do not disclose the substance of legal communications. Without that first requirement, relatedness and fairness cannot independently create a categorical waiver across the entire investigation file.

## Communications and facts

Upjohn Co. v. United States, 449 U.S. 383, 389–97 (1981), protects confidential communications made to obtain legal advice, not the underlying billing facts. Calder may seek facts from employees, ordinary business records, and nonprivileged chronology evidence.

Upjohn leaves Calder free to ask employees what happened and obtain ordinary claim data. Sky Angel U.S., LLC v. Discovery Communications, LLC, 885 F.3d 271, 276–77 (4th Cir. 2018), focuses waiver on information actually revealed. Hawkins v. Stables, 148 F.3d 379, 383–85 (4th Cir. 1998), confirms that Cinder Lake must substantiate each privilege claim, but burden allocation does not fill a missing disclosure.
