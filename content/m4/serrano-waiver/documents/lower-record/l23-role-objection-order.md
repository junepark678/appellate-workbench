## Governing Standard

Section 3B1.1(b) adds three levels when the defendant was a manager or supervisor and the criminal activity involved five or more participants or was otherwise extensive. The government bears the burden of proving the adjustment by a preponderance of the evidence.

The role must include management or supervision of at least one other criminally responsible person. Management of property, assets, or activities by itself does not satisfy that person-focused requirement. See United States v. Steffen, 741 F.3d 411, 414–16 (4th Cir. 2013). Formal employment or direction of every participant is not invariably necessary.

The court must evaluate the relationship shown by the whole record. Knowledge, coordination, and personal importance to an offense do not automatically establish supervision. Conversely, a participant's discretion over details does not prevent a finding that another participant exercised managerial responsibility for a defined task.

The Guidelines are advisory, but the court must resolve disputed facts and correctly calculate the starting range before applying the statutory sentencing factors. The plea agreement left this role issue open and supplied no agreed enhancement.

## Competing Inferences

The defense characterizes Serrano's communications with Pared as requests among independent participants pursuing a shared goal. It argues that follow-up reflects coordination and knowledge, not authority. Because Pared retained choices and no formal command structure existed, the defense says the proof is at least evenly balanced.

The government characterizes the same sequence as assignment, monitoring, and correction. It argues that recurring direction concerning a defined responsibility is managerial even if Pared could decide minor details and was free to refuse. Probation adopted that interpretation in the final report.

Both parties correctly separate the numerical predicate from the role question. The existence of five participants does not itself prove that Serrano supervised one. Both also agree that management of assets alone would be insufficient.

The court gives weight to Pared's autonomy but finds the repeated combination of selection, expectation, monitoring, and corrective direction more consistent with limited supervision than equal exchange. The conclusion is narrower than a finding that Serrano organized the conspiracy or controlled all participants. It resolves only the factual issue necessary for § 3B1.1(b).

## Findings and Calculation Effect

The government has proved by a preponderance that Serrano managed or supervised Alina Pared as a criminally responsible person. The finding rests on the relationship across the protected events rather than any single communication. It does not rest on ownership, control of property, or Serrano's general importance.

Because the activity involved exactly five responsible participants and Serrano supervised at least one, § 3B1.1(b) adds three levels. The resulting total offense level is 20. With criminal history category I, the advisory imprisonment range is 33 to 41 months.

Had the objection been sustained, total offense level 17 would have produced a range of 24 to 30 months. That alternative remains part of the transparent calculation record but is not adopted. Other calculation components are undisputed.

The role ruling determines the Guidelines starting point; it does not dictate the sentence. Judge Venn separately considers 18 U.S.C. § 3553(a), defense mitigation, the government's recommendation, restitution, and supervision. Nothing in this order limits the court's discretion to select a sufficient sentence after correctly calculating the range.
