Mateo Serrano, through Celia Arden, objects to the proposed three-level adjustment under U.S.S.G. § 3B1.1(b). He accepts responsibility for knowing participation in the charged conspiracy and does not dispute that the offense involved exactly five criminally responsible participants. The objection recognizes that § 3B1.1(b) asks whether the defendant was a manager or supervisor and that the criminal activity involved five or more participants or was otherwise extensive. It argues that the numerical condition alone cannot replace the required person-management finding. ## Specific Role-Enhancement Objection

The proposed `Role-Beryl` finding states that Serrano managed or supervised Alina Pared during the conspiracy. The defense objects because the cited exercise episodes show requests and shared planning, not authority over her conduct. Pared is one of the five responsible participants, but participant status does not establish that another participant supervised her.

The defense emphasizes that the plea left role unresolved. Serrano admitted knowing participation from August 4 through November 2, 2025, and stipulated to aggregate loss and restitution of $186,400. He did not admit that Pared acted at his direction. The sealed summaries describe Pared as retaining discretion to decline, modify, or independently complete her part of the fictional activity.

Under the defense reading, § 3B1.1(b) requires proof by a preponderance that Serrano exercised managerial or supervisory responsibility over at least one other criminally responsible person. See United States v. Steffen, 741 F.3d 411, 414–16 (4th Cir. 2013). Familiarity, coordination, or a significant personal role does not necessarily answer that interpersonal question. Serrano therefore asks the court to find total offense level 17 rather than 20 and to calculate the advisory range without the disputed three levels.
