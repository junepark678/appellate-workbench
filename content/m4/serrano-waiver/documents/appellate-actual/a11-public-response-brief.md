## Rule 32 and Complete Denial

Rule 32(i)(4)(A)(ii) requires the sentencing court to address the defendant personally and permit him to speak or present information in mitigation before sentence is imposed. Jennings distinguishes a total failure to offer that opportunity from reasonable limits on the duration or subject of an allocution that actually occurred.

The transcript here fits the complete-denial side of that line. The district judge invited counsel to present argument, heard both attorneys, resolved the advisory calculation, and announced sentence. The court never turned to Serrano with a personal invitation. Because no opportunity was offered, this is not a complaint that the judge cut off a statement, confined repetition, or misunderstood something Serrano said.

The government therefore does not defend the procedure as substantial compliance. Counsel cannot waive away the personal address requirement by speaking about a client. The absence of an objection changes the review standard, not the nature of the omission. The first two plain-error elements—error that was clear under settled law—are satisfied on this certified transcript.

## Remedy if the Plain Error Is Corrected

When complete denial satisfies the plain-error test, the appropriate course is to vacate the sentence and remand for resentencing. A new proceeding gives the defendant the required personal invitation before sentence and permits the district judge to exercise sentencing discretion on a current, properly developed record.

The remand should not direct a particular term. It also should not freeze the historical advisory calculation or decide the merits of the dismissed role challenge. At full resentencing, the district court may recalculate the Guidelines, resolve preserved disputes, hear both parties, address Serrano personally, and impose a lawful sentence after considering the statutory factors.

The conviction should remain intact because the allocution error occurred after the guilty plea was accepted and does not undermine plea validity. Restitution, supervised release, the assessment, and other components of the sentence would be reconsidered only to the extent ordinarily incident to full resentencing. This remedy respects the narrow contractual carve-out while enforcing the remainder of the waiver and the May 5 order.
