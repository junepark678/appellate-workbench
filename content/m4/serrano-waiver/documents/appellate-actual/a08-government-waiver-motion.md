## Knowing and Intelligent Agreement

Waiver enforcement is reviewed de novo. Under United States v. Boutcher, 998 F.3d 603, 608–09 (4th Cir. 2021), the Court asks whether the government honored the plea agreement, whether the defendant knowingly and intelligently agreed, and whether the appealed issue fits the accepted scope.

The plea transcript satisfies Rule 11(b)(1)(N). Judge Elara Venn addressed Serrano rather than relying on counsel. She explained that the waiver covered appeals from the conviction and sentence, including an ordinary dispute about the advisory role calculation. She then distinguished the separately retained claim concerning a total omission of allocution.

Serrano confirmed both propositions in his own answers. He understood that preserving an objection for sentencing did not itself preserve appellate review. He also understood that the special exception did not encompass every alleged allocution defect or unrelated Guidelines claim. Celia Arden confirmed that she had advised him consistently.

Smith requires examination of the real colloquy and surrounding circumstances when validity is contested. United States v. Smith, 134 F.4th 248, 255–61 (4th Cir. 2025). Nothing in this record suggests confusion, coercion, an undisclosed promise, or a guaranteed sentence. The agreement was knowing and intelligent.

## Enhancement Target Falls Within Scope

Serrano's role target asserts that the district court erred by adding three levels and using offense level 20 rather than 17. That is a conventional challenge to the application of the Sentencing Guidelines. Section 3742 provides the familiar statutory framework for such review, and the waiver expressly reaches appeals of that kind.

The issue remains ordinary even though it was preserved below and would change the advisory range. Preservation establishes that the district court had an opportunity to rule; it does not undo a later-enforced appellate surrender. The plea agreement did not promise a particular calculation or exempt § 3B1.1(b).

The government's motion requires no protected factual account. The lower court found that Serrano managed Pared for a limited portion of a five-person conspiracy. Serrano disagrees. The contested inference is precisely the sort of case-specific Guidelines application covered by the waiver.

Nor can the role claim borrow the allocution exception. A possible future resentencing after relief on another ground does not convert the present enhancement appeal into a complete-denial claim. The Court should classify and dismiss the target on its own terms while leaving the reserved procedural issue untouched.

## Express Reservation Remains Effective

The government agrees that the writing's allocution exception must receive operative effect. Serrano alleges that the district court proceeded from counsel's advocacy to sentence without inviting him personally to speak. That allegation describes the category of claim the exception retains, even though the parties have not completed merits briefing.

United States v. Ashford, 103 F.4th 1052, 1056–57 (4th Cir. 2024), holds that a generic sentencing waiver can encompass allocution. The decision does not require the same result when the parties add an express exclusion. Here the special language distinguishes this agreement from the generic provision considered there.

Recognizing the reservation does not weaken the broad surrender. It demonstrates why enforcement should occur at the level of the target. The Court can dismiss the ordinary Guidelines challenge and retain jurisdiction to decide whether complete denial occurred, whether the absence of an objection matters under plain-error review, and whether Serrano has shown a real possibility of a lower sentence.

The government takes no position on those merits in this motion. Its concession is one of waiver scope only: the complete-denial target is outside the written surrender and remains available for the ordinary adversarial process.

## Dismissal Without a Merits View

The correct consequence of an enforceable waiver is dismissal of the covered target, not affirmance of the district court's person-management finding. Boutcher's threshold framework prevents unnecessary merits review once validity and scope are established.

Accordingly, the Court need not decide whether repeated requests and follow-up constituted supervision, whether Pared retained sufficient autonomy, or how the preponderance standard applies to the protected record. It should not describe the factual finding as correct or incorrect. Those matters fall outside the ruling requested here.

The distinction has practical importance. A dismissal records only that Serrano surrendered this appellate challenge. It does not dictate how the district court must conduct any later proceeding if the sentence is independently vacated on the reserved allocution ground. At a new sentencing, ordinary Guidelines calculation would remain a district-court responsibility governed by the then-applicable record and law.

The requested order should likewise avoid confidential information. The public record establishes the identity of the adjustment, the five-person predicate, the limited alleged supervisee, the alternative offense levels, and the sentence. Nothing more is needed for target-specific waiver enforcement.
