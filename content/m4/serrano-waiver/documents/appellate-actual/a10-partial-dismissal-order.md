## Validity, Invocation, and Scope

Under United States v. Boutcher, waiver enforcement is reviewed de novo and requires a knowing agreement, absence of government breach, and an appealed issue within the accepted scope. 998 F.3d 603, 608–09 (4th Cir. 2021). The government invoked the provision in its first substantive filing requesting disposition.

Judge Elara Venn complied with Rule 11(b)(1)(N). She addressed Serrano personally, distinguished ordinary conviction-and-sentence appeals from the special allocution reservation, and explained that preservation of the role objection below did not itself retain appellate review. Serrano confirmed that understanding in his own answers. The totality described in Smith supports knowing and intelligent assent. United States v. Smith, 134 F.4th 248, 255–61 (4th Cir. 2025).

The covered target asserts that the district court misapplied § 3B1.1(b), selected offense level 20 rather than 17, and thereby used a higher advisory range. It is a standard challenge to the application of the Guidelines and falls within the agreement's broad sentence-review language, including its reference to 18 U.S.C. § 3742.

Serrano alleges no government breach, and the record reveals none. The waiver is valid and enforceable against this target.

## Limited Dismissal; Reserved Claim Continues

The enhancement target is DISMISSED. This is a threshold disposition, not a merits affirmance. The Court expresses no view on whether the protected interactions showed supervision, whether Pared acted independently, or whether the district court correctly applied the preponderance standard. Steffen identifies the person-management boundary but need not be applied to the disputed facts here. See 741 F.3d 411, 414–16 (4th Cir. 2013).

The dismissal also does not dictate a calculation at any later district-court proceeding. It establishes only that Serrano surrendered appellate adjudication of this enhancement challenge in the present appeal.

The complete-denial claim is not dismissed. Ashford recognizes that generic sentence-waiver language may reach allocution, but this agreement adds an express reservation for total omission. United States v. Ashford, 103 F.4th 1052, 1056–57 (4th Cir. 2024). Giving that limitation effect is consistent with enforcing the general surrender against the role target.

The Court makes no present finding about the sentencing sequence, the effect of counsel's advocacy, the absence of an objection, the possibility of a lower sentence, or the relief that would follow an established error. Those matters remain for adversarial briefing.
