## Standards of Review

The Court independently reviews whether the waiver was knowing and whether a particular appellate demand fits within the accepted words. Boutcher, 998 F.3d at 608–09. Smith supplies further guidance when the defendant questions the adequacy of the plea-court explanation. Scope remains an interpretive inquiry directed to the agreement actually made.

The absence of a sentencing objection makes plain-error review applicable to allocution. Jennings, 130 F.4th 157, 161–64 (4th Cir. 2025), recognizes an absolute opportunity subject to reasonable control once offered. Muhammad, 478 F.3d 247, 249–51 (4th Cir. 2007), addresses total omission and asks whether another sentence was possible.

A reviewable role decision would ordinarily present legal application together with a clear-error standard for the district court's factual finding. Steffen, 741 F.3d 411, 414–16 (4th Cir. 2013). The panel need not apply that merits standard when disposing of a covered target under the waiver.

Finally, remedial analysis follows the claim that survives. Complete denial with the required effect calls for a new sentencing proceeding. It does not call for reversal of the conviction or an appellate factual ruling on `Role-Beryl`.

## Person-Management Boundary

Section 3B1.1(b) required supervision or management of at least one responsible person. Steffen rejects reliance on property, assets, or activities alone and places the burden on the government to prove the interpersonal role by a preponderance. 741 F.3d at 414–16.

The 2025 Manual governs because the conspiracy continued beyond its November 1 effective date. Its current formulation preserves the person requirement. The lower court expressly limited its finding to Pared, acknowledged her discretion, and declined to infer authority over Bell, Darius Venn, or Holt.

The defense response stored under `Role-Beryl` disputed whether repeated requests and follow-up amounted to authority. The government's version emphasized direction of a defined responsibility. These facts explain why the issue is a conventional application dispute, but they are not submitted for appellate resolution on the threshold question.

If the panel enforces the agreement, it should not endorse either account. A dismissal states that Serrano surrendered the right to obtain a merits answer; it does not establish that the enhancement was correct. This distinction also leaves the sentencing court able to perform any lawful recalculation should the sentence later be vacated on the independent allocution ground.

## Complete Denial at Sentencing

The critical sequence appears near the close of the sentencing hearing. Arden finished her reply, and Judge Venn asked whether counsel had anything else. After Arden and Ives declined, the judge announced that she would proceed to statutory analysis and sentence. She did not turn to Serrano or ask whether he wished to speak.

Only after every term had been stated did it personally address Serrano, and then solely to confirm that he understood filing-time advice. Earlier, the court had asked whether Serrano could hear and consult with counsel. That preliminary exchange also contained no invitation to offer mitigation. Counsel's extensive presentation, including her description of a prepared statement, could not satisfy a rule that gives the defendant a personal voice.

Jennings permits management of remarks after an opportunity exists; it does not permit omission of the opportunity. Muhammad treats complete denial as plain error despite the absence of an objection when the record supports the necessary effect. The transcript here presents omission, not an allegedly inadequate or restricted speech.
