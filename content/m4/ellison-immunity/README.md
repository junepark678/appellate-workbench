## Legal and modeling boundary

The exercise concerns collateral-order review of a summary-judgment qualified-immunity
denial after a welfare-check seizure. The fixed plaintiff-favorable assumptions exclude a
forearm strike, grab, flight, reach toward duty equipment, and immediate safety threat,
while including noncompliance and pulling from painful wrist control. Obstructed camera
views leave the competing historical account for lawful factfinding.

The actual authored disposition dismisses the factual-sufficiency target for lack of
interlocutory jurisdiction, affirms the Fourth Amendment legal ruling on the assumed facts,
and affirms the clearly-established-law denial of qualified immunity. It does not declare
that Ellison's account occurred. The adverse plan likewise dismisses the factual target but
assumes without deciding constitutional excessiveness and reverses only the
clearly-established/qualified-immunity target. Each side bears its own appellate costs in
the actual June 30 judgment and in actual-disposition mandate branches.

*Barricks v. Wright* was issued March 3, 2026 and first appears in the appellate opening
brief. *Zorn v. Linton* was issued March 23, 2026 and enters the later response/reply
analysis. Neither authority appears as a district-court source, and neither can itself
supply clearly established notice for the February 3, 2025 incident. Their later analytic
use must remain distinct from incident-date law.

The Rule 41 documents encode separate ordinary, rehearing, stay-denial, and
stay-grant/dissolution paths. Their later-of calculations, shortened intervals, and
August 10–11 dissolution/release sequence are exact branch consequences, not a general
scheduling engine or a single combined procedural history.
