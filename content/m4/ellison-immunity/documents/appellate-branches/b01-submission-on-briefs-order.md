### Scope Preserved for Decision

This interlocutory appeal concerns an order denying summary judgment on Officer Rusk's qualified-immunity defense. The district court identified genuine disputes about resistance and threat, then separately held that Rusk lacked immunity if the facts are taken as Ellison described them. The distinction governs the panel's decisional boundary.

Under *Johnson v. Jones*, the Court may not use collateral-order jurisdiction to reweigh the obstructed recordings, choose between witnesses, or declare that Rusk's account occurred. Under *Barricks v. Wright*, it may determine the legal immunity question while accepting the supported plaintiff-favorable facts identified below. The parties' briefs address both forms of argument. Submission does not merge them.

For legal analysis, the panel will assume that Ellison was an unarmed, distressed welfare-check subject; that she recoiled and pulled after Rusk took her wrist; that she did not strike, grab, flee, threaten, or move toward police equipment; and that she presented no immediate danger when Rusk applied one five-second drive-stun cycle while forcing her down. Those assumptions are not trial findings. The district court remains the forum for resolving historical facts if any claim survives appellate review.

The Clerk shall serve this order electronically. The previously entered briefing schedule is discharged, the appeal is ripe, and judgment will issue only with a later opinion or dispositive order of the panel.
