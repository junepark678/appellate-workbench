# Notice — distinction preserved from the challenged order

The January 9 opinion contains an evidentiary-sufficiency determination and a separate legal
qualified-immunity determination. It finds a jury dispute over whether Ellison struck Rusk and
moved toward his duty belt or instead only recoiled and pulled from painful wrist control. It
expressly does not find either version historically true.

The opinion's legal analysis assumes Ellison was unarmed and distressed; did not strike, grab,
flee, reach toward equipment, or pose immediate danger; and received a five-second drive-stun
cycle during a takedown. Rusk disputes those facts in the district record but accepts the need to
address an assumed-facts legal question when appellate jurisdiction requires it.

This notice does not convert a challenge to evidence sufficiency into a question of law. Rusk's
appellate filings will identify the assumptions relevant to each legal contention. Ellison may
contest appellate jurisdiction over any argument that seeks to reweigh testimony, draw defense-
favorable inferences from the body-camera obstruction, or resolve the strike-and-reach dispute.
The court of appeals will determine its own jurisdiction.
