# Joint chart — objections, assumed facts, and courtroom use

The parties preserve ordinary foundation, hearsay-purpose, completeness, demonstrative, and expert-
scope objections. Sergeant Lila Grant's custody materials and Crane's integrity analysis support
authentication. Authentication does not waive an objection to a misleading crop, altered speed
without a label, omitted audio context, or argumentative annotation.

For Rule 56, the parties agree a legal analysis may use specified assumptions without declaring
them historically true. Ellison proposes: unarmed and distressed; pulling from painful wrist
control; no strike, grab, flight, belt reach, immediate threat, or safety exigency. Rusk proposes:
active twisting, a forearm strike, and movement toward duty-belt equipment. Each disputes the
other's history.

Any courtroom demonstrative must identify source, playback rate, common-time alignment, and the
restricted-view period. A still image may describe what appears in that frame but cannot imply
continuous visibility. A transcript may quote audible words but cannot insert bracketed physical
acts not independently heard or seen.

This chart resolves presentation conventions only. It does not decide credibility, force
reasonableness, qualified immunity, evidentiary sufficiency, or the scope of any later appellate
review.
