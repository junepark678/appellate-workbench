## Scene history and source attribution

Ellison complained principally of burning at two contact points, soreness and abrasion at the left wrist, and pain over the left shoulder with lifting the arm. ## Treatment and reassessment

Cole cleaned the wrist abrasion with sterile saline and placed a nonadherent dressing. The two contact areas were cooled briefly and covered with dry protective dressings consistent with superficial skin care. No embedded probe, barb, or foreign body was found. The crew did not apply a traction maneuver or attempt to reduce the shoulder.

Ellison accepted a cold pack positioned to avoid direct pressure on the contact sites. The left arm was supported in a position of comfort for transport. Medication options were discussed under the service protocol; Ellison elected to defer oral pain medication until hospital evaluation. No intravenous medication, sedative, or restraint was administered by the medical crew.

On reassessment, pulse was 92, respirations remained unlabored, and Ellison continued to speak coherently. She reported persistent shoulder soreness and reduced burning. Distal hand movement, sensation, and circulation remained intact. No new neurological complaint developed.

Cole discussed transport with Ellison using short explanations and pauses. Ellison agreed to go to Alder Creek Regional Medical Center. The crew coordinated with officers so the transition would not involve simultaneous commands. Any police handcuffs were managed by officers; the medical team did not use them as a clinical device.
