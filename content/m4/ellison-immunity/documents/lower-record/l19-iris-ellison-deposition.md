# Deposition of Iris Ellison

Taken September 22, 2025; designated with Ellison's December 12, 2025 opposition.

Q. Please identify yourself and explain why you called for assistance on February 3, 2025.

A. My name is Iris Ellison. Mara Ellison is my sister. That evening I called 911 because Mara
was overwhelmed, had missed medication, and needed help getting through a mental-health crisis.
I wanted a medical welfare check. I did not report a crime, violence, a weapon, or a threat to
anyone. Mara was at Larkspur Court Apartments, 1844 Holloway Road, Building C, in Alder Creek.

Q. What did you tell the dispatcher about danger?

A. Dispatcher Lena Cho asked directly whether Mara had a weapon or had threatened herself or
someone else. I said no. I also said she was upset and might not welcome strangers touching her.
I asked for calm help and said I could remain available by telephone. I did not ask officers to
arrest Mara or force her into a vehicle.

Q. Are you offering a medical diagnosis?

A. No. I am describing my words, my relationship with Mara, and what I personally heard and saw.
I am not a clinician or police-practices expert.

# Iris Ellison deposition — condition before the call

Q. What led you to believe Mara needed a welfare check rather than ordinary conversation?

A. During our 7:30 p.m. call she was crying, speaking in short bursts, and saying she could not
settle her thoughts. She told me she had missed doses of prescribed medication after a pharmacy
delay. She did not say she wanted to hurt herself or anyone else. She said she wanted quiet and
did not want to go to a crowded emergency room. I became concerned because the call ended while
she was still distressed and I lived too far away to arrive promptly.

Q. Had Mara threatened an officer before that night?

A. Not to my knowledge. I told the dispatcher that Mara could recoil if someone grabbed her
without warning because touch sometimes made an anxious episode worse. That was a request for
careful communication, not a prediction that she would fight. I never used the words violent,
assaultive, armed, or dangerous.

Q. What was your understanding of Mara's location?

A. She had stepped from her apartment into the open breezeway outside Building C. The area had a
railing, stairs, and exterior lights. I relayed that location so responders could find her. I did
not say she was fleeing. She remained at the complex throughout the call and encounter as far as
I could determine.

# Iris Ellison deposition — the recorded 911 exchange

Q. Have you reviewed the dispatch recording marked as an exhibit?

A. Yes. The recording begins at 19:42:11 and fairly captures my side of the call. It includes my
statement that Mara was experiencing a medication and mental-health crisis, my request for a
welfare check, and my answer that there was no known weapon. It also records the dispatcher
repeating that information before sending the call.

Q. Did the recording contain a statement that Mara had struck or grabbed anyone?

A. No. Nothing like that had happened before responders arrived, and I made no such report. The
dispatcher asked whether Mara was presently with another person. I answered that she was alone
in the Building C breezeway. I gave Mara's clothing description only to help responders identify
her.

Q. What did you mean when you asked the dispatcher to “please go slow”?

A. I meant that rapid commands or unexpected physical contact might increase Mara's distress.
I did not mean officers lacked authority to conduct a welfare check. I hoped someone would speak
with her and help connect her to care. The exact words matter because the later characterization
of the call as a possible safety threat does not match what I reported. My testimony about the
call is limited to what the recording and my memory establish; I cannot testify about what an
officer subjectively understood after dispatch.

# Iris Ellison deposition — dispatch updates and travel to Larkspur Court

Q. What happened after the dispatcher said officers were being sent?

A. I stayed on the line briefly, then began driving toward Larkspur Court. The dispatcher told
me at approximately 19:47 that the welfare call had been assigned. I again confirmed that Mara
had not mentioned a weapon. I called Mara once, but she did not answer. I did not send her a
message warning that police would arrest her because I did not expect an arrest.

Q. Did you reach the complex before the encounter ended?

A. I arrived after officers had contacted Mara but before the ambulance departed. From the
parking area I could see activity near Building C, though people and the stair structure blocked
parts of my view. I heard Mara crying and saw her on the breezeway floor with officers close to
her. I cannot place every movement during the earlier fourteen-second interval from personal
observation.

Q. Why is that limitation important?

A. Because I will not fill a gap with what I think must have happened. I can describe the call,
what I saw upon arrival, Mara's condition, and what people said in my presence. The body-camera
record and other witnesses address the contact before I arrived. My inability to see the pivotal
moment does not prove either that Mara struck an officer or that she did not; those competing
accounts must be evaluated from the evidence that actually covers them.

# Iris Ellison deposition — observations on arrival

Q. Describe Mara when you first had an unobstructed view.

A. She was on the concrete breezeway with her arms being secured behind her. She appeared
frightened and was breathing rapidly. I saw no object in either hand. Officer Nolan Rusk was near
her torso, and Officer Priya Vega was positioned to the side. By the time I reached the lower
landing, Mara was handcuffed. I did not see her try to run after that point.

Q. Did you see an injury?

A. I saw a red abrasion around one wrist and later saw two small contact marks that paramedic
Mateo Cole examined. Mara complained that her shoulder hurt. I did not see a fracture or a large
open wound. My description is not a substitute for the medical record.

Q. What did Mara say in your presence?

A. She said, “I pulled because he hurt my wrist,” and repeated that she had not reached for the
belt. Rusk said she had twisted and moved toward his equipment. Their statements conflicted. I
cannot resolve that conflict by claiming the view I lacked. I also heard Vega say that she had
not had a clear look at the hands during the tight part of the encounter. I remember the meaning
of that statement; the recording should control if my wording differs from the captured words.

# Iris Ellison deposition — sounds and recorded timing

Q. You reviewed the body-camera recordings during discovery. What did you recognize?

A. I recognized Mara's voice and the layout at Building C. At 19:53:11 the recording contains
Rusk saying “stop pulling” close in time to Mara saying “you're hurting me.” Between 19:53:08 and
19:53:22, the central view is obstructed by bodies, clothing, and movement. I can hear commands,
a cry, and movement noise, but I cannot see hands reliably through that obstruction.

Q. Does Mara's cry prove precisely how she moved?

A. No. It is consistent with her account of pain, but sound alone does not show whether a hand
struck a forearm or moved toward a duty belt. Nor does Rusk's command establish that his stated
perception was accurate. The recording documents what was said; it does not automatically decide
the disputed physical facts hidden from view.

Q. What occurs when the image clears?

A. Mara is down, the conducted-energy weapon has already been used in drive-stun mode, and the
officers are moving toward handcuffing. The parties have identified a single five-second cycle
from 19:53:16 through 19:53:21, with the clearer view returning at 19:53:22. I did not derive
those times myself. I read them from the authenticated media timeline and use them only to locate
the portion I reviewed.

# Iris Ellison deposition — medical transport and contemporaneous account

Q. What occurred after Paramedic Cole arrived?

A. Cole assessed Mara near the ambulance, cleaned the wrist area, examined the two superficial
contact marks, and asked about shoulder pain. Mara was transported to Alder Creek Regional
Medical Center. I followed separately. She remained upset but answered questions. I did not hear
her threaten Cole, the officers, or hospital staff.

Q. What did Mara tell you before transport?

A. She said Rusk grabbed her wrist, she pulled away because the grip hurt, and she then felt the
conducted-energy weapon while being pushed down. She denied striking him or reaching for his
belt. That statement was close in time to the encounter. I understand that repeating it here does
not make me an eyewitness to the obscured movements.

Q. What injuries were documented that night?

A. The records shown to me identify a wrist abrasion, two superficial contact burns, and a
shoulder strain. Imaging showed no fracture. I helped Mara fill a prescription and stayed with
her after discharge. I observed soreness and limited shoulder movement for several days, but I
cannot assign a medical cause beyond what the treating records state. The seriousness of the
force and the legal effect of the injuries are questions outside my lay testimony.

Q. Can you say whether Rusk genuinely perceived a belt reach?

A. I cannot know what he genuinely perceived. I can say that I never warned of a weapon, that I
saw no weapon when I arrived, and that the pivotal hand movements are not clear on the video I
reviewed. I also can report Mara's contemporaneous denial. I cannot convert those facts into
testimony about Rusk's internal state. Nor can I treat the absence of a clear image as proof of
either account. My role in this record is a caller, family witness, and post-contact observer.

# Iris Ellison deposition — exhibits and points of agreement

Q. Which exhibits can you identify without relying on someone else's technical analysis?

A. I identify the audio as the call I placed, photographs as showing the Building C breezeway,

A. The call sought welfare assistance, reported no weapon, and preceded the officers' contact.

Q. What should a reader avoid taking from your testimony?

A. A reader should not claim that I saw the hidden wrist-and-belt movements, that a cry alone
proves compliance, or that a police command alone proves active aggression. The same caution
applies to snippets detached from their timestamps. My evidence supplies context and limited
observations; it does not resolve the disputed factual core by itself.
