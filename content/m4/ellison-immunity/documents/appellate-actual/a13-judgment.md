# Judgment

Entered June 30, 2026.

In accordance with the published opinion entered today, the Court adjudges as follows. The portion
of Officer Nolan Rusk's interlocutory appeal that asks this Court to reassess whether the district
evidence can support Mara Ellison's account is dismissed for lack of appellate jurisdiction. That
dismissal includes the request to derive a forearm strike, duty-belt reach, or immediate danger
from obstructed camera views, disputed testimony, and unattributed sounds.

The Court has jurisdiction to review the separate legal denial of qualified immunity on the facts
the district court assumed in Ellison's favor. On those assumptions, the denial is affirmed. The
assumed facts exclude a strike, grab, flight, reach toward police equipment, and immediate safety
threat, while including noncompliance and pulling from wrist control. The judgment does not declare
that this version occurred.

The case is returned for further proceedings consistent with the opinion. The district court and
any trial factfinder remain free to resolve the competing historical accounts through lawful
procedures. Each side shall bear its own appellate costs.

# Judgment boundary and administrative directions

This judgment does not affirm an evidentiary finding that Ellison never struck Rusk or moved
toward his belt. It likewise does not accept Rusk's contrary testimony. It preserves the January
9 order's conditional structure: qualified immunity is unavailable if the factfinder accepts the
plaintiff-favorable version, while the legal consequences of materially different trial findings
are not decided here.

The Clerk shall issue the mandate in the ordinary course under the Federal Rules of Appellate
Procedure unless a timely petition, motion, or further order changes that schedule. The district
court docket and counsel shall receive electronic notice of this judgment and the accompanying
published opinion.

The disposition is accurately summarized as: factual-sufficiency component dismissed; legal
qualified-immunity denial affirmed on assumed facts. Neither phrase should be severed from the
opinion's explanation of appellate jurisdiction, force proportionality, clearly established law,
and the post-incident status of intervening authorities.
