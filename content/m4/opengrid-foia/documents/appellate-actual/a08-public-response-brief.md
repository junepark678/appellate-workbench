## Review Framework

Jurisdiction arises under 28 U.S.C. § 1291 from the final district-court judgment entered January 2, 2026. The appellate record permits de novo assessment of the FOIA legal questions and review of whether the agency’s evidentiary showing supports summary judgment.

Agency declarations may establish an exemption when they are reasonably specific, logically connected to the statutory interest, and not contradicted by record evidence or an indication of bad faith. The agency need not expose the protected content to explain why it is protected. Functional descriptions and representative categories are appropriate where greater detail would defeat the exemption.

DOE also acknowledges the separate duties imposed by 5 U.S.C. § 552(a)(8)(A) and FOIA’s segregability sentence. It asserts that its declarants considered foreseeable harm and separability for each responsive record. Judicial access is governed by a different inquiry. Under Company Doe v. Public Citizen, a court must consider the source of access, alternatives to closure, and findings directed to the restricted material. DOE does not argue that an exemption automatically decides the access status of every judicial filing.
