## Seven(F) Demands an Individualized Danger Rationale

Exemption 7(F) protects law-enforcement information when production could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of an individual. The relevant question is the agency-supported consequence of disclosure, not whether a document uses vocabulary broadly associated with safety.

DOE grouped Role-Dovetail, Safety-Echo, and Channel-Fable under recurring danger predictions. The controlled context shows that their functions differ. Some placements may plausibly bear on an individual’s exposure; others concern offices, general references, or the fact of coordination. None of the inert words identifies a real person, channel, or safety measure, and no operational content accompanies them here.

EPIC v. Department of Homeland Security supports a nonidentifying explanation but does not make the individual-safety connection unnecessary. The thirty-nine fully withheld pages invoking 7(F) span several record families, yet DOE offered substantially categorical reasoning. Open Grid asks for renewed, narrower review, not a holding that 7(F) is unavailable for the collection. The agency should identify why the particular kind of information would create the statutory danger.
