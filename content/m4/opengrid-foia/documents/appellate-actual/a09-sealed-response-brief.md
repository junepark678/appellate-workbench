## Governing Review Principles

The January 2 final judgment supports jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1291. On de novo review of the FOIA questions, the Court considers whether the agency carried its burden through reasonably detailed, logically coherent declarations and supporting indexes.

Functional explanation is essential in a case where public repetition of the concealed relationship could defeat the asserted exemption. The agency is not excused from specificity, but it may state what a field does and what consequence disclosure would have without identifying the underlying content. The controlled positions let the Court test whether that account corresponds to the record.

Segregability and foreseeable harm are independent obligations within the FOIA inquiry. Court-record restriction proceeds under another body of law, including Company Doe v. Public Citizen. DOE does not contend that the agency’s ability to withhold a source record makes every litigation document inaccessible. It asks the Court to evaluate each question under its own standard and to preserve only those interim restrictions needed for four identified controlled counterparts.
