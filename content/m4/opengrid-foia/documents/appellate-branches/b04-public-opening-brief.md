## Exemption 7(E) Governing Principles

Exemption 7 applies only to records compiled for law-enforcement purposes. When that threshold is satisfied, 7(E) protects techniques and procedures whose disclosure would reveal nonpublic implementation detail. Under *Grey v. Alfonso-Royals*, the risk-of-circumvention phrase qualifies guidelines, while a protected technique or procedure need not satisfy an additional guideline test.

That distinction does not make every operationally themed sentence exempt. An agency must identify the technique or procedure and explain how the withheld material reveals it. A general subject, administrative instruction, title, or public description does not become a technique merely because it appears in the same record. A guideline requires a supported prediction that disclosure could reasonably risk circumvention.

Critical-infrastructure response records may possess a law-enforcement purpose when directed toward prevention of and response to criminal threats. *Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility* nevertheless requires attention to the particular information and claimed harm. The threshold does not collapse the remainder of the inquiry.

Open Grid accepts for this branch that the final Grid Sentinel exercise records were compiled for the stated preventive purpose. It disputes only whether every retained 7(E) segment stays within the narrow implementation and guideline categories preserved by the premise.

## Exemption 7(F) Governing Principles

Exemption 7(F) covers law-enforcement information whose disclosure could reasonably be expected to endanger an individual’s life or physical safety. The agency need not identify the threatened person by name. It must, however, demonstrate an expected connection between disclosure of the information and danger to some person.

Functional categories can organize the proof when they rest on document-by-document review and correspond meaningfully to the protected interest. *Zaid v. Department of Justice* does not permit a category label to replace that foundation. *Electronic Privacy Information Center v. Department of Homeland Security* likewise preserves a distinct segregability obligation after any safety showing.

The corrected premise improves DOE’s account by limiting 7(F) to four field types and attaching a causal explanation to each retained segment. The Court must test function and context. A reasonable expectation standard permits inference, not speculation. The question is whether disclosure of the actual retained information—not merely the existence of a cyber-response document—could produce the asserted human danger.
