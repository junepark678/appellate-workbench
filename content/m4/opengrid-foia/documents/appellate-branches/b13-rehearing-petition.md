## Exceptional Importance of the Combined Questions

Paired public and controlled records can preserve meaningful access while allowing a court to examine asserted sensitive placement. They also create a risk that sophisticated access mechanics will distract from the underlying legal questions: whether the content is exempt, whether public text can be separated, and whether judicial closure is justified.

The panel opinion addresses all three domains in one case. Clarification would help courts avoid four conflations. Law-enforcement purpose does not establish an exemption. One exemption does not prove another. A valid withholding does not resolve segregability. A FOIA determination does not automatically seal a judicial record.

The functional-category issue is similarly important. Agencies need not disclose a secret to justify withholding, and courts may accept coherent groups. Requesters must still be able to test what the groups do and how disclosure causes the protected harm. The line between safe abstraction and conclusory labeling deserves careful explanation.

Within this fictional exercise, those questions warrant en banc consideration if the panel does not narrow its reasoning. The petition does not assert that any real court has adopted the counterfactual opinion or encountered these synthetic facts.
