## Exemption 7(E)

ORER determined that the responsive playbooks were compiled and used for a fictional law-enforcement purpose: preventing, recognizing, and coordinating a response to unlawful attacks on the synthetic Grid Sentinel environment. The office does not rely merely on the agency’s general mission. It relies on the described function of the records and the custodian’s certification that approved editions organize prevention and response decisions.

Exemption 7(E) is applied to nonpublic decision conditions, the order in which fictional response choices are evaluated, implementation sequences, and conditional branches. Even where a general preparedness concept is familiar, the office concludes that the particular organization and application of that concept within an approved decision tree is not public. Guideline fields are also withheld where revealing a fictional threshold or branch would permit a reader to anticipate when the suite changes its response classification.

The asserted harm is that disclosure could enable an actor to shape conduct around the suite’s protected decision logic or frustrate an orderly response. This explanation does not reveal any protected value or sequence. The two governance records do not contain such operational details and are released in full. Portions of the other records that provide only general context are produced unless another exemption is asserted.

## Exemption 7(F)

The office applies Exemption 7(F) to fields that it characterizes as identifying fictional duty roles, response contacts, safety references, and escalation positions. ORER concludes at a categorical level that exposing those fields could permit interference with personnel performing protective functions or could redirect attention toward a location associated with human safety. Personal names and real contact information are not part of this synthetic collection.

The same rationale is applied to each record containing one of those field types. In partially released records, a 7(F) notation appears beside the affected blackout. In fully withheld records, the production manifest identifies whether the exemption overlaps with 7(E). The office does not identify a threatened individual by name and does not describe a particular incident; it treats the role and safety categories as sufficient to show an expected risk.

Some passages carry both exemptions because the agency views a fictional duty assignment as part of an operational sequence and as related to physical safety. The two bases are recorded separately even when the same words are removed. The determination does not invoke 7(F) for ordinary approval dates, version numbers, or general program descriptions, which remain visible where they can be separated from the protected field.

## Foreseeable Harm and Segregability

Reviewers considered whether disclosure would foreseeably harm the interests protected by the cited exemptions. For 7(E), ORER predicts interference with the effectiveness of the fictional response framework if a reader could reconstruct protected conditions and branch order. For 7(F), it predicts increased danger if protected role, contact, or safety-location fields were exposed. These predictions are based on the record categories and the custodian’s assessment.

The office states that each responsive page was examined for segregability. It released titles, dates, revision identifiers, general purpose language, administrative headings, and governance text when reviewers believed those portions could be understood apart from protected details. It withheld entire records when the operational organization was viewed as continuous and when separating individual words was considered likely to reveal the protected structure.

No further reasonably segregable information is identified. The agency’s conclusion is stated for the reviewed categories as a whole; the production manifest does not provide a line-by-line explanation for every retained blackout. The office will reconsider particular passages on administrative appeal if Open Grid identifies a concrete disputed record or administrative field. The foreseeable-harm and segregation findings are independent of the earlier scope exclusions.
