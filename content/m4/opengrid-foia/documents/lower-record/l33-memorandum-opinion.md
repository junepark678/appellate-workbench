## V. FOIA’s Disclosure and Segregation Rules

Summary judgment is appropriate because no material fact concerning the final production is genuinely disputed. DOE bears the burden of establishing its exemptions, and review is de novo. The Court considers the public declarations, functional Vaughn categories, paired exhibits, and controlled submission. Those materials must permit meaningful testing without quoting the information claimed to be protected.

FOIA favors disclosure but recognizes specified exemptions. Relevant here, 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(7)(E) protects certain law-enforcement techniques, procedures, and guidelines. Section 552(b)(7)(F) protects information whose disclosure could reasonably be expected to endanger an individual’s life or physical safety.

The agency ordinarily must also show foreseeable harm to an interest protected by the exemption. It may explain that harm categorically when the category is appropriately defined. The explanation need not reveal the information being protected.

The concluding paragraph of § 552(b) requires disclosure of any reasonably segregable nonexempt portion after deletion of exempt matter. Rein v. U.S. Patent & Trademark Office, 553 F.3d 353 (4th Cir. 2009), places the burden on the agency to show that it performed the necessary review.

Segregability does not require release of meaningless fragments. Nor must an agency preserve a heading or contextual cue when doing so would expose how protected information is organized. The central question is whether a remaining proposition has independent informational value without compromising the exempt relation.

## VI. Exemption 7 Law-Enforcement Purpose

The threshold question is whether the responsive operational records were compiled for law-enforcement purposes. DOE’s declarations state that the Grid Sentinel playbooks were approved to prevent, recognize, and coordinate responses to fictional unlawful attacks. The asserted purpose relates the records to prevention and response rather than to general agency administration.

Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility v. U.S. Section, International Boundary & Water Commission, 740 F.3d 195 (D.C. The Court finds that reasoning persuasive for the synthetic records described here.

The distinction between playbooks and governance instruments reinforces the finding. DOE released GS-GV-04 and GS-GV-03 because they record approval and supersession rather than organize a response. Administrative matter also appears within playbooks, but its presence does not change the compilation purpose of the operational record.

Open Grid accepts the threshold for these motions. The Court therefore concludes that the decision trees, escalation matrices, and coordination appendices are records compiled for a law-enforcement purpose and proceeds to the specific subparts.

## VII. Exemption 7(E) Legal Standard

Exemption 7(E) protects information that would disclose techniques or procedures for law-enforcement investigations or prosecutions. It also protects law-enforcement guidelines if disclosure could reasonably be expected to risk circumvention of law. The circumvention qualifier applies to guidelines, not to the separate techniques-and-procedures clause.

Grey v. Alfonso-Royals, 140 F.4th 173 (4th Cir. 2025), explains that implementation details can remain nonpublic even when the general technique is known. The agency need not demonstrate that the broad concept itself is secret. It must identify nonpublic content that reveals how the technique or procedure operates.

For a guideline, the agency must articulate a logical expectation of circumvention. It need not prove that circumvention will occur or disclose the guideline’s value. The prediction must be connected to the category and cannot rest only on a sensitive title.

These principles permit DOE to use safe public descriptions. The Court evaluates whether the asserted decision, ordering, and guideline relationships perform the functions claimed, while keeping any authentic operational content outside the open opinion. The inert labels in the controlled samples are evidentiary markers only.

## XI. Exemption 7(F) Legal Standard

Exemption 7(F) applies when disclosure could reasonably be expected to endanger the life or physical safety of an individual. The provision does not require the agency to name a threatened person. Electronic Privacy Information Center v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, 777 F.3d 518 (D.C. Cir. 2015).

The expected danger must still be connected to the withheld information. Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility cautions that a protective subject alone is not enough; the agency should explain how the operational information bears on safety. The burden remains DOE’s on de novo review.

DOE organizes the disputed information into duty assignments, safety references, and controlled communication categories. It asserts a common human-function relationship across the playbook families. Public notices identify those types without revealing a person, danger, or channel.

The Court evaluates the category as a whole because DOE states that its reviewers derived the group from document-level review. It does not require a distinct threatened individual or a separate factual scenario for each field. The issue is whether the stated function and expected interference provide a reasonable basis.

## XIII. Application of Exemption 7(F)

The Court finds DOE’s categorical explanation sufficient. The playbooks concern a protective exercise; the retained human-function fields associate assignments, safety classifications, and communication categories with that protective activity. Disclosing authentic relationships could reasonably facilitate interference directed at people involved in the response.

DOE need not identify an individual or describe a concrete threatened event. Its prediction is logical in light of the documents’ purpose and the field associations described in camera. The common rationale may be applied across the decision, matrix, and coordination families because the agency states that each retained segment was reviewed for the same functional relation.

Open Grid seeks a finer distinction among archival, distribution, and protective uses. The Court concludes that DOE’s assurance that neutral uses were released, combined with the sample placements and supplemental review, adequately draws that line. A more detailed public account could begin to reveal the asserted associations.

The Court therefore sustains DOE’s 7(F) categories. It reaches that conclusion at the functional-group level and does not require a separate harm finding for each role, safety, or channel field.

## XV. Segregability Standard

FOIA requires the release of any reasonably segregable nonexempt portion after exempt information is deleted. The agency must show that it conducted the analysis and cannot rely solely on the existence of an exemption. Rein, 553 F.3d 353.

Meaningful administrative text ordinarily should be released. Titles, revision entries, approval statements, neutral headings, and recordkeeping directions do not automatically become exempt through proximity to operational content. At the same time, context or placement can reveal an exempt relationship, and an agency need not disclose disconnected fragments with no informational value.

DOE’s final protocol asked whether a passage conveyed standalone administration, whether location exposed a protected relation, whether a heading remained understandable after removal, and whether neutral text could be separated from a human-function field. The protocol is consistent with the governing inquiry.

The remaining question is whether DOE adequately demonstrates that it applied the protocol and released the resulting coherent portions. The Court considers the declarations, the seventeen supplemental passages, the paired samples, and the account of the three fully withheld records.

## XVI. Segregability Application

The Court credits DOE’s statement that reviewers examined every responsive page and later performed a refined line review. That second review disclosed seventeen passages from eleven images: two passages on six images and one on five. The new material included administrative ownership, headings, approval status, edition history, archival directions, and general coordination.

DOE states that every remaining segment either expresses an exempt relationship, would reveal such a relationship through context, or would lack useful meaning after deletion. The samples demonstrate that the agency released surrounding propositions when it believed they could stand independently.

Open Grid argues that DOE reports many outcomes at the record-family level and does not give a public reason for every retained sentence. The Court does not require a sentence-by-sentence public log. The final declarations, together with the correction and in-camera review, sufficiently establish that the agency considered segregability rather than presuming whole-page protection.

The Court therefore finds that DOE released all reasonably segregable nonexempt information. The existence of a supplemental release does not undermine the operative determination; it shows that the final review produced additional disclosure when appropriate.

## XVIII. Disposition of the Cross-Motions

DOE has established a qualifying law-enforcement purpose. Its description of operational decision-tree triggers, ordering, branches, and implementation details satisfies Exemption 7(E). Its explanation of classification guidelines also establishes the required relationship and circumvention risk.

DOE’s categorical human-function presentation is adequate under 7(F). The agency need not name a threatened person, and the Court accepts the reasonable expectation that authentic field relationships could facilitate interference with protective functions. The final declarations and supplemental review establish segregability.

For those reasons, Open Grid’s motion for summary judgment is denied and DOE’s cross-motion is granted. The Court will enter a separate final judgment for DOE. The controlled counterparts remain governed by the sealing order.

This disposition resolves all claims concerning request DOE-FOIA-2024-OG-041. No claim remains concerning the adequacy of the search, fees, or production format. The Court expresses no view about a real agency record or cyber program because none is involved.
