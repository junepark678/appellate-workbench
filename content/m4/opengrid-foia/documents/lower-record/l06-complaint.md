# Complaint for Declaratory and Injunctive Relief Under FOIA

**Filed:** April 7, 2025
**Synthetic docket:** SYN-DMD-25-CV-0914
**Court:** Fictional United States District Court for the District of Maryland

Open Grid Archive brings this action against the United States Department of Energy under the Freedom of Information Act, 5 U.S.C. § 552. The suit challenges the withholding of portions of twelve fictional records concerning the Grid Sentinel playbook suite. It does not seek any live technical data, real infrastructure information, credential, actual incident record, or personal contact information.

DOE produced 146 page images from a 200-page responsive universe and withheld fifty-four pages in full. It invoked Exemption 7(E) for nonpublic decision conditions, sequencing, and branches, and invoked Exemption 7(F) for broad categories of role, contact, safety, and escalation fields. Open Grid alleges that the agency’s public explanations do not justify every application of those exemptions and do not establish that all reasonably segregable material was released.

The plaintiff seeks a declaration of its statutory rights, an order requiring adequate justification and release of nonexempt portions, and other relief authorized by FOIA. All names, records, identifiers, and events pleaded here are fictional exercise material.

## Exemption 7(E) Allegations

Open Grid does not allege that every redaction under 7(E) is improper. An approved operational decision tree can contain nonpublic implementation detail, and the plaintiff does not seek any genuine trigger order, protected condition, or functional sequence that satisfies the statute. The dispute is whether DOE has shown that each withheld portion falls within the exemption.

The public decision describes both techniques or procedures and guidelines but often assigns them one undifferentiated rationale. For guidelines, DOE must address the statutory circumvention condition. For a known general technique, it must identify the nonpublic implementation detail at issue without revealing that detail. The agency’s category-level statements do not establish that document-control fields, descriptive legends, and general administrative directions share the protected character of operational branches.

DOE withheld three records in full even though the released governance material and redacted playbooks show that Grid Sentinel records use distinct title blocks, revision histories, approval entries, and section headings. Open Grid alleges that at least some such material can be described and disclosed without reconstructing decision logic.

The agency bears the burden in this action. A court may rely on declarations and an index only if they provide a sufficiently specific factual basis for de novo review.

## Exemption 7(F) Allegations

DOE applied 7(F) to categories labeled duty role, contact channel, safety reference, and escalation position. The plaintiff does not contend that the agency must name a threatened person. It alleges instead that DOE must demonstrate a reasonable expectation that releasing the disputed information would endanger some individual’s life or physical safety.

The initial and appeal decisions treat the presence of a safety-related label as the necessary causal explanation. They do not distinguish an inert role heading from a protected contact value or identify why every generic coordination descriptor presents the same risk. Thirty-nine fully withheld pages and fifty-one produced pages carry a 7(F) assertion, yet the public account supplies no record-level differentiation among them.

Open Grid expressly disclaims any request for real names, telephone numbers, physical locations, facility layouts, or live response assignments. Its challenge can be resolved by release of harmless administrative words, narrower redaction, or an adequate explanation showing why a particular field cannot be disclosed.

Categorical proof may be appropriate when meaningful functional groups follow document-by-document review and the agency connects each group to the protected interest. Open Grid alleges that DOE’s broad labels and repeated harm language do not meet that standard for all withheld matter.

## Segregability and Foreseeable Harm Allegations

FOIA requires release of reasonably segregable nonexempt information after exempt portions are deleted. DOE states that reviewers examined each page, but its public explanation offers a collective conclusion. It does not specify which administrative passages were tested, why separation would leave meaningless fragments, or how disclosure of a revision field would reveal an operational sequence.

The produced pages show document structures capable of separation. Titles, approval status, effective dates, version markers, general statements of purpose, revision explanations, and neutral coordination headings can exist apart from protected values. Administrative text already visible in the partial productions demonstrates that the agency’s initial assertion is not self-proving and must be tested against the structure of each record.

DOE also must reasonably foresee harm to an interest protected by the invoked exemption or identify a legal prohibition on disclosure. A harm prediction tied to operational logic does not automatically extend to every word in the same record. A physical-safety prediction tied to a contact or placement does not automatically cover a nonidentifying section label.

Open Grid alleges that the agency’s generalized foreseeable-harm statements and segregability conclusions cannot sustain summary judgment without more specific evidence.

## Claims for Relief

**Count One—Improper Withholding.** Open Grid incorporates the preceding allegations. DOE has withheld agency records responsive to a perfected FOIA request. To the extent the withheld material is not protected by 7(E), 7(F), or another properly supported exemption, the withholding violates 5 U.S.C. § 552.

**Count Two—Failure to Release Reasonably Segregable Portions.** DOE has not demonstrated that all nonexempt information was released after deletion of exempt matter. Its categorical declaration and appeal decision do not resolve the separability of administrative and descriptive text within the three fully withheld and seven partially released records.

**Count Three—Inadequate Supporting Basis.** The public explanations do not correlate every functional category with a particular statutory requirement and foreseeable harm. Open Grid seeks a record-specific basis sufficient for adversarial testing and judicial review, not disclosure through the explanation itself.

No count challenges DOE’s ability to protect genuinely exempt operational implementation detail. No count requests an order compelling creation of a record, alteration of a real system, or disclosure of excluded incident material. The controversy concerns only the synthetic twelve-record collection processed under the identified request.
