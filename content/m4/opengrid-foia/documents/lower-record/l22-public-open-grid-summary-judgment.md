## Governing Summary-Judgment Standard

FOIA places the burden on the agency to establish that withheld material falls within a statutory exemption. Review is de novo. An agency may rely on declarations and a Vaughn presentation, but those submissions must describe the material and connect a claimed exemption to the withholding with enough specificity for adversarial testing and meaningful judicial review.

A functional category may sometimes organize a large record. It cannot replace document-by-document judgment or a reasoned connection between the category, the protected interest, and the predicted harm. The relation between `[REDACTED—FICTIONAL DECISION CONDITION]` and `[REDACTED—FICTIONAL RESPONSE ORDER]` illustrates why function matters more than a category name. Conclusory assurances are not transformed into evidence by repetition across several records.

FOIA separately requires release of every reasonably segregable nonexempt portion. The agency must show that it actually performed that analysis. A valid exemption for one field does not automatically absorb a title, approval statement, revision entry, neutral heading, or administrative sentence located nearby.

The foreseeable-harm provision also demands attention to the interest an exemption protects unless disclosure is prohibited by law. Open Grid does not contend that a court should reveal a protected detail to test the claim. Public descriptions, controlled review, and carefully bounded findings permit scrutiny without exposing disputed information. The evidence should be assessed at that useful level rather than through an all-or-nothing presumption.

## Sealing Must Be Document Specific

The public has a qualified right of access to judicial records. A party seeking sealing must identify the material, explain why closure is necessary, address less restrictive alternatives, and propose an appropriate duration. The Court must provide notice and make findings that permit review.

DOE has filed public counterparts for the controlled exhibits, which is an important alternative to wholesale closure. But its consolidated request groups distinct materials under broad operational and safety descriptions. A Vaughn index, sample playbook, party memorandum, and declaration serve different judicial functions. The need to protect a fabricated label on one page does not justify sealing all text in every counterpart.

Open Grid asks the Court to keep the public versions accessible, examine the controlled counterparts in camera if useful, and limit any sealing to passages supported by particular findings. It does not ask the Court to place controlled labels on the public docket. The issue is whether entire documents or broad sets of pages may be closed on aggregate reasoning.

Duration also matters. If a controlled exhibit remains necessary through judgment and appeal, the order should say so and should allow later reconsideration. A permanent or indefinite closure without a document-specific basis would exceed the justification presented.
