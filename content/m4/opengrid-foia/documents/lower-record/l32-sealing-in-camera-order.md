The Court finds that public notice occurred and that the parties had an opportunity to address access. The open motion and chart adequately identify the groups for present purposes. ## Governing Access Principles

Judicial records carry a presumption of public access under the applicable common-law or First Amendment framework, depending on their role in adjudication. Before closing a record, a court must identify the source of access, give notice, consider less drastic alternatives, and make findings sufficient for review. First Amendment closure additionally requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring.

The controlled materials accompany dispositive motions and will inform the Court’s decision. The Court therefore assumes the more demanding access framework applies. DOE invokes protection of asserted nonpublic operational organization and human-safety functions. Open Grid accepts that particular passages may warrant controlled review but argues that the requested scope is not tailored.

The Court recognizes that subject-matter sensitivity alone is insufficient. It also recognizes that disclosure of context can sometimes expose a relationship even where a value is omitted. Here, the use of coordinated twins creates a record in which the public can understand the dispute while the Court can inspect the fuller semantic presentation.

The Court proceeds from those general principles and the common interests identified by DOE. It does not separately analyze the access weight or asserted interest for each individual filing.

## Necessity and Alternatives

DOE contends that the controlled set displays category placement needed for in-camera evaluation and that unrestricted access would disclose the relationships it asks the Court to assess. The Court accepts that explanation for the set. It finds that preserving the controlled presentation is an important interest during adjudication.

The principal alternative is the existing public counterpart for each sealed item. Those versions present record purpose, category notices, factual background, and legal arguments without the inert labels. The Court finds generally that they provide substantial public access while omitting the controlled semantic relationships.

DOE also considered a single narrative declaration and passage-level redaction of controlled copies. It states that a single declaration would remove page correspondence and that repeated redaction would obscure the relation under review. The Court accepts those explanations without making findings about narrower treatment of each document or page.

Accordingly, the Court concludes at an aggregate level that sealing the controlled set is necessary and that the public twins are an adequate less restrictive alternative. It does not require DOE to submit document-specific redaction proposals. This conclusion applies equally to indexes, samples, memoranda, and declarations.

## Scope and Duration

The seal covers every controlled item listed in the certificate. Public counterparts, the motion, the certificate, and this order remain open. Access to one public item does not create access to its controlled partner. Authorized review occurs only through the Court’s controlled process.

The restriction will continue through final disposition in this Court, any appeal, and return of jurisdiction after appellate proceedings. It will then remain in effect until further order. A party may move to alter the scope or duration upon changed circumstances.

The Court adopts a common duration because the controlled items participate in the same exemption, segregation, and access disputes. It does not set shorter terms for advocacy documents or distinguish records that may cease to affect adjudication earlier. Administrative convenience and consistency favor one treatment.

The Court finds that this scope does not close the case or prevent public understanding of the parties’ positions. It acknowledges Open Grid’s argument that passage-specific or document-specific treatment could be narrower, but concludes that the paired structure is sufficiently protective of access for the current record.

## Order

DOE’s Consolidated Public Motion to Maintain Sealing is GRANTED. The Clerk shall accept and maintain under seal the controlled counterparts identified in the Certificate of Confidentiality and Sealed-Materials Chart. The corresponding public versions shall remain available on the open synthetic docket.

The Court will consider the controlled set in camera when resolving the cross-motions for summary judgment. Authorized counsel may use the controlled materials solely in this action and shall not reproduce them in a public filing. Nothing in this order authorizes access to another record or repository.

The seal shall remain in effect through district and appellate resolution and thereafter until modified by order. Any motion to modify must identify the affected item and proposed alternative. The Clerk need not create passage-level public versions beyond the counterparts already filed.

This order is based on common findings that the controlled set concerns asserted operational and human-safety relationships, that public counterparts are available, and that collection-wide restriction preserves the usefulness of in-camera review. The Court makes no merits ruling on the FOIA exemptions today.
