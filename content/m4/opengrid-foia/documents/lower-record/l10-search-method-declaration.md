## Terms and Field Logic

The collection combined the phrase “Grid Sentinel” with the controlled family terms “Operational Decision Tree,” “Cyber-Incident Escalation Matrix,” and “Response-Coordination Appendix.” Governance retrieval also used “revision approval” and “supersession.” Searches included common punctuation and spacing variants recorded in the repository indexes.

Field conditions required an approved or final status and an effective period overlapping the agreed range. For Driftglass, an edition approved earlier could qualify if its supersession date fell on or after January 1, 2022. An item approved after May 31, 2024, did not qualify merely because its description mentioned an earlier version.

I also queried version and replacement relationships to find a record indexed primarily under its family name. This avoided reliance on the suite name alone. Harborline results had to carry a final-instrument designation; a message containing the same words but lacking that status remained outside the collected governance subset.

The query did not include vulnerability, credential, live address, or incident terms. Those subjects were excluded, and adding them would have expanded rather than improved the agreed search. Candidate retrieval ended June 30, with export packaging finished July 2.

## Raw Results and Duplicate Reduction

The three exports contained 16,842 raw hits. Many were replicated index entries, distributed copies, format variants, or references to the same approved content. I first normalized repository-generated wrappers without changing the document body. I then compared exact content digests and approved metadata fields.

Exact duplicates were collapsed to one candidate linked to its authoritative location. Near duplicates were not automatically discarded. If two files differed in body text, version, approval state, or effective date, both remained for custodian review. A format-only conversion with identical readable content was associated with the same candidate but retained in the audit log.

That process yielded 1,284 distinct candidate files. Automated status and date filtering identified ninety-three items requiring a closer metadata check. I examined conflicting labels and referred ambiguous finality questions to the custodians. Forty-six candidates survived to substantive custodian review.

The deduplication log records the count removed at each step and the reason code. It does not preserve or expose any excluded operational data. A reviewer can reconstruct the numerical path from raw hits to candidates without opening a real system or learning a protected playbook value.

## Quality Controls and Search Adequacy

I ran a reverse check from the final version chain. For each current family record, I followed its fictional predecessor field through Driftglass until the chain moved outside the date boundary. I compared that list with the candidate set. Every qualifying approved edition had been collected.

I separately checked the approval identifiers attached to the retained editions against Harborline’s final-instrument index. The comparison located the two unique governance records already selected and no additional final memorandum. I sampled discarded exact-duplicate groups to confirm that the retained authoritative copy contained the same readable body.

Search terms were also tested without the Grid Sentinel suite phrase. Family-only retrieval found records already present in the result set and did not identify an omitted authoritative edition. Custodians certified that the invented repositories covered their ordinary recordkeeping locations.

These controls led me to conclude that the search was reasonably calculated to locate records within the agreed scope. The conclusion is limited to that scope. It says nothing about excluded incident files, drafts, or live technical information, none of which was sought. If another responsive authoritative record were later identified, the search log would record and route it rather than treat this declaration as a bar to correction.
