# Government Response Brief
## Standard of review

Under 8 U.S.C. § 1252(b)(4)(B), administrative factual findings are conclusive unless any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to conclude otherwise. Predictions about future events, the likelihood that actors will cooperate, and the probable official response are factual determinations. The Court reviews legal questions, including the governing CAT framework, without deference, but it may not reweigh the record under the label of meaningful consideration.

The agency stated the correct burden: A.R.M. had to prove that torture was more likely than not after consideration of all relevant evidence and that qualifying harm would involve official action or acquiescence. AR163, AR165. It also recognized that awareness may include willful blindness and must be followed by breach of a duty to intervene. AR165. The respondent acknowledges that a failure to aggregate or apply that definition would be legal error. The dispute is whether the decisions actually made those errors, not whether this Court would assign different weight to the evidence.

# Government Response Brief
## Aggregation principle

The respondent accepts the rule of Rodriguez-Arias, 915 F.3d at 974–77: an adjudicator must consider risk from all supported sources in the aggregate and may not demand that one freestanding source independently establish the CAT threshold. That rule does not require the agency to assume that uncertain links occur, add probabilities unsupported by the record, or treat a merely conceivable sequence as more likely than not.

The terminology at AR171 is not ideal in isolation, but the surrounding decision did more than create three unrelated boxes. It described Unit Four’s prior conduct and registry access, Harbor League’s alleged information role, registry notification capacity, contacts among those sources, and Varo’s institutional map. AR168–AR176. The judge then found the asserted contacts too uncertain to alter the outcome. The Board likewise addressed the claimed connections and found they did not overcome the individual evidentiary weaknesses. AR212–AR214. The respondent submits that this was an aggregate assessment of an inadequately supported chain, even if expressed source by source.

## Registry evidence

The record shows that Kalyrian systems hold arrival, employment, and household information and that a regional security liaison may request it. AR75, AR79. Audit samples included unsupported queries. Yet Varo could not identify a current flag for A.R.M., determine that every arrival notification reaches Unit Four, or show that officials outside Merrow would act on an old municipal inquiry. For registry identification to advance the asserted chain, an active or generic flag must identify A.R.M., a liaison must transmit the information, Unit Four must retain harmful interest, and the encounter must result in qualifying abuse. The respondent reads the decisions as weighing evidence bearing on those steps and treating too many transitions as unsupported. That factual judgment is not converted into legal error merely because the decision also rejected registry staff as an independent source of torture.

# Government Response Brief
## Combined pathway response

A.R.M.’s proposed sequence is logically possible: a registry event identifies a returnee, a contractor contributes a location or procurement reference, and Unit Four uses official authority to act. Varo mapped those nodes and explained that overlapping steps should not be counted twice. AR86, AR154, AR159. On the respondent’s reading, the agency did not reject the theory because several actors were involved; it rejected the theory because the evidence did not establish that the disputed links were sufficiently likely to form the asserted future chain.

Past detention provides a strong starting fact, but the later steps remain uncertain. There is no current flag, identified future messenger, open case document, or proof that a national query would reach Merrow. The country sample was limited and selected for data availability. AR155. Formal rules and possible relocation supplied contrary evidence. AR173–AR176. Aggregation requires those strengths and weaknesses to be evaluated together; it does not turn several possibilities into a probability above one half. The agency’s reasoning permits review of that conclusion.

# Government Response Brief
## Acquiescence rule

The parties agree on the governing regulation. Official acquiescence requires awareness of torturous activity, including actual knowledge or willful blindness, followed by breach of a legal responsibility to intervene. 8 C.F.R. § 1208.18(a)(7). Express approval by a senior official is not required. The immigration judge stated that rule verbatim before considering the complaint and protective institutions. AR165.

A.R.M. relies on the later observation that the record lacked direct proof of a senior official’s advance knowledge or express approval. AR172. The respondent reads that sentence as an evidentiary finding, not a replacement legal test. The same section acknowledged institutional receipt, lack of an independent interview, the troubling pending status, and Pel’s routing explanation. It then found the evidence insufficient to establish future awareness or acquiescence. The Board also considered the route and formal safeguards. AR215. Although its explanation was concise, it did not hold that only express consent could satisfy the regulation.

# Government Response Brief
## Safeguards evidence

Kalyrian law prohibits torture and retaliation, requires registered custody, establishes a national inspector hotline and external review desk, and reports discipline against some officers. AR173. These measures do not automatically defeat acquiescence, and the respondent does not defend such a categorical rule. They are nevertheless relevant to the likely official response and must be weighed with evidence of imperfect implementation.

The immigration judge did that. The decision discussed the rerouted complaint and Varo’s municipal sample before giving substantial weight to national mechanisms. AR172–AR176. A.R.M. had not used the inspector hotline after the municipal route failed, and the record did not establish that the national office would return a complaint to Unit Four. The sample comprised forty selected municipal files and was not nationally representative. AR155. A reasonable adjudicator could therefore regard the local experience as serious evidence without treating it as proof that all available Kalyrian authorities would remain deliberately blind to future abuse.

## Meaningful consideration

Rodriguez-Arias requires the agency to engage substantial CAT evidence, but it does not impose an item-by-item opinion-writing rule. The decisions here addressed the central materials: the credited detention, medical corroboration, complaint route, Pel’s testimony, Varo’s map and sample, Harbor League attribution limits, registry capacity, formal protections, and relocation. The immigration judge called the pending status and absence of interviews troubling, while assigning limited weight because no decisionmaker or later response was identified. AR172. The Board then found no clear error and declined remand. AR215–AR216. The respondent submits that this path is discernible even if the Court might have preferred a more detailed explanation.

## Proffer exclusion

The Court correctly declined to add PA1–PA8 to the administrative record. The T.R. declaration describes events alleged to have occurred after the Board’s January 13 order and was signed February 20. It was not part of the evidence on which the removal order was based. Section 1252(b)(4)(A) confines review to that agency record, and Rule 16(b) corrects omissions rather than admitting later proof into a closed merits proceeding.

The corrected certification confirms that the proffer supplied no byte to AR1–AR238. AR235, AR237–AR238. That boundary protects both sides: A.R.M. receives the complete record the agency considered, including P-7, while the respondent is not required to litigate new factual allegations for the first time in a reviewing court. The respondent has not used PA1–PA8 to challenge credibility, and A.R.M. may not use it to establish future risk or official awareness in this petition. Any later-evidence procedure remains outside this appeal.
