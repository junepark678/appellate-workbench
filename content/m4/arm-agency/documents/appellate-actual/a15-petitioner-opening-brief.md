# Petitioner Opening Brief
## Questions presented

A.R.M. files this opening brief on April 14, 2025. The petition presents five related but distinct questions. First, whether Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 16(b) permitted the parties to correct the certified record by restoring the unchanged declaration admitted as Agency Exhibit P-7. Second, whether the court may treat the later T.R. declaration at PA1–PA8 as agency evidence. Third, whether the agency lawfully assessed Unit Four, Harbor League, and registry-enabled risk one source at a time instead of evaluating their supported connections. Fourth, whether the agency meaningfully applied the regulatory acquiescence standard to the documented complaint route and lack of independent response. Fifth, whether the February 11 petition tender satisfied the thirty-day filing requirement notwithstanding its next-day cure.

A.R.M. submits that the P-7 correction was proper, the post-order proffer remains outside the merits record, and the petition was timely. On the CAT questions, A.R.M. seeks vacatur and remand for the agency—not this Court—to evaluate aggregate risk and official acquiescence under the correct framework.

# Petitioner Opening Brief
## Aggregate-risk rule

CAT protection turns on whether torture is more likely than not after all relevant evidence is considered. 8 C.F.R. § 1208.16(c)(2)–(3). Under Rodriguez-Arias v. Whitaker, 915 F.3d 968, 974–77 (4th Cir. 2019), an adjudicator may not divide feared sources into separate claims and demand that one source independently exceed the threshold. The inquiry must account for the cumulative probability of torture from all supported sources. Where pathways overlap, the agency should not count the same encounter twice; neither may it ignore a supported link because an enabling actor does not personally inflict the ultimate harm.

The agency stated the opposite method here. The immigration judge evaluated Unit Four, Harbor League, and registry personnel as distinct sources, found each below one half standing alone, and denied relief without assessing a connected chain. AR171. The Board endorsed that reasoning and again treated the individual findings as sufficient. AR212. Uncertainty affects weight, but it does not authorize omission of the aggregate inquiry Rodriguez-Arias requires.

# Petitioner Opening Brief
## Acquiescence definition

Under 8 C.F.R. § 1208.18(a)(1) and (7), official acquiescence does not require an express agreement to torture. The relevant public official must have awareness of the activity—through actual knowledge or willful blindness—and then breach a legal responsibility to intervene. That standard directs attention to information reaching official channels, steps taken or deliberately avoided after notice, and whether available protective duties operated in practice. Formal prohibitions remain relevant evidence, but their existence does not itself resolve awareness or response.

The agency announced portions of this framework yet applied a materially narrower inquiry. The immigration judge emphasized the absence of direct evidence that a senior official knew future torture was planned or expressly approved abuse. AR172. The Board likewise found no direct senior-official approval. AR215. That formulation did not address willful blindness or determine whether institutional recipients failed to perform the independent-review and protective duties triggered by A.R.M.’s complaint. The missing analysis is legal error independent of any disagreement about ultimate factual weight.

## Formal safeguards

The record contains genuine contrary evidence. # Petitioner Opening Brief
## Review standard

Section 1252(b)(4)(B) makes administrative factual findings conclusive unless any reasonable adjudicator would be compelled to reach the contrary conclusion. A.R.M. does not ask the Court to replace the agency’s predictions with its own or to decide that future torture is established. The questions presented instead concern the legal framework and whether the agency supplied reasoning adequate for review: aggregate consideration under § 1208.16(c)(3), acquiescence under § 1208.18(a)(7), and meaningful engagement with material evidence.

Those inquiries respect the statutory division of labor. Whether the agency used three isolated probability tests is visible on the face of AR171 and AR212. Whether it required direct senior-official knowledge while omitting willful blindness is visible at AR172 and AR215. Whether its stated path addresses the file-specific routing evidence can be assessed without choosing among disputed inferences. If the correct framework requires additional findings about links, notice, response, or relocation, the agency must make them on remand. Vacatur for analytical error is not appellate factfinding.

# Petitioner Opening Brief
## Meaningful consideration

An adjudicator need not discuss every exhibit or answer every argument. Rodriguez-Arias nevertheless requires meaningful engagement with substantial, material CAT evidence sufficient to permit judicial review. 915 F.3d at 977–80. The requirement matters most where credited facts and the chosen analytical route appear to pull in different directions. A.R.M.’s prior official detention was credited; the complaint’s transfer to the implicated regional command was documented; and the expert identified shared registry, contractor, and Unit Four nodes. AR86, AR154, AR159, AR167.

The Board answered that the immigration judge assigned permissible weight to Varo, Sore, S.M., and Pel. AR216. It did not address the source map, complaint sample, or file-specific sequence beyond passing references, nor did it explain how three standalone findings amounted to aggregation. Concision is not the problem. The decisions leave no discernible path from the evidence of connected sources and routed notice to the conclusions that aggregate risk and acquiescence were insufficient. Remand is needed for the agency to provide that path.

## No first-instance findings

Several factual questions remain open and should remain so. The record does not establish a current personal registry flag, identify every Harbor League messenger, show that every query reaches Merrow, or reveal what response—if any—followed the June 30 complaint status. It also contains evidence favoring the Government: lawful departure, elapsed time, formal legal protections, potential relocation, and acknowledged limits in Varo’s sample. AR168–AR176. Accordingly, vacatur would not hold that torture is more likely than not, that a particular official will acquiesce, or that any contractor-official connection is proved. It would hold only that the agency must evaluate supported sources in combination, use the full regulatory awareness standard, and explain its treatment of material evidence. The agency may reach the same or a different result after applying that framework, provided its findings rest on the certified record and its reasoning permits review.
