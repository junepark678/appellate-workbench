# Government Response to Record Motions
## Position on supplementation

The respondent opposes adding T.R.’s February 20 declaration to the administrative record. The document did not exist when the immigration judge closed evidence or when the Board entered its January 13 order. No agency adjudicator admitted it, tested its foundation, evaluated credibility, or relied on it. Section 1252(b)(4)(A) confines the pending petition to the record on which the removal order was based.

The respondent also opposes treating the supplementation motion as proof of changed risk. Filing a declaration in the court of appeals does not establish the asserted residence search or its connection to any feared actor. The court should deny administrative-record treatment and leave any separate agency procedure to the law governing such a request. The proffer may remain on the appellate docket as the object of the motion, but merits briefs should not cite it as agency evidence.

# Government Response to Record Motions
## No merits concession

The respondent’s agreement about omission does not accept A.R.M.’s aggregate-risk theory. The respondent continues to maintain that the agency considered Unit Four, Harbor League, registry evidence, country conditions, expert limits, relocation, and formal safeguards adequately. It also disputes that complaint routing and the absence of an independent interview compel a finding of official acquiescence. Those questions belong in merits briefing after the record is fixed.

The respondent’s position is that the account cannot be used as administrative-record evidence in review of the January 13 order. Separating procedural status from factual truth allows the court to grant correction, deny supplementation, and later decide whether the agency’s explanation on the proper record permits review or requires remand.
