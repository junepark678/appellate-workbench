# Timeliness Show-Cause Order
## Post-Riley question

On June 27, 2025, the court addresses the petition received February 13, thirty-one days after the Board’s January 13 order. The court deferred the filing-time issue while the Supreme Court considered the character of Section 1252(b)(1). On June 26, Riley v. Bondi held that the thirty-day requirement is a nonjurisdictional claims-processing rule and explained that such a rule ordinarily must be timely invoked by the party it benefits.

The institutional respondent shall state by July 7 whether it invokes Section 1252(b)(1) against this petition. Any motion must identify the January 13 order, February 13 receipt, absence of an asserted alternate trigger, and the relief requested under Riley. This order does not enforce the rule on the court’s initiative, determine that the respondent has forfeited it, or decide the CAT and record-composition questions presented by the petition.
