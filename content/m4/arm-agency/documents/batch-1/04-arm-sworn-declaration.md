# Sworn declaration of A.R.M.
## Identity protocol and basis of knowledge

I use only the initials A.R.M. under the privacy protocol. I do not provide an expanded name,
address, birth date, registration number, or other unnecessary identifier.

Except when I identify a message or office record, I describe events I personally saw, heard,
or experienced. I worked in the procurement-records annex for the municipality of Orin Vale.
My duties let me retrieve invoice registers, vendor permits, approval logs, and correction
requests. I was not an investigator and did not decide whether a contract was lawful.

I make this declaration in support of my CAT application and offer it as Agency Exhibit P-7. The

# My records-annex work

I began records-annex work in 2019. My desk received completed procurement packets after a buyer
and department approver signed them. I checked that each packet had a vendor code, purchase-order
number, invoice, receiving entry, and correction history before scanning it into the municipal
archive.

The annex used project nicknames as well as numbers. “North Quay Lamps” referred to a
Harbor League street-light maintenance contract. Only staff with procurement credentials could
see the nickname-to-number table. I had read access but no authority to change an approval.

In early 2023 I noticed several invoices just below the secondary-review threshold. Each covered
work on the same streets over adjacent dates. The receiving entries used different descriptions,
but the inspection initials and vehicle permit were the same. I recorded the packet numbers in my
work notebook so I could ask how to file them.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# The split-invoice pattern

On April 18, 2023, I compared six North Quay Lamps packets. Three invoices were entered on one day
and three the next. Together they covered one maintenance cycle, but each was coded as a separate
urgent repair. A correction log showed that an analyst had first grouped them and a supervisor
later removed the group link.

I copied the invoice face pages and the correction-log view using the annex's authorized audit
function. The export stamped my employment code and the time. I did not remove an original or
alter a value. I kept the copies in the sealed internal-review folder available to compliance.

The Harbor League vendor permit on the packets matched the permit later recorded outside my
family residence. That connection is why the number mattered to me. I cannot say who drove the
vehicle on either occasion, only that the permit string was the same.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Report to Nadia Pel

On May 2, I met Nadia Pel, then a municipal compliance officer, in a conference room near the
annex. I showed her the six packet numbers, the export stamp, and the correction history. She said
the split pattern should receive independent review because one maintenance cycle appeared to be
treated as six emergencies.

Pel asked me not to email the pages outside the internal system. She created referral `CIV-23-184`
and copied the ledger list into the compliance queue. I watched her select “independent review.”
She printed a receipt for me showing the referral number and nine listed source pages.

Pel warned that the default routing table sometimes sent municipal-security complaints back to
regional commands. At that time our referral concerned procurement, not my later detention, so I
did not understand why that warning would become important.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Events before the detention

During the next two weeks, two people I did not know came to the annex counter and asked for the
North Quay Lamps correction history. They did not present archive request forms. One wore a Unit
Four badge clipped inside a jacket. I directed both to the records supervisor.

On May 16, my supervisor told me a regional-security office had asked for my employment code and
access history. The supervisor did not accuse me of misconduct. I saw a personnel-index note that
read “regional inquiry pending” and referenced `CIV-23-184`.

On May 18, Pel sent me a short internal message: “Do not delete your receipt. Routing changed.”
The message did not explain who changed it. I saved a printout in the family packet. The next
morning, two uniformed Unit Four officers arrived at the annex and asked me to come with them.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Removal from the annex on May 19

The officers arrived at approximately 8:40 a.m. in a gray municipal vehicle. One showed a Unit
Four credential. Neither showed an arrest order. They said a supervisor needed me to explain an
unauthorized export and that I would return after questions.

I asked to call S.M. The taller officer took my telephone and said the call could wait. My records
supervisor watched me leave and later told S.M. that Unit Four had taken me. I sat in the rear of
the vehicle between the officers. The doors could not be opened from inside.

We drove about twenty minutes to a service building I recognized from municipal fleet records.
The officers logged my employment code at a side entrance. They did not give me a receipt, charge,
or opportunity to contact counsel. I knew the officers were acting officially because of their
uniforms, credential, vehicle, building access, and command radio.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# First questioning session

In the first room, the officers placed the six packet numbers on a table. They asked where the
export pages were and who outside procurement had received them. I said I used the authorized
audit function and gave the material to compliance officer Pel.

One officer asked whether Pel had “sent anything beyond the municipal wall.” I said I did not
know. The officer struck the table and told me the Harbor League kept roads and lights operating
when the city could not. He said my copies were creating a problem for people who protected the
district.

They required me to stand facing a wall with my hands above shoulder level while they repeated
the questions. When my arms dropped, an officer forced them upward and kicked the inside of my
right ankle. I was frightened and in pain. I did not know when I would be released.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Intentional harm during the first night

After dark, the officers moved me to a small room with a bench. They kept the overhead light on
and woke me whenever I lay down. During two later questioning periods, they demanded the receipt
Pel gave me and the name of anyone who had seen the correction history.

An officer twisted my left arm behind my back until my shoulder felt as though it tore. Another
pressed a folded cloth over my mouth and poured small amounts of water while asking whether I had
copied the vendor-permit list. I coughed and could not answer. They stopped, let me breathe, and
then repeated the threat.

The officer said the purpose was to make me remember where the copies were. He also said S.M.
could be brought in if I remained uncooperative. I believed the harm was deliberate punishment
and coercion, not an accident or ordinary restraint.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Second day of detention

On May 20, a different officer questioned me about `CIV-23-184`. The officer had a printout with
the referral number and the words “independent review” crossed out. I had not told Unit Four the
number. Its presence showed me that the compliance channel had shared or routed the referral.

The officer asked me to sign a statement saying I exported procurement data for personal use. I
refused because that was false. The officers again forced me to stand, denied sleep, and struck
my ribs and upper legs where clothing would cover the marks.

They played a recording of a person saying S.M.'s initials and the street name of our family
neighborhood. I could not identify the speaker. The officers told me the Harbor League knew how
to “finish an audit.” I understood that as a threat involving both official custody and the
contractor network.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Release and warning on May 21

On the morning of May 21, the first officer returned my telephone but not its memory card. He said
I would be released if I stopped pursuing the procurement matter. He warned that another complaint
would be routed back to the same command and that leaving Orin Vale would not remove the inquiry.

The officers drove me to a bus stop several neighborhoods from the annex. No release paper,
property list, or case number was provided. I had bruises on my ribs and legs, swelling around my
right ankle, and limited movement in my left shoulder.

I called S.M. at 10:18 a.m. from a shop telephone because my own device would not start. S.M.
arrived about thirty minutes later and photographed the visible injuries with the family tablet.
We went to a neighborhood clinic that afternoon. I told the clinician that municipal officers had
hurt me, but I asked that the public visit summary say only “reported assault” because I feared
the file would reach Unit Four.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Physical and psychological effects

For several weeks I could not lift my left arm above shoulder height. My ankle was swollen for
about ten days, and it hurt to climb stairs. The clinic supplied a sling, anti-inflammatory
medicine, and instructions to return if breathing pain worsened. S.M. helped me dress and carry
groceries.

I began waking when lights turned on and panicking when a gray municipal vehicle stopped nearby.
I slept in short periods and sometimes smelled wet cloth even when none was present. I avoided
the annex and used accrued leave while deciding what to do.

I am not a medical expert. I can describe my symptoms and treatment but do not diagnose their
cause. A trauma evaluation may compare my account with observed limitations and explain
alternative possibilities. The family photographs and clinic extract are separately authenticated;
this declaration does not make them self-proving.

# Complaint to the Office of Civic Integrity

Despite the warning, I wanted an office outside Unit Four to know what happened. On June 7, S.M.
and I went to the Office of Civic Integrity intake desk in Merrow Province. Through a written
statement, I described the detention, the procurement questions, the injuries, and the threat
against S.M.

The intake officer stamped receipt `OCI-23-441` and gave us a copy. I asked whether Unit Four
would see it. The officer said serious allegations were screened centrally before assignment.
The receipt initially displayed route code `IC-2`, which Pel later explained meant independent
conduct review.

No independent investigator contacted us. Three days later, my personnel index displayed
“forwarded RC-4.” Pel told me that code meant Unit Four regional command. The complaint's specific
contents had returned to the organization I accused.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# What I learned about the routing

Pel met me on June 15 in a public library foyer. She brought no confidential file. She said she
had checked the audit trail for the procurement referral and could see that `CIV-23-184` and the
new conduct complaint were linked under my employment code.

Pel explained that the default protocol sent complaints involving “operational security” to the
regional command for an initial response. She had warned supervisors that routing allegations
against Unit Four to Unit Four created a retaliation risk. The protocol was not changed.

Pel said a supervisor instructed her not to reopen the procurement referral and removed her from
the review queue. Her authentication statement is included in the family corroboration packet.
That statement is separate from this P-7 declaration and addresses only the records and routing
within Pel's knowledge.

# Harbor League threat on July 11

On July 11, S.M. and I left our family residence shortly after 7:00 p.m. A dark utility vehicle
was parked near the corner. S.M. recognized its vendor permit as `HL-47`, the same permit on the
North Quay Lamps packets. I could read the final digits from where we stood.

Two people approached. One said I had confused recordkeeping with power. The other used the
project nickname “North Quay Lamps” and said the arrival desk would see my regional inquiry even
if I left Orin Vale. One touched S.M.'s shoulder and said family members could answer questions
when the clerk would not.

We walked into a busy market and the people did not follow. S.M. wrote the time, phrases, permit,
and clothing descriptions in the family log that night. I did not know their names and cannot
state whether either person was a government employee.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Attempt to obtain protection after the threat

On July 12, S.M. and I went to a municipal public-safety desk outside our neighborhood. We showed
the desk officer the civic-integrity receipt and described the utility vehicle and threat. The
officer entered my employment code into a terminal and said a regional inquiry was already open.

The officer would not create a separate incident number. He said contractor disputes belonged
with procurement and Unit Four would decide whether security was involved. I asked for an escort
to retrieve belongings. He declined and suggested avoiding the vehicle.

I cannot know what the officer privately believed. I know he had notice of the threat, saw the
existing Unit Four flag, and sent us back toward the same channels without interviewing a witness
or recording the permit. That event supports my claim of official awareness and failure to take
available protective steps.

# Departure from Kalyra

After July 12, I stayed with different relatives for short periods. Messages referring to the
procurement pages continued to reach my old device account. One message contained the internal
correction-log time, a detail not stated in our public complaint.

I arranged travel using valid travel documents and departed Kalyra on August 4, 2023. I did
not announce my destination to former coworkers. I entered the United States on August 8 as a
temporary visitor and later remained after the authorized period.

Leaving did not end contact with S.M. S.M. reported that people asked about my return and that a
regional-security vehicle passed the family residence. I understand those reports are S.M.'s
observations, not mine. I rely on my own past experience, the records I saw, and corroborating
sources for my prediction of what would happen if I returned.

# Why relocation does not resolve my fear

I lived only in Orin Vale before leaving, so I do not claim personal experience in every Kalyrian
province. My concern is that the identifying flag is tied to my employment code and appears in a
central arrival and municipal-security system, not merely a neighborhood list.

The Unit Four officer warned that leaving the city would not remove the inquiry. The Harbor League
messenger separately referred to the arrival desk. When the public-safety officer entered my code,
he immediately saw the regional inquiry. Those events are why I believe relocation after return
would not prevent initial identification.

I also lack a safe way to seek official help without revealing the same code. My June complaint
was routed back to Unit Four. The facts do not prove that every Kalyrian official would harm me;
they show why ordinary relocation and complaint channels may not reduce the combined risk enough.
