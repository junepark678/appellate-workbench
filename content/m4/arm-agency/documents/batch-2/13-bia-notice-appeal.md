# Grounds concerning aggregate risk

The decision separately measured risk from Unit Four, Harbor League, and registry personnel and
ended the inquiry because no source alone exceeded one half. The notice challenges that method.
The admitted evidence described a connected sequence in which registry or contractor information
could enable official detention. The aggregate inquiry must consider the combined chance of
torture without counting overlapping steps twice.

A.R.M. will argue that the error is visible on the face of the decision and the hearing evidence.
The appeal does not ask the Board to accept a mathematical sum or an unsupported percentage.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Grounds concerning acquiescence and evidence

The decision credited the prior official detention and complaint route but relied heavily on
formal prohibitions and a hotline. The notice argues that it did not meaningfully address the
regional command's receipt, the lack of an independent interview, the June 30 “regional response
pending” status, and Pel's explanation of the routing protocol.

The notice also preserves challenges to the treatment of Varo's source map, the relocation
analysis, and the weight assigned to the medical and corroborating evidence. These are not claims
that every item compelled one factual conclusion; they concern the agency's obligation to consider
material evidence under the proper framework.

# Relief and certification

A.R.M. asks the Board to vacate the denial and remand for an aggregate-risk analysis and a renewed
acquiescence assessment that addresses the admitted evidence. In the alternative, A.R.M. requests
a reasoned Board decision applying the correct standard. No request is made for the Board to add
post-hearing evidence.
