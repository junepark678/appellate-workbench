# Trauma and medical evaluation
## Evaluator, consent, and limited purpose

Dr. Elian Sore is a licensed clinician asked to document A.R.M.'s reported symptoms and
observable findings. The evaluation occurred on May 22 and May 29, 2024 with a qualified
Kalyrian-language interpreter. A.R.M. consented to examination and release of this report to the
immigration court after counsel explained that it would be available to DHS.

The report is not treatment advice and does not determine who caused an injury. It evaluates
whether current findings are consistent, inconsistent, or nonspecific in relation to the history
A.R.M. supplied. Sore reviewed the CAT application, A.R.M.'s declaration, the May 21, 2023 clinic
notation, and photographs whose chain of custody is described elsewhere. No unnecessary personal
identifier or expanded name appears.

# History supplied for evaluation

A.R.M. reported being held for two nights in May 2023, struck on the ribs and thighs, restrained
at the wrists, deprived of ordinary sleep, and questioned about copied procurement ledgers. A.R.M.
described bruising after release, ankle swelling, transient pain with deep breathing, nightmares,
and heightened alertness near uniforms or mechanical door sounds.

The evaluator did not assume this account was true. Questions separated memories of the event
from facts learned later through family members. A.R.M. consistently distinguished what was seen,
felt, inferred, and reported by S.M. The interview disclosed no loss of consciousness, fracture
diagnosis, surgery, or hospital admission. Those negative details limit the conclusions.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Records and source reliability

The May 21, 2023 clinic notation records registration at 14:20, limited left-shoulder elevation,
right-ankle swelling, rib tenderness, hoarse voice, and advice to return if breathing worsened. It
contains no diagnosis of the person or institution responsible. The copy was authenticated through
a clinic-source statement and comparison with the family packet. Its translation identifies two
ambiguous abbreviations and preserves both possible readings.

Photographs show discoloration and swelling but contain no embedded location data. A family log
fixes the date by reference to a message exchange and clinic visit. Sore treats the images as
corroborative of appearance, not as proof of cause or exact timing. No later treatment file was
available beyond ordinary primary-care notes unrelated to the claimed detention.

# Physical examination

The 2024 examination found a narrow, healed area of reduced pigmentation near the left wrist,
mild tenderness at one rib margin without instability, and full ankle range of motion. There was
no current swelling, neurological deficit, or limitation in ordinary gait. Vital signs and basic
respiratory observations were within expected ranges for the examination setting.

These findings are compatible with many causes and are not diagnostic of restraint or assault.
The passage of one year reduces the ability to distinguish mechanisms. The wrist finding could be
consistent with prior friction or pressure; the rib tenderness is nonspecific. Sore records both
consistency and uncertainty rather than converting an observation into an attribution.

# Psychological symptom interview

A.R.M. described recurrent dreams involving the metal door sound, avoidance of procurement
offices, increased startle response, difficulty concentrating after official correspondence, and
guilt about exposing family members to attention. Symptoms reportedly began after the May 2023
event and fluctuated after departure from Kalyra. A.R.M. continued daily activities and attended
all scheduled legal appointments.

Screening responses met a threshold for further trauma-focused assessment but did not establish a
standalone diagnosis in this limited evaluation. Cultural and interpretation factors can affect
word choice, and litigation stress may intensify symptoms. The report therefore describes the
pattern and functional effect without claiming that symptom presentation proves the underlying
account.

# Consistency analysis

The reported timing of bruising, swelling, and rib pain is consistent with the clinic notation and
family log. The wrist observation one year later is also compatible with prior pressure, though
not specific to it. The absence of a fracture or hospital record does not contradict the described
soft-tissue injuries, but neither does it independently confirm detention.

The symptom narrative remained materially stable across two interviews. Minor differences
concerned whether a threat preceded or followed a change in questioning topic. That sequence does
not alter the medical assessment and is preserved for the factfinder. Sore assigns no legal
credibility label and expresses no opinion on CAT probability.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Alternative explanations and limits

Possible alternative causes include occupational strain, ordinary accidental injury, preexisting
anxiety, migration stress, and sleep disruption associated with litigation. The available notes
do not document a comparable wrist or rib complaint before May 2023, but absence from a limited
file is not proof that none existed. No toxicology, imaging, or contemporaneous specialist
examination was performed.

Sore did not interview Unit Four personnel, Harbor League members, or the original clinic worker.
The evaluator cannot date the healed wrist finding to a particular day. Translation and memory
limitations are disclosed. Conclusions are framed as consistency judgments, not causal certainty.

# Function and treatment history

A.R.M. reported avoiding crowded transit platforms, waking several times per week, and needing
extra time to review official documents. At the same time, A.R.M. maintained housing, assisted
counsel with exhibit organization, and communicated regularly with S.M. The report records both
impairment and retained function.

Brief supportive counseling occurred through a community clinic beginning in March 2024. The
notes emphasize grounding techniques and sleep routine; they do not contain a forensic conclusion.
No medication change is attributed to the claimed events. Future treatment decisions belong to a
treating clinician and do not determine the adjudicative question.

# Relationship to future risk

Past physical and psychological findings may provide context, but this evaluation cannot predict
what will occur after a return to Kalyra. Sore has no expertise in registry practices, security
units, contractor networks, or government protection. The report therefore offers no opinion on
relocation, official acquiescence, or aggregate probability.

If the factfinder credits the account, the findings are medically compatible with portions of it.
If the account is not credited, the findings do not independently identify a perpetrator. This
conditional statement is the outer boundary of the evaluator's evidence.
