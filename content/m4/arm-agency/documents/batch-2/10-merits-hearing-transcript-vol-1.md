# Merits hearing transcript — volume 1
## September 17, 2024 session opens

The immigration court convenes at 9:02 a.m. in proceeding
`SYN-EOIR-24-RM-0113`. A.R.M. appears through retained counsel. DHS appears through an institutional
trial attorney. Interpreter K-27 appears remotely and confirms an unobstructed audio connection.
The judge states that removability has been conceded and that the only application is protection
under the Convention Against Torture.

The judge identifies the burden and explains that testimony, exhibits, and objections will be
received before findings are made. No party asks for a closed hearing. The initials-only protocol
continues. Counsel confirm that the hearing may extend to a second volume and that all exhibit
rulings must be stated aloud for the clerk's receipt.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Interpretation and preliminary stipulations

Interpreter K-27 is sworn to translate completely between English and Kalyrian and to flag any
term without a direct equivalent. A.R.M. states through the interpreter that the connection is
clear. Counsel agree that dates and exhibit numbers may be repeated in English to prevent a
transcription error.

The parties stipulate for this proceeding that A.R.M. worked as a municipal procurement-records
clerk, left Kalyra on August 4, 2023, entered the United States four days later, and timely filed
the CAT application. DHS does not stipulate that detention, abuse, threats, official awareness, or
future harm occurred. The judge reserves credibility, weight, and the legal effect of the evidence.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Exhibit receipt and ruling on P-7

The clerk identifies A.R.M.'s eighteen-page sworn declaration, lodged April 12, 2024 as Agency
Exhibit P-7. The judge asks DHS to state every objection. DHS answers that it has no authenticity
objection, no timeliness objection, and no objection to admission; it reserves cross-examination
and weight arguments. A.R.M.'s counsel confirms that the offered copy matches the lodged copy.

The judge admits P-7 into evidence. The clerk states for the transcript: “P-7, eighteen pages,
lodged April 12, admitted September 17.” The judge directs that this ruling appear both in the
hearing transcript and exhibit receipt. The ruling does not deem every statement true; it places
the declaration in the agency evidence considered with the testimony.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# A.R.M. sworn and background established

A.R.M. is sworn and confirms the use of initials. Counsel asks about education and municipal work.
A.R.M. completed a two-year records-administration course and began work in the Orin Vale
procurement office in 2019. Duties included indexing vendor invoices, preserving revision history,
and routing packages for threshold review. A.R.M. did not approve contracts or decide whether a
vendor violated law.

A.R.M. explains that a records clerk could see the original entry, later corrections, user code,
and approval path. Copies were permitted for internal compliance review. The job provided no
security authority and no access to national intelligence systems.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Discovery of divided invoice entries

Counsel displays the two authenticated register versions. A.R.M. explains that six Harbor League
invoices, NQL-3101 through NQL-3106, each fell below the 25,000-unit secondary-review threshold and
totaled 137,400 units. The first version grouped all six under maintenance cycle `NQ-APR-A`; the
corrected version removed the grouping and labeled each an urgent standalone repair. On April 18,
2023, A.R.M. copied both visible versions to preserve that sequence.

A.R.M. does not claim expertise in procurement law and cannot say why each invoice was divided.
The concern was preservation: the change history identified different approval routes and might
be overwritten in the ordinary archive. A.R.M. stored the copies in a compliance folder and did
not publish them or seek payment.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Report to Nadia Pel

On May 2, A.R.M. met Nadia Pel, then a municipal compliance officer. A.R.M. showed the two ledger
versions and asked how to preserve the audit trail. Pel said she would check the routing fields and
advised A.R.M. not to confront a vendor. A.R.M. left a duplicate set with Pel and retained one set
for the complaint file.

Pel did not promise that fraud had occurred or that an investigation would result. A.R.M. recalls
Pel recognizing one Harbor League contact number from municipal dispatch records. The source of
that recognition and the registers' format are addressed in Pel's own testimony rather than
adopted as A.R.M.'s expertise.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# First day of detention

A.R.M. testifies that Unit Four officers arrived at the records annex on May 19. Two wore municipal
security markings; one displayed a Unit Four credential. They asked where the copied ledgers were.
A.R.M. was taken in an unmarked vehicle to a storage compound and placed in a room with a metal
door and overhead machinery noise. No custody receipt or charge was provided.

During questioning, an officer placed pressure on A.R.M.'s wrists and struck the left side of the
ribs after A.R.M. denied sending the copies outside the city. A.R.M. describes fear, pain, and
confusion but does not claim to remember every question in exact order. Water was provided; ordinary
sleep and contact with family were not.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Second night and demands for records

Questioning resumed the next day. Officers named the maintenance cycle and asked whether Nadia Pel
had a copy. One speaker said Harbor League kept the port running and that clerks should not create
security problems. A.R.M. was made to stand for extended periods and was struck on the thigh after
requesting a lawyer. The testimony identifies no medical emergency during custody.

A.R.M. states that the officers wanted the copied versions, the names of anyone who had seen them,
and a promise not to file a complaint. The combination of official credentials, the place of
detention, and knowledge of internal ledger details caused A.R.M. to understand the questioners as
Unit Four personnel rather than private messengers.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Release and observable condition

On May 21, A.R.M. was released near a municipal transit stop. An officer warned that a complaint
would be routed back to the regional command. A.R.M. walked to S.M.'s residence. S.M. observed wrist
discoloration, right-ankle swelling, limited left-shoulder movement, and guarded breathing. The
North Bell clinic registered A.R.M. that afternoon at 14:20.

A.R.M. did not report to police immediately because the speakers had identified themselves with a
municipal unit and appeared to know family information. The delay is not offered as medical proof.
The clinic notation and S.M.'s testimony separately document what was observed and when.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Clinic visit and complaint filing

The clinic worker recorded the conditions shown in the translated notation and advised follow-up
for worsening symptoms. A.R.M. gave only a short account because the clinic intake area was open
to other patients. No officer was named in the note. A.R.M. later prepared a complaint with S.M.
and submitted it to the Office of Civic Integrity on June 7.

The receipt identifies complaint `OCI-23-441`, accepted June 7 under initial route `IC-2`. A.R.M.
expected an outside review because the complaint named Unit Four. The audit links it on June 9 to
`CIV-23-184` through `ARM-RX-14`, changes destination on June 10 to `RC-4`, Unit Four regional
command, and reports “regional response pending” on June 30. No independent interviewer contacted
A.R.M. or S.M. These facts come from the receipt and routing object, not unseen files.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# July threats to the household

On July 11, two people approached A.R.M. and S.M. outside the family residence. One referred to the
copied book and split orders; the other said the household would be easier to find if A.R.M. left
Orin Vale. A July 13 message said the North Quay Lamps file remained open. A.R.M. did not recognize
the speakers as Unit Four officers.

The reference to nonpublic ledger details led A.R.M. to associate them with Harbor League contacts.
That is an inference, not direct identification. The translated messages preserve the wording, and
S.M. supplies an independent account of the encounter. No testimony claims that every Harbor League
member participated.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Departure from Kalyra

A.R.M. left Kalyra on August 4 using a valid passport and ordinary departure control. No officer
stopped the departure. A.R.M. testifies that the ability to leave reduced immediate danger but did
not erase concern about what might happen on return, when entry information would identify the
traveler and intended region.

A.R.M. stayed briefly with relatives before traveling and did not attempt long-term relocation
inside Kalyra. The decision was influenced by the complaint routing, household threats, and access
to registry data. A.R.M. acknowledges having no direct proof of a current watch flag.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Feared harm from Unit Four

Asked what is feared now, A.R.M. first identifies another unregistered detention by Unit Four.
The officers in 2023 demanded the ledger copies and names of collaborators. Those materials were
never returned to the office, and the complaint itself supplied a reason to view A.R.M. as
disloyal. A.R.M. believes questioning would be more severe after an international departure.

Counsel asks for facts rather than a percentage. A.R.M. identifies the prior encounter, knowledge
of internal data, the regional routing code, and family contacts. A.R.M. does not know whether the
same officers remain assigned or whether a formal case is open.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Feared harm from Harbor League actors

A.R.M. next describes concern about contractor messengers who knew the ledger subject and household
location. Harbor League entities continued receiving municipal work according to the country
materials. A.R.M. fears those actors could locate a workplace or residence and share information
with Unit Four.

The testimony does not claim that contractors possess independent detention authority. The feared
path involves coordination: contractor leads, official data access, and a security unit able to
detain. Counsel confirms that overlapping steps should not be counted as separate complete risks.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Feared identification through the registry

A.R.M. learned through municipal work that household and employment fields could be queried by
regional liaisons. The country report describes a return-notification pathway. A.R.M. has not seen
a personal registry flag and does not claim a current database entry. The fear arises from the
system's capacity and the prior officers' knowledge of family details.

On entry, A.R.M. expects to use genuine documents and provide accurate information. The asserted
chain is that entry data reach a regional liaison, which can alert Unit Four or expose an address
to connected contractors. The judge notes that the expert will address system operation and that
A.R.M.'s testimony establishes only personal knowledge and concern.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Internal relocation testimony

A.R.M. knows people may live in other Kalyrian provinces without formal permission. A move could
avoid some Orin Vale acquaintances. It would not necessarily avoid a central entry record,
employment registration, or household-link query. A.R.M. has no close family outside Merrow and
would need registered work to support ordinary housing.

DHS asks whether anonymous cash lodging is possible. A.R.M. agrees it may be possible temporarily
but states that avoiding all services indefinitely is not a stable life plan. The judge reserves
the legal relevance of relocation and asks both parties to distinguish feasibility from certainty
of discovery.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Medical and symptom evidence

A.R.M. describes the clinic visit, current sleep disturbance, startle response, and wrist
discoloration. Counsel does not ask A.R.M. for a diagnosis. The medical evaluation is offered for
consistency and limitations. A.R.M. acknowledges maintaining daily activities and assisting with
the case despite symptoms.

The judge confirms that the testimony is not being used to identify a perpetrator through a scar.
The relevant personal evidence is the reported experience and observed aftermath, evaluated with
the clinic note, family testimony, and clinician's conditional conclusions.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Corroboration and unavailable evidence

A.R.M. identifies S.M.'s declaration, the family log, Pel's statement, the complaint receipt, and
the ledger versions as available corroboration. No video of detention, custody register, or signed
officer acknowledgment exists. A.R.M. asked counsel to seek an external-review file, but the
available extract ended June 30 with “regional response pending” at `RC-4`.

The absence of a custody record is consistent with A.R.M.'s account of an unregistered site but is
not independently decisive. A.R.M. states which facts depend solely on memory and does not
attribute any material outside the exhibits tendered at this hearing to the agency evidence.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Cross-examination on copied records

DHS establishes that A.R.M. copied municipal materials without a written instruction from a
supervisor. A.R.M. answers that internal compliance practice allowed preservation of audit trails
and that the copies were shown only to Pel and retained for a complaint. DHS suggests the ledger
change may have been an ordinary correction. A.R.M. agrees that the register alone does not prove
fraud.

DHS also notes that no Harbor League name appears in the threat sender label. A.R.M. agrees. The
association rests on the message content, vendor details, contact overlap, and timing. The judge
states that the concessions go to weight and do not remove the exhibits from evidence.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Cross-examination on reporting history

DHS asks why A.R.M. did not use the provincial inspector hotline after the Civic Integrity route
failed. A.R.M. did not know whether the hotline was independent and feared that another report
would reveal the household location. The formal hotline existed; A.R.M. did not test it.

DHS introduces the national anti-torture prohibition and discipline totals. A.R.M. acknowledges
the written safeguards and cannot identify every enforcement action. A.R.M. maintains that the
personal complaint was sent to the implicated regional command and produced no independent
interview. Both facts remain in the testimony.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Cross-examination on detention details

DHS explores the lack of an exact address for the storage compound and differences in the order of
questions. A.R.M. recalls a twenty-minute drive, machinery noise, and the metal door but could not
see a street sign. The sequence of two questions differs slightly from the declaration; the demand
for ledgers and Pel's identity remains consistent.

DHS confirms that the clinic registered A.R.M. on May 21 at 14:20, after release that morning, and
recorded the right ankle and left shoulder. The note does not name Unit Four. A.R.M. agrees. The
judge directs that the transcript preserve the evidentiary limit rather than infer causation from
the clinic notation alone.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Cross-examination on current conditions

DHS asks about lawful departure, the passage of time, formal prohibitions, and possible relocation.
A.R.M. agrees that no official blocked departure and that no direct threat was received after
leaving Kalyra before the agency hearing. Family messages reported continuing inquiries, but A.R.M.
did not personally witness them.

A.R.M. also agrees there is no proof of a present registry flag. The response is that the prior
detention, complaint route, contractor access, and registry capacity should be evaluated together.
The judge notes that expert testimony will address the institutional propositions.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Redirect on connected risk pathways

On redirect, counsel asks whether the feared sources operate separately. A.R.M. answers that a
contractor cannot use the entry registry alone and a registry clerk does not perform detention;
the concern is the chain connecting them to Unit Four. The earlier threat used ledger details,
officers knew about Pel, and the complaint returned to a regional command.

A.R.M. does not add percentages or claim certainty. Counsel confirms that the same potential
encounter should not be counted more than once merely because several actors enable it. DHS's
concessions and alternative explanations remain for the judge.

# Volume-one adjournment and exhibit status

The judge completes A.R.M.'s testimony at 4:18 p.m. and states that cross-examination may be
reopened only for newly identified impeachment material. The clerk confirms admission of P-1
through P-9 subject to the rulings stated in the transcript. That range includes the P-4 messages
and P-5 complaint-routing materials. No objection remains to P-7's authenticity, timeliness, or

The next session will hear S.M., Nadia Pel, Ilyan Varo, and DHS's institutional witness. The judge
