# Expert report of Dr. Ilyan Varo
## Scope, qualifications, and assignment

Ilyan Varo is a researcher retained to explain institutions and reporting practices in the
Republic of Kalyra. The assignment is limited to the period from January 2021 through June 7,
2024 and to three questions: how Directorate Unit Four receives information, how Harbor
League contractors interact with municipal officials, and what ordinarily happens when a local
officer is named in a complaint. The report does not decide whether A.R.M. is credible or whether
the legal standard for CAT protection is satisfied.

Varo's qualifications include twelve years studying public-procurement controls,
regional security commands, and complaint-routing systems. The file contains a curriculum
summary, a list of prior consulting engagements, and the compensation agreement.
Counsel supplied the pleadings and selected exhibits, but no party dictated the conclusions. Every
factual statement identifies its source class and confidence limit.

# Method and source hierarchy

The report uses four source classes. First are published Kalyrian directives and budget tables,
which reliably show formal structure but not necessarily field practice. Second are municipal
register extracts and routing logs authenticated by their custodians. Third are interviews with
former employees, coded to protect confidential identities and tested against dates or documents.
Fourth are press and civil-society summaries used only when at least two independent items describe
the same event.

Varo distinguishes a verified document from an inference. A verified routing code may establish
where a complaint traveled; it does not by itself establish what every recipient knew. Interview
accounts are reported with material inconsistencies rather than harmonized. No numerical torture
probability is assigned. The analysis instead identifies pathways, overlap, and evidence that
makes a pathway stronger or weaker.

# Government structure in Merrow Province

Kalyra's formal system divides national registry administration, provincial security, municipal
procurement, and civilian complaint intake. In Merrow Province, however, shared personnel and data
terminals connect those nominally separate offices. Municipal identity queries are logged through
a provincial gateway, and Unit Four maintains a liaison desk inside the regional registry office.
The liaison desk may request travel, employment, and household-link information using a case code.

The national charter bars detention for reporting procurement irregularities. Provincial manuals
also require an independent referral when a complaint accuses the receiving unit. Those rules are
relevant safeguards. The available routing logs nevertheless show that the independence rule was
not consistently followed in Orin Vale. The distinction between written structure and observed
operation is central to this report.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Directorate Unit Four mandate and access

Unit Four is described in Directive 18 as a municipal-security coordination unit for
infrastructure fraud, document theft, and threats to public systems. The directive permits short
investigative holds only at registered facilities and requires a custody entry, medical screening,
and notice to a supervisor. It does not authorize punishment, coercive questioning, or recovery of
documents for a private contractor.

Staffing rosters place Unit Four officers at both the Orin Vale records annex and the Merrow
regional command. Terminal audit samples show recurring queries for procurement clerks and their
household contacts. Some queries lack a corresponding registered case. The audit cannot establish
who stood behind every query, but it demonstrates that the unit had the practical ability to find
a former clerk and linked family members after a return to Kalyra.

# Procurement controls and invoice splitting

Orin Vale required secondary review when an invoice reached 25,000 units. The municipal ledger
extract shows six Harbor League invoices—NQL-3101 through NQL-3106—each below that threshold and
totaling 137,400 units. The first register version groups all six under maintenance cycle
`NQ-APR-A`; the corrected version removes that grouping and labels each invoice an urgent
standalone repair. The versions carry different approval paths, which is why the change could
attract attention from a records clerk.

The report does not determine that any named vendor committed fraud. It explains why copied ledger
versions could matter to officials and contractors: they reveal who approved the entries, when the
grouping changed, and whether a review threshold was avoided. Nadia Pel's authentication statement
supports the format of the routing and approval fields. The underlying motives remain for the
factfinder.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Harbor League network

Harbor League is an association of maintenance contractors, dispatch firms, and security
subcontractors. Membership lists overlap with municipal framework agreements, but membership alone
does not establish misconduct. Three companies share a dispatch office with a former Unit Four
logistics supervisor. Two vendor contact numbers also appear in access-request logs for the records
annex.

Interviews describe Harbor League messengers as using official-looking vehicles and knowing the
names of municipal complainants before those names became public. The accounts are consistent on
timing but differ on insignia and uniforms. Varo treats the overlap as evidence of access and
coordination, not proof that every contractor acts under color of law. The risk pathway becomes
material when combined with registry and Unit Four access.

# Central registry identification pathway

Kalyra's entry registry records passport scans, arrival terminals, declared destination regions,
and municipal identity queries. Returnees may be flagged for a routine address confirmation. A
separate witness-association flag can be added by a regional security liaison, but the policy
requires a recorded case number and supervisor approval.

Audit excerpts show that liaisons sometimes used generic infrastructure case codes that did not
identify an open matter. Once a flag exists, a regional notification may reach Unit Four before a
traveler leaves the terminal. The report cannot say that A.R.M. presently has such a flag. It can
say that A.R.M.'s prior municipal role and household links are data fields available to the same
regional network involved in the complaint history.

# Office of Civic Integrity intake protocol

The Office of Civic Integrity receives written complaints through municipal counters and a central
portal. Its manual requires staff to preserve the original, assign a neutral intake number, and
send allegations against security personnel to an external review desk. A complaint against a
contractor may be sent to municipal procurement unless it also alleges official participation.

Complaint `OCI-23-441` was accepted June 7, 2023 under initial route `IC-2`, a valid independent-
conduct intake pattern. The June 9 audit links it to procurement referral `CIV-23-184` through
employment code `ARM-RX-14`; the June 10 destination changes to `RC-4`, Merrow regional command.
The June 30 status remains “regional response pending.” Nadia Pel explains that staff were
instructed to use that command route when a complaint mentioned protected infrastructure. The
protocol therefore sent the matter back to officials implicated in the allegation even though the
written manual called for separation.

# Complaint outcomes in the sample

Varo reviewed forty complaint entries from Orin Vale during the source period. Twelve
named security personnel. Of those twelve, nine were routed to a regional command, two went to the
external review desk, and one was returned as incomplete. None of the nine regional referrals
contains an interview entry for the complainant. Four contain a notation that the named unit
reported no policy breach.

The sample is too small to estimate a national rate and was selected because routing data were
available. It nevertheless tests the claim that a formal complaint automatically produces
independent protection. In this municipal sample it often did not. The report does not infer the
outcome of a complaint merely from a blank field; it notes where the file closes without the steps
the manual describes.

# Formal prohibitions and enforcement evidence

Kalyrian law formally prohibits torture, unregistered detention, retaliation against procurement
reporters, and disclosure of complaint identities. The Ministry of Justice publishes annual
discipline totals and operates an inspector hotline. Those safeguards are genuine parts of the
conditions record and weigh against a categorical claim that no official remedy exists.

The same annual tables combine minor paperwork violations with serious abuse findings and omit the
originating unit. Provincial records show few referrals from the inspector hotline to an outside
prosecutor. Varo therefore treats the prohibitions as relevant but not conclusive. The question is
whether they function for a person whose complaint is routed to the same regional command and
whose identity can be queried through that command's liaison.

# Detention practices attributed to Unit Four

Former municipal employees described questioning at two sites: a registered regional interview
room and an unlisted storage compound near the records annex. Accounts concerning the compound
share details about a metal interior door, overhead machinery noise, and the absence of a custody
receipt. Dates span 2022 through early 2024. Two witnesses described demands for copied procurement
records; another described questioning about a contractor protest.

No interviewee identified A.R.M. by full name, and the report avoids constructing such a name.
The consistency supports a practice-level inference but does not prove A.R.M.'s particular two-night
detention. Medical observations, family testimony, and A.R.M.'s own account must be evaluated on
their own foundations. The conditions evidence supplies context for whether the described method
is plausible and officially connected.

# Monitoring of procurement witnesses

Municipal circular 7-C instructs supervisors to preserve access logs when an employee reports an
invoice irregularity. A separate security appendix permits monitoring when copied records are
classified as infrastructure-sensitive. Neither document authorizes threats. In practice, the
classification decision is made by a municipal-security liaison without an adversarial hearing.

Three former clerks reported home visits after copying ledger material. Two received written
requests to return documents; one reported only an oral demand. The cases differ in seriousness,
but each involved a registry or household query shortly before contact. This pattern supports a
possible information chain from municipal records to security actors. It does not establish that
all whistleblowers are detained or that return alone triggers harm.

# Internal relocation

Kalyra has no formal internal-passport barrier between provinces, and large cities offer anonymous
short-term lodging. Those facts could reduce exposure for a person known only to local actors. They
are less protective when central entry records and household links are part of the asserted chain.
The registry gateway permits a regional liaison to query a new municipal address after a service
registration or formal job application.

Varo identifies practical distinctions rather than deciding legal reasonableness. Avoiding public
employment and registered housing could reduce discoverability but would also limit lawful work,
health care, and ordinary residence. The report has no evidence that Unit Four conducts continuous
nationwide surveillance. It finds only that relocation does not sever every pathway described in
the record.

# Combined pathways and overlap

The three asserted sources are not independent boxes. A registry notification can identify a
returnee; a Harbor League contact can supply a household or workplace lead; Unit Four can use
official access to detain or question. Counting those as three separate events would exaggerate
risk where they lead to the same encounter. Ignoring the links would understate risk where one
actor enables another.

Varo maps the shared nodes: identity data, regional command routing, vendor contacts, and the prior
procurement complaint. The map contains no percentage and no legal conclusion. Its purpose is to
show which evidence bears on a combined chain and which evidence is genuinely distinct, such as a
separate threat delivered to S.M. outside the family residence.

# Protective response and duty to intervene

Formal responsibility for an unlawful detention lies with the provincial inspector, municipal
police, and the external review desk. The available records show no independent interview after
the June complaint and no request for the clinic source described by the family. The routing audit
ends June 30 with status “regional response pending” at Unit Four regional command.

This evidence may bear on awareness and response, but it does not prove the mental state of every
official. The report separates actual receipt from constructive awareness and avoids equating
bureaucratic delay with consent. The strongest evidence is the directed routing to the implicated
command combined with the absence of an independent interview or recorded response through the
audit cutoff.

# Events after A.R.M.'s departure

The source period includes threats reported to S.M., continued vendor access to municipal work,
and registry queries connected to the family residence. It does not include facts or materials
arising after the June 7, 2024 source cutoff. No later event is inferred from the patterns described
in this report, and the report addresses only the sources available when it was signed.

Varo identified no reliable report that Unit Four disbanded or lost registry access before the
source cutoff. A revised complaint circular was announced, but implementation records were not
available. The absence of a documented reform is not affirmative proof that practices remained
unchanged. It is recorded as an uncertainty relevant to how much weight to give older incidents.

# Limits of inference about A.R.M.

The report does not authenticate A.R.M.'s copied ledger, diagnose injuries, determine that a
detention occurred, or decide credibility. It accepts the asserted facts only to identify whether
the conditions evidence would make them institutionally coherent. It also does not infer that a
person who reports corruption necessarily faces torture.

Specific limits include incomplete provincial discipline data, coded interview identities, and a
source cutoff fixed without awaiting an adjudicative ruling. The Harbor League evidence shows contacts and
access, not a universal command structure. The registry evidence shows capability, not a present
flag. These limits remain material even when the pathways are considered together.
