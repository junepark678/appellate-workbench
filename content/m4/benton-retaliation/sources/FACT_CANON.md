# Benton synthetic fact canon

All identifiers and facts below are fictional and fixed for this exercise.

## People and organizations

- Leora Benton: Blue Cedar senior compliance audit manager; plaintiff/appellant.
- Blue Cedar Compliance, Inc.: Richmond-based compliance-services company;
  defendant/appellee.
- Draven Pike: vice president for operations and final RIF decisionmaker.
- Mira Solis: human-resources director and custodian of the RIF workbook.
- Naomi Voss: Benton's direct supervisor through termination.
- Tessa Wynn, Omar Ibarra, and Calvin Keene: retained comparator employees.
- Sable Finch: Blue Cedar finance director who supplied the savings target.
- Counsel, clerks, and the composite panel use plainly fictional or institutional labels.

## Immutable chronology

- 2025-01-17: Benton sends a written opposition complaint concerning sex-based allocation of
  high-visibility audit leads and the scoring consequences of that allocation.
- 2025-02-03: Benton files an EEOC charge identifying the same allocation and anticipated
  retaliation.
- 2025-02-10: Blue Cedar receives the EEOC participation notice; Solis acknowledges it.
- 2025-02-10–11: The packet is delivered to Pike on February 10; he opens it and replies on
  February 11, as shown by the routing log, with the February 12 calendar attachment providing
  separate context.
- 2025-02-14: Blue Cedar opens the RIF workbook with a stated savings target of $612,000.
- 2025-03-04 through 2025-03-14: calibration edits lower Benton's score while retained
  comparators' exception credits are preserved.
- 2025-03-24: Blue Cedar terminates Benton in the RIF, exactly six weeks after its February 10
  receipt of the EEOC notice.
- 2025-05-22: the EEOC issues the synthetic right-to-sue notice.
- 2025-06-16: Benton files the synthetic district action.
- 2025-08-13: the synthetic district court enters ECF 12, closing fact discovery on October 10,
  setting October 17 as the supplementation cutoff, fixing October 27/November 10/November 17
  summary-judgment briefing dates, and setting trial for January 26, 2026.
- 2025-09-29 through 2025-10-09: Solis, Wynn, Ibarra, Voss, and Blue Cedar's prepared Rule
  30(b)(6) designee are deposed before fact discovery closes.
- 2025-10-13 through 2025-10-15: Wynn recalls and reports a materially new subject from her
  March 7 conversation with Voss; Benton forwards it to counsel two days before the cutoff.
- 2025-10-17: the supplementation cutoff passes without disclosure of the new subject or a motion
  for leave.
- 2025-11-03 through 2025-11-10: Wynn signs a declaration, Benton first serves it on November 4,
  and Benton files it with her November 10 summary-judgment opposition.
- 2025-12-01: the district court excludes only the late subject and Wynn declaration under Rules
  26 and 37 while preserving her timely disclosed comparator testimony.
- 2025-12-19: the district court grants Blue Cedar summary judgment and enters final judgment.
- 2026-01-16: Benton files the notice of appeal.
- 2026-01-23: the district clerk certifies the 37-document, JA1–JA262 record for transmission.

## Fixed evidentiary wedge

The complete record contains Benton's opposition and participation, Pike's knowledge, the six-week
sequence, RIF versions, comparator criteria, contemporaneous scores, deposition testimony, and
changing explanations. It also contains one late declaration subject. Immediately after the March
7 calibration session, Voss asked Wynn: “Leora has made this an HR and EEOC problem; if her position
is removed, can you take Northline?” The statement is Voss's, not Pike's; it does not attribute any
words, retaliatory instruction, or protected-activity motive to Pike.

Wynn's October 2 deposition covers the possible Northline handoff but not that protected-activity
link. She does not deny the later statement. Counsel knows the new subject on October 15 but waits
for a signed declaration rather than serving a subject-only supplement or requesting leave by
October 17. The district court finds the declaration important but excludes only the new subject
and JA194–JA195 because surprise, cure, disruption, and counsel's explanation favor exclusion. It
does not invoke the sham-affidavit rule and leaves Wynn's disclosed comparator testimony intact.

The admitted record independently retains Pike's knowledge, six-week timing, Benton's 84-to-68
score change, preserved comparator credits, target-reaching alternatives, unresolved consistency
flags, redistributed work, and changing explanations. The district court nevertheless grants
summary judgment at pretext and but-for causation. No appellate disposition is authored in this
record-complete boundary.

The covered RIF population is fixed at twenty-three authorized positions: twenty-one filled and two
vacant. B-7 ends five filled positions and eliminates both vacancies, leaving sixteen filled
positions and no covered vacancy. Thus `23 = 21 + 2`, `16 = 21 - 5`, and `16 = 23 - 5 - 2`.

The no-knowledge counterfactual changes one premise only: Pike neither receives nor learns of the
opposition or EEOC participation before March 24. It does not change scores, comparator duties,
the savings target, termination timing, Wynn's late declaration, or the exclusion ruling. No
engine component may infer the answer; questions are authored, while the structured disposition
remains deferred.

The immutable complaint at JA9 calls a calendar attachment February 11. The controlling routing
records establish a February 11 open and reply and a February 12 calendar event. Successor
testimony consistently uses February 12 and treats the complaint wording only as a pleading-date
misdescription. Keene received the 2024 Lantern lead credit, while Wynn owned the later transition
through May 30. Benton's employment ended March 24; April 1 is only the modeled payroll-savings
effective date. Solis certified formula reconciliation, not consistent treatment: variance flags
remained open when Pike approved the selection under “business continuity.”
