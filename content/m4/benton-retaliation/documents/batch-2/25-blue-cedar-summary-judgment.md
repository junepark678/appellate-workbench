> **FICTIONAL TRAINING ARTIFACT — NOT FILED — NOT LEGAL ADVICE**

UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT FOR THE EASTERN DISTRICT OF VIRGINIA
Richmond Division — Civil Action No. SYN-EDVA-25-CV-0412

# BLUE CEDAR'S MEMORANDUM IN SUPPORT OF SUMMARY JUDGMENT

Filed: October 27, 2025

Blue Cedar Compliance, Inc. moves for summary judgment on Leora Benton's sole claim of Title VII
retaliation. The record establishes a genuine cost-reduction program, a documented comparison of
overlapping manager functions, and a final selection made to exceed a $612,000 annualized savings
target while maintaining client coverage. Benton engaged in protected activity, and Blue Cedar
does not contend otherwise on this motion. The disputed question is whether the evidence would
allow a reasonable factfinder to conclude that retaliation, rather than the reduction in force,
was the but-for cause of her selection.

Temporal proximity does not carry that burden after Blue Cedar has produced a legitimate reason.
The contemporaneous materials show that managers were scored, coverage alternatives were tested,
and seven authorized positions—five filled positions and two vacancies—were removed. Benton
disagrees with several calibration judgments, but disagreement with business judgment is not
proof that the stated reason was false. Blue Cedar therefore requests judgment on the claim and
entry of a separate final judgment after the Court resolves this motion.

# Procedure and issues presented

Benton filed this action on June 16, 2025. Blue Cedar answered, the parties exchanged initial
disclosures, and the Court entered a scheduling order that closed fact discovery on October 10.
The parties took the depositions of Benton, Draven Pike, Mira Solis, Tessa Wynn, Omar Ibarra,
Naomi Voss, and Blue Cedar through its designated representative. They also produced the native
RIF versions, scorecards, assignment records, performance measures, communications, and the
headcount ledger. Blue Cedar files this memorandum on October 27, within the dispositive-motion
schedule.

The complaint contains one retaliation count. Blue Cedar accepts for present purposes that the
January 17 written opposition and February 3 agency charge were protected and that termination
was materially adverse. The motion addresses the remaining causal question and the pretext stage
of the burden-shifting framework. The record does not present a separate discrimination claim,
challenge the lawfulness of reducing payroll, or request review of every assignment decision as
an independent violation. Nor does Blue Cedar ask the Court to choose between competing witness
accounts at trial. It submits that even crediting Benton's admissible evidence, the record does
not support a finding that the real savings and coverage explanation concealed retaliatory
selection.

# Governing Rule 56 and retaliation standards

Rule 56 directs entry of judgment when the movant shows no genuine dispute of material fact and
is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. The Court considers cited record materials, draws
reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, and does not weigh credibility. A disagreement is
material only if it could affect the result under the governing substantive rule; speculation or
a collection of immaterial differences does not create a trial issue.

Title VII prohibits an employer from taking adverse action because an employee opposed an
unlawful practice or participated in an agency proceeding. Under the pretext method described in
Foster, the plaintiff may establish a prima-facie causal showing without proving ultimate
causation at the first step. Once the employer supplies a legitimate reason, however, she must
identify evidence from which a reasonable factfinder could conclude that the stated reason was
not the real reason and that retaliation was a but-for cause.

Blue Cedar does not argue that the existence of a reduction in force automatically defeats a
retaliation claim. The question is narrower: whether the timing, score changes, comparator
differences, alternatives, and explanation records, considered together, permit the required
inference about the actual selection decision.

# The savings program was real and independently documented

Finance director Sable Finch supplied a $612,000 annualized payroll-and-benefit target. The
covered population contained twenty-three authorized operations and support positions:
twenty-one filled and two vacant. The approved B-7 scenario removed five filled positions and
eliminated both vacancies, leaving sixteen filled positions. Its projected savings were
$618,400. The affected jobs included Benton and four nonmanager positions; the vacancies had no
occupants. The arithmetic appears consistently in the workbook, headcount ledger, and corporate
testimony.

The process was not devised only for Benton. Finance identified the shortfall, operations
grouped roles with overlapping functions, and human resources preserved a read-only baseline and
dated calibration versions. Completion, continuity, credentials, availability, and role
uniqueness supplied the written criteria. The model did not mechanically terminate the lowest
score because management also had to retain viable client coverage, but manual changes remained
visible in the version log.

Benton agrees that Blue Cedar faced a real savings objective and does not claim the other four
terminations or two vacancy eliminations were fictitious. That undisputed economic setting does
not decide every inference, but it supplies a legitimate, contemporaneous reason for conducting
the selection process when Blue Cedar did.

# Pike's awareness does not establish retaliatory use

Blue Cedar does not seek judgment on the theory that Draven Pike was wholly unaware of protected
activity. The February routing log shows that Mira Solis sent him the agency packet, the system
recorded an open event, and Pike replied before attending the February 12 preservation and
staffing-input meeting. Pike also knew that Benton had raised an internal assignment concern.
Those facts permit an inference of awareness before his March 21 approval.

Awareness is not the same as retaliatory motive. The workbook opened after finance supplied its
target, and the written criteria did not include employee-relations status. Solis reminded the
calibration group that protected activity could not influence scoring. No contemporaneous entry
directs a deduction because Benton complained, and no participant testified that Pike instructed
the group to punish her. Pike approved a combination that exceeded the financial threshold and
kept each active function assigned.

The six-week interval between Blue Cedar's February 10 receipt and the March 24 termination is
relevant, but timing cannot erase the documented intervening work: baseline compilation, four
calibration sessions, a variance audit, coverage modeling, finance review, and final approval.
Knowledge and proximity therefore establish context, not the required conclusion about but-for
cause.

# Benton's calibration reflected forward coverage judgments

Benton's imported manager score was 84. Her final score was 68 after continuity decreased from
22 to 14, availability from 12 to 6, and role uniqueness from 9 to 7. Completion remained 27 and
credentials 14. Blue Cedar acknowledges that the reductions were calibration judgments, not
corrections to the underlying completion, credential, transition, or travel records.

The continuity adjustment reflected the conclusion that Northline and Oriole could be handed to
retained managers. The availability adjustment treated Benton's December short-notice decline as
relevant to forward coverage. The uniqueness adjustment reflected a plan to divide remediation
review between Keene and Voss. Solis questioned the magnitude and consistency of those choices,
and Pike approved them under the stated heading of business continuity.

That record may support criticism of the process, but criticism is not evidence that the
criteria were invented after the decision. Each field was part of the written protocol before
calibration, each change remained in the version history, and the proposed recipients actually
received Benton's work after termination. Rule 56 does not authorize a jury to replace operational
judgment merely because another weighting was possible; Benton must connect the judgments to a
retaliatory reason.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# The retained managers had documented coverage differences

Tessa Wynn, Omar Ibarra, and Calvin Keene shared core audit-manager functions with Benton, but
their current assignments were not identical. Wynn owned the Lantern transition scheduled
through May and received a continuity exception. Ibarra coordinated western-region work and had
offered to accept additional second-quarter travel. Keene maintained the audit-reporting script
library. Management treated those needs as supporting retention while planning to distribute
Benton's client and review work among the same managers.

Benton emphasizes that her Northline and Oriole transitions extended later than Lantern, that
she had more completed travel than Ibarra, and that the script library was accessible. Those are
fair comparisons. They show that the calibration group exercised judgment and that other weights
could have produced a different ranking. They do not establish that Wynn's active transition,
Ibarra's future availability, or Keene's automation responsibility was fictional.

The comparators also had different salaries, portfolios, credentials, and proposed roles in the
post-RIF plan. Title VII does not require the employer to prove that its decision was optimal. To
show pretext, the comparison must support a reasonable inference that Blue Cedar did not honestly
rely on the identified coverage distinctions. The undisputed existence of those distinctions
does not support that inference.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Alternative models do not show that B-7 was a sham

The scenario workbook confirms that Benton was not mathematically indispensable to reaching the
savings target. The nonmanager position base yielded $470,400. Substituting Wynn, Ibarra, or
Keene for Benton produced position savings below the target, but adding a separately modeled
$14,600 archive-hosting consolidation raised those combinations to $624,000, $617,000, and
$613,000. Blue Cedar does not dispute that each total crossed the stated threshold.

Those arithmetic alternatives did not preserve the same allocation of work. C-4 retained Benton,
so Northline, Oriole, and her remediation-quality work stayed with her; selecting Wynn required
reassignment only of Wynn's active Lantern duties. D-2 likewise retained Benton's work and
required reassignment only of Ibarra's planned western travel. E-3 retained Benton's work and
required transfer only of Keene's automation and reporting-script responsibilities. Operations
rejected those scenarios for continuity, summer travel coverage, and automation support,
respectively. B-7 instead eliminated Benton and distributed her work among retained staff.

Pike testified that he rejected the alternatives on coverage grounds, not because finance had
forbidden them. That concession prevents Blue Cedar from claiming mathematical necessity, but it
also identifies the actual choice presented. Evidence that a different qualifying choice existed
does not by itself establish retaliatory choice; Benton must show why the coverage explanation
was not honestly held.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# Timing and later detail do not displace the contemporaneous reason

The March 24 termination letter identified payroll savings and redistribution of redundant
management work. It did not accuse Benton of misconduct or poor performance. The April agency
response referred additionally to relative completion and availability, and Pike's July
custodian interview used the phrase client confidence. Blue Cedar maintains that those later
terms supplied detail about the calibrated coverage decision rather than new and incompatible
reasons.

The contemporaneous record supports that relationship. Completion and availability were two of
the five written criteria. Client continuity was a third. The March versions recorded Benton's
completion score, availability adjustment, transition dates, and redistribution plan before the
termination. The March 20 communication discussed salary and consolidation; the March 21 approval
adopted B-7 under business continuity. None of the later descriptions converted the termination
into discipline, and no record asserts that Benton failed a client engagement.

The Court should consider the sequence, but a concise termination letter need not reproduce every
workbook field. Where the earlier files already contain the later-described subjects, expanding
the explanation does not reasonably show that the savings-and-coverage reason was fabricated to
hide retaliation.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# No reasonable factfinder could find but-for retaliation

Benton can show protected activity, Pike's awareness, close timing, favorable performance,
manual score changes, different comparator credits, qualifying alternatives, redistributed work,
and explanations expressed with increasing detail. Considered together, those facts permit
scrutiny of Blue Cedar's decision. They do not supply evidence that protected activity caused the
selection or that the documented savings and coverage judgment was not honestly made.

The undisputed record contains a real target, a broader seven-position reduction, preserved
baseline and calibration versions, identified operational distinctions, and an approved model
that exceeded the target. The challenged adjustments concerned criteria written before the
selection, and the planned transfers occurred. No decision participant attributes a deduction or
selection instruction to Benton's opposition or agency charge. Drawing reasonable inferences for
Benton cannot substitute conjecture about motive for evidence of but-for causation.

Blue Cedar therefore requests summary judgment on the sole retaliation claim. It asks the Court
to decide only whether the admissible record presents a triable causal dispute, not to declare
that every calibration choice was the best available choice. Because that record does not meet
the ultimate Foster standard, judgment should be entered for Blue Cedar.
