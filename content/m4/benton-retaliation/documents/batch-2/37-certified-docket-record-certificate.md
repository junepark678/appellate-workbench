The Clerk certifies the following docket history for *Leora Benton v. Blue Cedar Compliance,
Inc.* through January 23, 2026. The entries identify the filings, discovery materials designated

exchanges and deposition transcripts are listed when designated even though they were not filed

administrative quality-control entries are not treated as separate substantive documents.

The district docket number is SYN-EDVA-25-CV-0412. The Fourth Circuit docket opened from the
notice of appeal is SYN-CA4-26-CV-4101. The caption remained Leora Benton v. Blue Cedar
Compliance, Inc. throughout the district action. The case involved one Title VII retaliation
claim, one plaintiff, and one defendant. Unless an entry states otherwise, filing and service
occurred electronically on the date shown, and no document was sealed. This certificate records
events; it does not add findings beyond the orders and judgment themselves.

# June through August 2025 — pleadings, service, and schedule

ECF No. 1, filed June 16, is Benton's complaint and jury demand alleging retaliation based on her
January opposition and EEOC participation. ECF No. 2 issued the summons June 17, and ECF No. 5
recorded service and Blue Cedar's appearance June 23. ECF No. 7, filed July 7, is Blue Cedar's
answer. It admits the real savings target, termination, protected communications, agency notice,
and Pike's decision authority while disputing knowledge, comparator similarity, pretext, and
but-for causation. No counterclaim or additional party was added.

ECF No. 11, filed August 11, is the joint Rule 26 report proposing discovery subjects and dates.
ECF No. 12, entered August 13, adopts the schedule. It sets August 15 for initial disclosures,
September 5 for substantial production, October 10 for the discovery close, and October 17 for
supplementation absent leave. Dispositive motions are due October 27, oppositions November 10,
and replies November 17; jury trial is set for January 26, 2026. The parties exchanged initial
disclosures August 15. Each identifies Wynn for comparator duties, metrics, scoring, continuity,
and retained assignments, but no motive-related conversation. Routine ECF Nos. 8 through 10 concern
counsel, electronic service, and the initial conference and add no separately designated evidence.

# September 2025 — production and first depositions

The parties certified substantial document production on September 5. The designated production
includes the administrative charge file, policies, opposition emails, EEOC routing records, RIF
workbook, comparator scorecards, calibration minutes, explanation communications, performance
metrics, and headcount ledger. Native workbook data and message metadata were made available for
inspection. No motion to compel remained pending after the certification.

Benton was deposed September 18. She testified about duties, opposition, EEOC participation,
records learned in discovery, score changes, comparators, termination, and the stated limits of
her Wynn disclosure. Pike was deposed September 25. He testified about his authority, the savings
target, receipt and awareness of the EEOC matter, calibration approval, comparator exceptions,
alternative scenarios, redistribution, and explanations. Solis was deposed September 29 about
employee-relations intake, routing, workbook custody, variance review, schedule administration,
and the distinction between witness identity and information subjects. The transcripts were
designated in full for the district record subject to ordinary objections.

# October 2025 — remaining depositions and close of discovery

Wynn was deposed October 2 concerning duties, source metrics, calibration score, continuity
exception, and assignments. She discussed a conditional Northline coverage question but did not
identify motive-related wording. Ibarra was deposed October 3 concerning travel, duties, metrics,

decision authority, explanations, and post-RIF organization.

Fact discovery closed October 10. Wynn reviewed her transcript October 13, contacted Benton
October 14, and Benton relayed a newly recalled subject to counsel October 15. The October 17
supplementation deadline passed without a subject correction or request for leave. Counsel
interviewed Wynn October 20. ECF Nos. 28 and 29, filed October 27, are Blue Cedar's motion for
summary judgment and supporting memorandum. ECF No. 30 is its statement of facts.

# November 4 through November 10 — new declaration and opposition

On November 3, Wynn signed a declaration recounting a March 7 conversation with Voss that included
a reference to Benton's HR and EEOC activity. Benton first served the declaration on November 4.
The service occurred after discovery and supplementation had closed and after Blue Cedar filed its
summary-judgment motion. The parties conferred about use of the new subject but did not reach an
agreement. No motion for leave to supplement was filed before Benton used the declaration.

ECF No. 34, filed November 10, is Benton's opposition to summary judgment. ECF No. 35 is Benton's
response and statement of disputed facts. The two-page Wynn declaration at ECF No. 36-1 is part of
the procedural record but later became subject to the Court's use ruling. The opposition relies
separately on admitted routing records, timing, score changes, comparator exceptions, alternative
scenarios, performance evidence, redistribution, and explanation communications. Electronic
service was completed that day. The filings did not add a new claim or request an extension of the
dispositive schedule.

# November 12 through November 24 — exclusion briefing and merits reply

ECF No. 37, filed November 12, is Blue Cedar's motion to exclude the newly asserted Wynn subject
under Rules 26 and 37. It requests no restriction on her timely disclosed comparator testimony.
The motion addresses disclosure history and the five considerations applied in *Benjamin v.
Sparks*. ECF No. 39, filed November 17, is Blue Cedar's summary-judgment reply. It treats the Wynn
declaration as disputed procedural material and argues that the admitted record does not support
but-for retaliation.

ECF No. 41, filed November 19, is Benton's opposition to exclusion and counsel's chronology
proffer. It does not replace or duplicate the declaration attached to the summary-judgment
opposition. Benton offers limited curative discovery and explains counsel's decision to await a
signed account. ECF No. 43, filed November 24, is Blue Cedar's exclusion reply. It argues that a
subject-level supplement could have been served before October 17 and that cure would require
reopening multiple witnesses. The Court took the use motion under advisement without oral
argument.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->
# December 2025 — use ruling, joint chart, opinion, and judgment

ECF No. 44, entered December 1, grants the exclusion motion in limited part. It bars use of the
new March 7 motive-related statement and declaration on the pending motion, at a hearing, or at
trial. It preserves Wynn's timely disclosed comparator testimony, imposes no fee, makes no
sham-affidavit ruling, and does not resolve credibility between Wynn and Voss. The ruling rests
solely on Rules 26 and 37 after consideration of surprise, cure, disruption, importance, and the
explanation for delay.

ECF No. 45, entered December 2, directs a joint admitted-record chart; ECF No. 46, filed December
5, supplies it and labels the excluded declaration procedural only. ECF No. 49, entered December 19, is the
memorandum opinion granting Blue Cedar summary judgment on the admitted record. ECF No. 50,
entered separately the same day, is final judgment for Blue Cedar on the sole claim. The Clerk
served both entries, terminated the pending motion, canceled the January trial, and closed the
district action.
