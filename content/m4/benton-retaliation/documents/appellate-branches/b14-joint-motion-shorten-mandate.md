# Counterfactual Joint Motion to Shorten Mandate
## Good cause

Good cause exists because the remanded claim requires prompt district-court case management. The prior schedule anticipated trial before the appeal, and the parties must now address a new pretrial sequence, witness availability, and the treatment of discovery completed before judgment. Earlier return of jurisdiction will permit the district court to set a conference and determine those matters without waiting through periods that neither party plans to use.

The requested date also allows the clerk time to process the motion and prepare the opinion and judgment for transmission. The parties do not ask this court to supervise remand scheduling or decide whether any discovery should reopen. They ask only that the district court regain authority to consider those questions. The affirmed exclusion component will remain binding, while the vacated summary judgment will no longer prevent further proceedings. Accelerating mandate changes the effective date of that existing disposition, not its content.

The parties recognize that issuance of mandate does not add the late Wynn subject to the merits record, resolve liability, compel a trial date, or prevent the district court from addressing matters left open by the opinion. They also recognize that the court may deny the motion or select a different date. If relief is denied, the ordinary post-judgment schedule will continue to govern unless another qualifying filing or order intervenes. No party asks the clerk to act before a court order grants shortening and releases mandate.
