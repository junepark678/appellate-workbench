# Claims, defenses, and disputed elements

Benton identifies one Title VII retaliation claim arising from protected opposition and EEOC
participation followed by RIF termination. Blue Cedar identifies a legitimate cost-reduction
defense and disputes knowledge, comparator similarity, pretext, and but-for causation.

The parties agree the RIF had a real savings target but disagree whether that fact explains the
selection of Benton. They agree the March 24 termination was adverse. They dispute the meaning of
the January 17 email, Pike's February knowledge, calibration changes, comparator exceptions, and
the relationship among Blue Cedar's explanations.

Discovery will separate admitted contemporaneous materials from later witness recollections.

# Electronically stored information

Blue Cedar will produce the RIF workbook in native spreadsheet form with formulas and revision
history, plus a rendered human-readable copy. Email will retain sender, recipient, sent time,
subject, and attachment relationships. Calendar exports will retain organizer and attendee data.

The parties agree not to treat duplicate exports as separate substantive documents. A checksum
may identify identical files, but the record will preserve a distinct dated version when values
or metadata changed.

The parties will redact personal identifiers under Rule 5.2 and seek protective treatment for
confidential client or personnel information when necessary. Privilege assertions require a log;
no category is designated sealed by this proposed plan.

# Depositions and unresolved evidentiary matters

The parties reserve depositions of Benton, Pike, Solis, Wynn, Ibarra, Voss, and a Blue Cedar Rule
30(b)(6) designee. Keene and Finch may be examined only if existing witnesses cannot authenticate

information was otherwise made known, substantially justified, or harmless. The district court
will resolve any concrete motion on a specific record.
