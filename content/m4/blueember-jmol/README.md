# Blue Ember JMOL authoring candidate

This tree prepares the schema-v2 root `us.ca4.m4.blueember-jmol@1.2.0` for the
fictional exercise *Blue Ember Biologics, LLC v. Granite Heron Logistics, Inc.*
(`SYN-WDNC-24-CV-0520`; `SYN-CA4-26-CV-4105`). It is synthetic training
material, not legal advice, a representation of a real dispute, an independently
reviewed pack, or a release-ready gold pack.

All parties, people, facilities, addresses, testimony, identifiers, events, and
dockets are fictional. Real courts appear only as institutional procedural actors.
The Fourth Circuit bench uses fictional/composite profiles supplied by an
exact-pinned foundation.

## Legal and modeling boundary

Both actual Rule 50(a) motions challenge causation only and affirmatively leave
mitigation for the jury. Granite Heron first raises mitigation in its Rule 50(b)
motion. The district court nonetheless grants mitigation JMOL, reducing the
$3.9 million verdict to $2.7 million, and conditionally grants a Rule 59 damages
retrial. The actual authored appellate disposition reverses the mitigation JMOL,
affirms the conditional new-trial ruling and damages-only scope, vacates the amended
judgment, and remands for a damages retrial.

B01 changes one premise on its never-filed docket: both Rule 50(a) motions expressly
preserve mitigation. The counterfactual disposition therefore affirms the mitigation
JMOL and amended judgment without deciding the conditional Rule 59 ruling or retrial
scope. Nothing in B01 or PA109–PA226 alters the actual district record or appeal.

The authority model distinguishes *Plyler*'s unavailable new Rule 50(b) basis from
*Wiener*'s permitted variation between a preserved Rule 50(b) issue and appellate
theory and *Gautier*'s same-issue incorporation. Those authored distinctions, the
damages arithmetic, and the separability conclusion remain subject to qualified
independent civil-trial and appellate review.
