## Quarantine, testing, and disposition

Blue Ember took physical receipt at 15:26, kept the sealed lot frozen, downloaded the monitoring record, and opened deviation BE-QA-DEV-2024-0912. A receiving signature did not constitute product release. Granite Heron later supplied operational records and an investigation.

Blue Ember rejected the validation lot on September 16. Confirmatory testing did not show that every vial lacked biological activity. The parties agree that retained measured activity and compliance with a validation-custody criterion are different subjects.

Blue Ember contends the excursion and resulting uncertainty made the lot unusable for its intended validation purpose. Granite Heron disputes that this quality decision proves the full causal and economic consequences claimed. It does not contend that its personnel could overrule Blue Ember’s quality organization.

The lot remained segregated during investigation and was later destroyed through controlled procedures. Blue Ember did not use it as conforming validation material or sell it. The parties disagree about possible research use, salvage value, the reasonableness of complete rejection, and the expense properly attributable to replacement.

## Cobalt Finch and Aurora Quay

## Claimed loss and mitigation arithmetic

Blue Ember’s asserted gross loss is 4.2 million dollars. Its direct subtotal is 2.2 million dollars, comprising 1.35 million dollars attributed to the rejected validation lot and 850,000 dollars attributed to replacement production and associated incremental work. Its remaining 2 million dollars is a bounded delay category.

Granite Heron disputes the foundation and recoverability of the gross computation. For its affirmative mitigation defense, it conditionally starts with Blue Ember’s model and asserts that Cobalt Finch would have avoided 1.8 million dollars of delay. It subtracts 300,000 dollars of incremental transfer and qualification cost to reach a 1.5 million dollar net reduction.

Applying the full proposed mitigation deduction to the gross request produces 2.7 million dollars. Applying a 300,000-dollar net deduction produces 3.9 million. Those are arithmetic scenarios, not stipulated outcomes.

The parties agree that a mitigation reduction, if any, concerns delay and should not revive the rejected lot. The jury must avoid counting the same expense, credit, alternative cost, or avoided loss twice. No insurance payment duplicating a claimed item had been identified before this filing.

## Matters remaining for decision and signatures

The disputed questions include material breach, causal relationship between the excursion and validation rejection, recoverable direct loss, certainty and foreseeability of delay, application of contractual limitations, reasonableness of Blue Ember’s response, feasibility of Cobalt Finch, and the net amount of any mitigation deduction.

Expert agreement on arithmetic does not establish the underlying premise. Brooks supports the 4.2 million gross model and rejects a mandatory 1.5 million reduction. Park concludes that the earlier alternative would have reduced the supported loss by that net amount. Both opinions remain subject to examination and the Court’s instructions.
