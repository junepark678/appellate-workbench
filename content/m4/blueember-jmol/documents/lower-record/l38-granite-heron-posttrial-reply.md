Cobalt Finch held an identified suite, possessed relevant equipment and personnel, found no fatal incompatibility, proposed specific transfer milestones, and estimated incremental cost. ## Independent new-trial ground and limited scope

Rule 59 permits the Court to weigh evidence and order a new trial when a verdict is against the clear weight or would work an injustice. That authority does not require the Rule 50 ground to have been preserved. The carrier timely joined its alternative request within twenty-eight days of the October 15 judgment.

The requested retrial should cover gross damages and mitigation, not liability or shipment causation. The special verdict fixed contract formation, breach, and causal responsibility before itemizing financial consequences. A new jury can apply those settled premises while reevaluating original-lot value, replacement expense, delay, and avoidable loss.

Gross delay and mitigation must be tried together because the latter cannot exceed caused loss and must account for incremental alternative cost. Keeping them together avoids mismatched assumptions. Retrying the Fletcher event would add expense and risk inconsistency without assisting the financial comparison. Granite Heron therefore maintains a bounded scope even though it seeks relief from the award.

## Conclusion

Granite Heron asks the Court to grant Rule 50(b), set the net mitigation deduction at 1.5 million dollars, and amend the 3.9-million-dollar judgment to 2.7 million dollars.
