## Causation argument concerning the lot

**MR. MERCER:** On original-lot value, no witness testified that every vial became inactive. The assay evidence points the other way. Blue Ember made an internal validation decision without an external directive, and Solis acknowledged possible research use. That discretionary decision cannot alone establish that transport legally caused loss of the full 1.35 million dollars.

On replacement expense, Blue Ember chose complete repetition instead of further testing or narrower use. Brooks assumed the disposition rather than evaluating its scientific necessity. His ledger can add numbers but cannot supply the missing causal proof.

The contract does not make Granite Heron an insurer of Blue Ember’s validation judgment. It requires evidence that the particular excursion naturally produced the claimed loss. An agreed no-tolerance specification may establish nonconformity, but the jury would have to speculate that the entire asset and every replacement item followed from the measured interval rather than Blue Ember’s independent policy.

We ask for judgment on the claim or, at minimum, on the original-lot and replacement categories for lack of a legally sufficient causal basis. Our motion does not depend on resolving witness credibility in Granite Heron’s favor; it addresses the absence of proof connecting retained activity to complete economic loss.
