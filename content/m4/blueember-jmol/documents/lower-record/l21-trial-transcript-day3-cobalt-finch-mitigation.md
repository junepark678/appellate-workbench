# Trial Transcript Day Three: Cobalt Finch and Mitigation

## October 8, 2025 — witness taken by agreement

**THE COURT:** Members of the jury, today’s evidence concerns an opportunity that arose after Blue Ember rejected the validation lot. The Court has allowed Granite Heron to examine Dr. Nia Calder first because Cobalt Finch is not a party and the witness has a limited schedule. This order does not change either party’s burden.

**MR. MERCER:** Granite Heron calls Dr. Nia Calder.

**CLERK:** Dr. Calder, do you swear that the testimony you give will be truthful?

**DR. CALDER:** I do.

**MR. MERCER:** Dr. Calder, are you offering opinions on carrier liability or the amount this jury should award?  
**DR. CALDER:** No. I am here to describe Cobalt Finch’s capacity, proposal, process, cost estimate, and uncertainty as they existed in October 2024.

**Q.** Is Cobalt Finch a litigant?  
**A.** No. It responded to third-party discovery and was reimbursed for reasonable time.

**Q.** Did counsel ask you to turn hindsight into a guarantee?  
**A.** No. I will distinguish what we knew, what we proposed to learn, and what no one could promise.

## Cobalt Finch and Dr. Calder’s role

**Q.** Where was the proposed work located?  
**A.** At Cobalt Finch Bioprocessing, 720 Meridian Commons Drive in Durham.

**Q.** What was your position?  
**A.** Director of Technical Operations. I evaluated incoming processes, coordinated suite availability, and developed transfer plans with quality, finance, and operations colleagues.

**Q.** Could you promise a regulatory outcome?

**Q.** Were you authorized to speak for Blue Ember’s quality organization?

**Q.** Did you inspect the rejected lot?

## The October 2 proposal

**Q.** When did Cobalt Finch issue its writing?  
**A.** October 2, 2024.

**Q.** What did it reserve?  
**A.** A Durham suite window for a campaign planned to start December 2, conditioned on prompt acceptance and completion of the transfer schedule.

**Q.** Was the date simply a marketing aspiration?

**Q.** What did the proposal include besides a date?

**Q.** Did it say BE-214 was already approved at Cobalt Finch?  
**A.** No. It explained that transfer and qualification work remained.

**Q.** Would signing make the final batch automatically releasable?  
**A.** No. It would reserve capacity and begin the work leading to a later readiness decision.

**Q.** Was an actual team available?  
**A.** We had planned technical, quality, and operations resources contingent on timely booking.

**Q.** Did the proposal identify that contingency openly?  
**A.** Yes. Staffing depended on reservation and prompt sponsor participation.

## Acceptance deadline and commitment

**Q.** What deadline did Cobalt Finch set?  
**A.** October 4 at 17:00 Eastern Daylight Time.

**Q.** Why only about forty-eight hours?  
**A.** Another sponsor wanted the opening, and the proposed transfer had to begin the following week. Holding scarce capacity prevents us from selling it elsewhere.

**Q.** What would acceptance do commercially?  
**A.** Reserve the suite, authorize defined preliminary work, commit Blue Ember to staged charges, and launch controlled document exchange.

**Q.** Would the entire estimate become nonrefundable at once?  
**A.** No. Charges accrued by milestone, although the capacity reservation and completed work could create cancellation liability.

**Q.** Could Blue Ember walk away without consequence after an audit?  
**A.** It could stop for a critical issue, but work performed and certain reserved-capacity charges would remain payable.

**Q.** Was a deadline extension guaranteed?  
**A.** No. We said a short extension might be possible if the competing customer had not committed.

**Q.** Did the other customer ultimately book the window?  
**A.** Yes, after Blue Ember declined and the hold ended.

**A.** Approximately 300,000 dollars of incremental transfer and qualification cost beyond an ordinary replacement campaign.

**Q.** What did it include?  
**A.** Transfer coordination, focused facility qualification, document adaptation, selected analytical work, training, engineering confirmation, and an expedited-capacity premium.

**Q.** Did it include the full cost of making a replacement lot?

**Q.** Was 300,000 a guaranteed cap?  
**A.** No. It depended on assumptions that detailed diligence could change.

**A.** No. The estimate appeared in the October proposal process.

**Q.** Could it be lower?  
**A.** Yes, if transfer occurred cleanly and contingencies were not used.

**Q.** Could it be higher?  
**A.** A material method or equipment issue could increase work. No such issue was known by the deadline.

**Q.** Did the estimate contain any amount for claimed lost contribution?  
**A.** No. It was a service-cost estimate, not a valuation of Blue Ember’s economic loss.

**Q.** Did uncertainty make the slot fictitious?

**Q.** Could a sponsor reasonably consider the option?  
**A.** Yes. We believed a prepared team could pursue it responsibly.

**Q.** Could a sponsor also prefer an established site?

**Q.** Did Cobalt Finch tell Blue Ember it would certainly save 1.8 million dollars?

**Q.** Did you tell it the campaign could not succeed?  
**A.** No. We proposed the work because we believed it was achievable.

**Q.** Would the word “elevated” ordinarily call for active risk management?  
**A.** Yes. It did not mean abandonment; it meant milestones, owners, and stop criteria mattered.
