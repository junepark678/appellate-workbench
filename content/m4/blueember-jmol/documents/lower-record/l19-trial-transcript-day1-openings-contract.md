## Blue Ember opening statement

**MS. HOLT:** A promise to carry a validation lot continuously between minus eighty and minus sixty degrees matters because the record of compliance is part of the product’s intended use. Granite Heron made that promise in a set of writings it reviewed and signed. Blue Ember tendered the identified lot, the assigned logger, and clear emergency contacts.

You will see the master agreement dated November 1, 2023, a quality protocol from January 2024, an August statement of work, the operative release specification, the purchase order, and the September route confirmation. Different documents serve different functions, but they point to one handling rule. The trailer target was minus seventy, and no excursion window was authorized.

Granite Heron will not deny that it held the shipment. It will not deny the recorded curve. The dispute begins with what that failure did. Dr. Mira Solis will explain why a validation lot needs reliable custody evidence, not merely a later sample that shows some remaining activity.

This is a contract case. Blue Ember does not ask you to punish a carrier or infer every damage from a single number. It asks you to follow the sequence from accepted obligation to rejected intended use and a measured replacement schedule.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->

**MS. HOLT:** On September 12, Granite Heron signed for lot BE214-VL-2409A at Solstice Gate Analytics. Its tractor left Greenville at 11:47. During the planned Fletcher stop, the tractor was shut down after a standby cable was inserted but before anyone verified that electricity reached the refrigeration unit.

The CryoTrace monitor crossed the warm boundary at 13:08. The trailer controller was discovered dark later. Refrigeration restarted only after the product had warmed substantially, and the logger recorded minus 42.8 degrees at its highest point. The lot did not return within the specified band until 14:02.

You will hear from Tess Holloway, Granite Heron’s own Transport Operations Manager. She will identify the failed power transition, missed telemetry route, and delayed call. You will also hear that the load reached Blue Ember at 15:26, after the warm interval had already occurred inside Granite Heron’s operation.

Blue Ember placed the material in quarantine rather than pretending nothing happened. Its quality team reviewed the curve, stability information, limited testing, and the lot’s validation purpose. It did not claim every vial was inert. It concluded that the lot could not supply the controlled validation evidence for which it was produced.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->

**MS. HOLT:** The financial proof will be divided so you can inspect it. First is 1.35 million dollars already embodied in the rejected lot. Second is 850,000 dollars of incremental replacement work. Third is 2 million dollars tied to a limited delay period. Kellan Brooks will show the sources and the adjustments he made.

Granite Heron raises a separate question about what Blue Ember did after rejection. Cobalt Finch offered a December manufacturing window, but BE-214 had never been run there. Detailed transfer, quality review, analytical responsibility, and readiness work remained. Blue Ember had forty-eight hours to make a commitment and chose the established Aurora Quay path beginning March 3.

You should evaluate that decision from October 4, not with hindsight. Dr. Nia Calder will confirm that Cobalt Finch was capable and that the slot was real. She will also confirm that the offer was conditional, the estimate could change, and success was not guaranteed. A real option can still carry material technical risk.

At the end, the verdict form will ask you for gross damages and any net mitigation deduction separately. Blue Ember trusts you to decide each question without using the later facility dispute to erase the earlier transport facts.

## Granite Heron opening statement

**MR. MERCER:** Granite Heron will not ask you to ignore its staging failure. The refrigeration interruption occurred, and our witnesses will explain it candidly. But an operational deviation is not a blank check. Blue Ember must prove that the contract failure caused each dollar it seeks.

The assay evidence did not show universal destruction. The sealed packaging was intact. Cooling resumed, and the product reached the destination within the delivery window. Blue Ember controlled whether material could serve another purpose, how much work to repeat, which facility to choose, and how to measure a development program whose future remained uncertain.

The contract itself makes those distinctions. It does not promise regulatory approval or scientific success. It requires proof of a natural connection, reasonable certainty, and losses within the parties’ contemplation. Declared value is not an automatic award, and a pre-event forecast is not a guarantee.

Listen closely to what the experts assume. Brooks begins with Blue Ember’s quality decision and its own forecasts. He organizes numbers, but the assumptions remain for you. Granite Heron will challenge causation and the asserted gross amount even while using those figures conditionally to show the effect of reasonable mitigation.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->

**MR. MERCER:** The October offer was not a vague possibility. Cobalt Finch held an actual Durham suite for a December 2 start. It had comparable development experience, a defined transfer sequence, an engineering confirmation, and a readiness gate. Blue Ember’s questions were legitimate, but the evidence will show no fatal incompatibility.

The alternative cost was approximately 300,000 dollars beyond an ordinary replacement campaign. Using Blue Ember’s own timing framework, the earlier path would have prevented 1.8 million dollars of claimed delay. Subtract the cost, and the net avoidable amount is 1.5 million dollars.

Reasonable mitigation does not require a guarantee. Businesses facing a loss commonly secure scarce capacity and complete diligence in stages. Blue Ember instead surrendered the window and waited for the option it preferred. Preference is not necessarily reasonable when another manageable course can prevent a much larger loss.

You will receive separate questions. Granite Heron asks you to hold Blue Ember to proof on causation and gross damages. If you reach mitigation, apply the evidence to the decision as it existed in early October. An admitted mistake during transport does not relieve the claimant of its own responsibilities afterward.

**THE COURT:** Thank you. Openings are complete. Plaintiff may call its first witness.

## Validation purpose and lot preparation

**Q.** Why could an environmental record affect validation use apart from chemical activity?  
**A.** Validation asks whether defined conditions were followed and documented. A limited sample may retain activity yet leave uncertainty about uniformity and the integrity of a campaign intended to demonstrate control.

**Q.** Does that mean any paperwork error destroys a lot?  
**A.** No. The quality team evaluates materiality. Here the monitor recorded a significant warm interval, not a missing initial.

**Q.** What was the lot number?  
**A.** BE214-VL-2409A.

**Q.** Who produced it?  
**A.** Solstice Gate Analytics at its Greenville facility.

**Q.** What accompanied it?  
**A.** Certificate COC-BE214-091224, sealed qualified shippers, the custody sheet, and the designated logger.

**Q.** Was exterior damage recorded at origin?  
**A.** No.

**Q.** Was the starting temperature within range?  
**A.** Yes according to the agreed records.

**Q.** Did Blue Ember ask Granite Heron to assess regulatory approval?  
**A.** No. It asked the carrier to maintain a physical condition and report the evidence.

**Q.** Could the sealed packaging make refrigeration unnecessary?  
**A.** No. The packaging supplied a qualified margin within an actively controlled transport system; it was not designed as indefinite passive storage.

<!-- PAGE BREAK -->

**Q.** Did Granite Heron know the lot’s purpose?  
**A.** Yes. “Validation lot” appears in the statement, purchase order, certificate references, and shipment descriptions.

**Q.** What did that communicate economically?  
**A.** That failure could require replacement work and affect a timed development sequence. It did not tell the carrier that every remote future event would be charged to it.

**Q.** Was Aurora Quay already part of planning?  
**A.** It was an established manufacturing relationship and later became the dependable replacement path, with a March 3, 2025 campaign.

**Q.** Did Blue Ember expect an excursion?  
**A.** No, but the quality agreement created contingency steps because controlled shipments can experience exceptions.

**Q.** Why list urgent contacts?  
**A.** Early consultation can reduce exposure, direct backup storage, or determine whether to return or continue. Delay limits available choices.

**Q.** Could recovery within the band remove the duty to report?  
**A.** No. Maximum, duration, cause, and response remain necessary to assess what happened.

**Q.** Was this testimony prepared from business records?  
**A.** Yes, together with my own involvement in the contracts and later decisions.
