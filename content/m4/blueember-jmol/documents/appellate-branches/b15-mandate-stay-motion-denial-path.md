## Rule 41 standard invoked

Rule 41(d)(2)(A) requires a motion to show that a petition for certiorari would present a substantial question and that good cause supports a stay. A stay may not exceed ninety days unless the petition is filed or the filing period is extended. If a petition is filed, the stay continues until the Supreme Court’s final disposition, subject to this Court’s authority and any required security.

Granite Heron submits that the substantial-question inquiry can consider an anticipated petition by another party because Rule 41 permits any party to move. It identifies the possible question as whether a court may direct a fixed mitigation deduction through Rule 50 when the affirmative defense was expressly preserved but the evidence of technical qualification, timing, and success remained disputed.

The branch opinion resolved that issue against Blue Ember. It held that the Cobalt Finch opportunity and economic evidence compelled the $1,500,000 net deduction once preservation was assumed. Granite Heron contends that a petition attacking that holding could affect the operative amended judgment and create avoidable enforcement activity if mandate issues first.

The movant does not claim that merely mentioning certiorari satisfies the rule. It relies on the legal importance of the Rule 50 sufficiency standard and on the possibility that Blue Ember may seek review. Granite Heron asks the Court to accept that possibility as adequate despite the absence of a commitment from the party that lost the appeal.

## Proposed certiorari question and present uncertainty

The potential petition would not contest the assumed Rule 50(a) language. The isolated branch premise expressly preserved mitigation, identified Cobalt Finch, stated the arithmetic, and supplied an opportunity to cure. Any Supreme Court question would concern the later merits determination: whether unresolved program qualification and execution risk permitted a rational jury to select less than the full defense model.

Granite Heron expects Blue Ember to argue that the panel treated disputed inferences as conclusive when it affirmed JMOL. Granite Heron would answer that the held suite, relevant capability, staged schedule, absence of a known fatal conflict, and bounded financial calculation established a legally sufficient floor. Those competing characterizations are preserved in the branch briefs and opinion.

The difficulty for this motion is present ownership of the proposed petition. Granite Heron has not identified a ruling adverse to it that it intends to challenge. It seeks to preserve the status quo because Blue Ember might petition, yet Blue Ember has not authorized Granite Heron to speak for it. The movant therefore requests a short administrative stay even if the Court finds the present showing insufficient for a full ninety-day stay.

Nothing in this proposed question concerns the actual causation-only motions or the separate actual appellate disposition. All facts and requested relief remain confined to this never-filed branch.
