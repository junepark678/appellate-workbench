# City of Norvale Department of Parks and Civic Spaces

## Pilot Permit and Enforcement History

Summary current through December 15, 2025

The Department prepared this summary from permit files, event reports, dispatch abstracts, and staff notes concerning the initial administration of Ordinance N-2025-18. The ordinance became effective October 1, 2025, and the standardized application was issued November 3. Because the period reviewed is short, the Department cautions against treating the small number of matters as a statistical measure of future administration. The summary identifies operational decisions without evaluating the message of any speaker.

Four organizers contacted the office about recurring events with compensated presentations. Two submitted complete applications on the new form, one submitted an incomplete site plan, and one sought guidance without filing. One complete application concerned a winter craft market with two paid demonstrations. It was approved with a condition preserving a twelve-foot emergency route after a fire-department measurement. The other concerned a food fair with three paid stage presentations and was approved after the organizer moved the stage away from a bus boarding area. Neither decision discussed the presenters' opinions.

The incomplete applicant received a written list of missing logistical items and later supplied them. No application was denied during the reviewed period. Staff did not issue a citation or close an event under the paid-speaker ordinance. The limited history therefore shows the mechanics of intake and conditions, but it does not establish how every late change, larger audience, or disputed risk would be treated.

## Piedmont Booksellers Guild Permit History

The Guild has received event permissions for Cedar Commons since 2019 under the City's general reservation rules. Archived forms describe free Saturday fairs with bookseller tables, a reading area, temporary chairs, and scheduled author appearances. Attendance estimates varied by season. Vendor counts generally fell between eighteen and twenty-eight, while programs identified four to seven authors receiving honoraria or travel support. Before October 2025, the City did not charge a separate amount for each paid author.

Event reports contain no violence, arrest, or property damage attributed to a Guild fair. Two dates generated minor medical calls: one attendee experienced heat-related dizziness and another reported a fall near a curb. Both calls were resolved without evacuation. Parks employees occasionally asked vendors to reopen a service path after tables or boxes shifted during setup. Transportation notes describe routine delay on Market Lane during unloading and short pedestrian queues near the crosswalk when readings ended.

Guild representatives have characterized these matters as ordinary conditions of a well-attended community event. City staff agree the history is generally peaceful but maintain that a lack of serious injury does not eliminate prospective planning needs. The records do not quantify every informal direction given by an employee or every moment of congestion. They also do not show that a speaker's topic caused a safety event. This summary preserves those limits so that the operational history is not overstated in either direction.

## Inquiries and Preliminary Administration

After Form PS-1 was published, the Guild's executive director asked whether its six proposed 2026 dates could be handled in one filing. Intake personnel answered that materials could be delivered together but that each event required a separate application, base payment, and decision. Staff explained that the dates were February 14, April 11, June 13, August 8, October 10, and December 12, and that conditions might differ because of seasonal programming or nearby work.

The director also asked how to list authors not yet confirmed. Staff advised that a complete submission must name each paid speaker then included in the proposed program and provide a short topic and time. They stated that a later substitution could be requested but could not promise approval without reviewing its timing and operational effect. The file does not contain a direction to omit a particular author or subject. It also does not contain a categorical assurance that every late addition will be accepted.

No final enforcement decision concerning a 2026 Guild fair had been entered as of December 15. Staff notes reflect discussion of the recurring base charges and the Guild's concern that a six-author program would require $510 for one date. The Department's position was that the published schedule controlled unless changed through lawful process. The Guild's position was that speaker-based charges and early disclosure would reduce participation even before a denial occurred. Those competing characterizations remained unresolved at the close of this summary.

## Limitations and Custodian Certification

The reviewed materials show neutral language in the two completed decisions and no recorded inquiry into a presenter's viewpoint. They also show that the pilot period involved few applications, no contested denial, and no completed Guild application for the announced 2026 series by December 15. It would therefore be inaccurate to conclude either that the ordinance can never burden a late literary program or that administrators have used it to discriminate against a message. The record is evidence of early practice, not a prediction of every later outcome.

The Department recognizes several gaps. Routine congestion was not measured by continuous counts. Informal communications were not always reduced to memoranda. Departmental time entries were recorded in broad task categories and do not isolate minutes spent on each paid speaker. The fixed fees consequently cannot be tested against every application's actual processing time from these files alone. Conversely, dispatch abstracts do not capture preventive staffing or employee attention that produced no call for service.

I certify that this four-part summary accurately reflects the permit and incident materials located after a reasonable search of Department files through December 15, 2025. Attachments maintained with the original include the two conditional approvals, the completeness notice, Guild reservation forms, event reports, and dispatch abstracts. The certification concerns the records reviewed and does not express a legal conclusion about Ordinance N-2025-18 or the constitutional claims later asserted by any person.
