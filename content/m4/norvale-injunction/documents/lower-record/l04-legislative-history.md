> **SYNTHETIC MUNICIPAL RECORD — FICTIONAL CITY — NOT AN ACTUAL GOVERNMENT RECORD**

# City of Norvale

## Staff Report, Meeting Minutes, and Legislative History for Ordinance N-2025-18

Administrative compilation dated November 3, 2025

The Department of Parks and Civic Spaces submitted this report to the City Council after reviewing recurring uses of Cedar Commons and two smaller municipal plazas. Staff described a growing practice of adding scheduled presentations to fairs and markets that had originally been permitted as dispersed vendor events. According to the report, a timed presentation can draw attendees from separate tables into one area for twenty to sixty minutes. The report did not identify any message or category of ideas as a concern. It focused on aisle width, loading activity, transit access, emergency lanes, sanitation, and personnel assignments.

Staff recommended a uniform advance process when an organizer compensates a scheduled speaker. The proposed trigger was intended to distinguish a planned program under the organizer's control from incidental conversation between a vendor and customer. Staff acknowledged that compensation is not a precise measure of crowd size and that unpaid speakers may also draw listeners. It selected the paid-speaker trigger because organizers ordinarily know and schedule compensated appearances and because the trigger could be administered from objective application information.

The report listed three goals: adequate time for interdepartmental review, a predictable method for recovering routine processing expense, and written explanations for restrictive decisions. It cautioned that disagreement with a speaker, fear of lawful controversy, and generalized community opposition could not support a permit restriction.

## Cedar Commons Operational Assessment

Cedar Commons occupies a downtown block bordered by Laurel Street, Market Lane, a public-library walk, and two bus boarding areas. The central lawn accommodates temporary tables, but the paved paths narrow near a fountain and service entrance. Parks personnel reported that recurring fairs typically remain within the site's posted capacity. Their principal concern was episodic concentration: attendees moving toward a reading area while vendors continued unloading or customers remained at adjacent tables.

Transportation staff recorded routine vehicle delay on Market Lane during several community events, especially when unloading extended beyond an approved window. The report did not attribute all congestion to speaking programs. It instead concluded that a presentation schedule could help determine when a crossing monitor or temporary loading restriction would be most useful. Fire personnel asked that every plan preserve the service entrance and a continuous emergency route. Emergency medical staff sought a reliable on-site contact rather than dedicated coverage for every ordinary fair.

The assessment specifically noted the Piedmont Booksellers Guild's history at the Commons. Its fairs had operated since 2019 without a violent incident known to the reporting departments. Staff recalled occasional crowding near readings, routine congestion, and two minor calls for medical assistance. Guild representatives disputed whether the crowding exceeded conditions at other markets. The report treated that disagreement as a reason to document future observations, not as proof that the Guild had operated unsafely.

## Timing and Fee Options Considered by Staff

The working group considered filing periods of thirty, forty-five, and sixty days. Parks staff favored thirty days for organizer flexibility. Transportation and police planners preferred sixty days during festival season. The report recommended forty-five days as a compromise that would usually encompass two departmental scheduling cycles while remaining shorter than the period required for some large street closures. Staff recognized that authors' travel and publication schedules can change late and recommended allowing substitutions when the approved operational plan and available resources could accommodate them.

Financial staff estimated the average intake, routing, site review, and written-decision work for a covered recurring event. Their blended estimate supported a $240 base amount. They separately estimated limited additional review for each scheduled presentation and recommended $45 per compensated speaker. The worksheet assumed that many applications would require less time and some would require more. It did not estimate the cost of police, barricades, sanitation, or equipment, which remained governed by other provisions when actually requested or required.

Two alternatives were rejected. A percentage of vendor revenue was considered difficult to verify and unrelated to administrative work. A fee based solely on expected attendance would not disclose when attendees were expected to concentrate. The report also rejected charging by subject category or predicted controversy as unlawful and administratively unreliable. Several staff members advised that a fixed per-speaker charge could burden small organizations even when marginal review was slight; the final recommendation retained the amount but proposed annual review of receipts and processing time.

## Public Workshop Summary

At a July 14, 2025 workshop, recurring-event organizers, nearby businesses, transit users, and residents addressed the proposal. A representative of the Piedmont Booksellers Guild explained that its fairs are open without charge and depend on modest author honoraria. The representative said booksellers often confirm a visiting writer within the last month, when a tour route or release date becomes certain. Requiring names and topics forty-five days ahead, the Guild argued, could eliminate useful last-minute programs and make small shops bear costs caused by expression rather than by total attendance.

Two nearby merchants supported clearer loading windows and advance notice of high-attendance programs. One stated that congestion was usually brief and did not justify excluding the fairs. A transit advocate asked the City to preserve access to both bus stops and suggested temporary signs during the busiest presentation. A civil-liberties speaker urged the Council to omit topic descriptions and to set a firm decision date. City counsel responded that a concise format description could assist operational review but warned that staff could not request a script, viewpoint, or political affiliation.

Police and fire representatives emphasized planning rather than enforcement. Neither described violence at a Guild fair. A fire supervisor explained that tables occasionally drifted into the service route after setup and recommended a clearly marked lane. Several speakers suggested applying the rule to any scheduled audience event, paid or unpaid. Staff answered that such a rule might sweep in informal community speech and would be harder to define consistently.

## Committee Consideration and Amendments

The Council's public-space committee considered revised language on July 28 and August 11, 2025. The first draft authorized restrictions for “anticipated public response.” After objections from organizers and counsel, the committee removed that phrase and substituted documented crowd, traffic, and municipal-resource risks. It added a sentence prohibiting reliance on hostility to a lawful message. The committee also required written reasons identifying evidence used for a condition or denial.

The committee discussed whether compensation should remain the coverage trigger. One member observed that an unpaid celebrity could attract more people than a paid local poet. Staff agreed but replied that total attendance and site capacity remained subject to ordinary rules, while the proposed ordinance addressed controlled programs at recurring events. Another member proposed exempting nonprofit organizations. Financial staff cautioned that operational review does not necessarily diminish with tax status. The committee retained uniform coverage but directed staff to identify any generally available accommodation on the application form.

The Guild requested a guaranteed late-substitution procedure and a decision deadline. The committee adopted language allowing prompt notice and review of a change but declined to guarantee approval after the filing cutoff. Members asked staff to respond quickly and to explain any rejection in writing. No fixed decision period was added. Minutes reflect concern that complex applications and interdepartmental conflicts vary, while public commenters warned that delay can chill a scheduled program. The committee forwarded the ordinance with that disagreement unresolved.

## Council Minutes of August 18, 2025

The Council took up Ordinance N-2025-18 in open session. Parks staff summarized the operational assessment, fee analysis, and committee amendments. The City Attorney advised that compensated literary and civic presentations remain protected speech and that administrators must apply the ordinance without regard to viewpoint. The Attorney further advised that written findings should connect each condition to identified facts and that anticipated disagreement with an event is not an operational risk.

Guild representatives repeated that six recurring fairs could produce substantial aggregate charges and that author schedules are unusually fluid. They asked the Council to use an attendance-based fee and a shorter deadline. A nearby resident supported the fairs but described delivery vans and listeners converging near Market Lane. Another resident stated that routine congestion should be managed through ordinary traffic rules. The minutes do not record a violent incident or a claim that any Guild author caused disorder.

Council members debated the fixed charge, the exemption for nonspeaking vendors, and the absence of a guaranteed late amendment. The sponsor stated that the measure regulated planning characteristics rather than literary content. An opposing member predicted that payment would become an arbitrary line between otherwise identical speakers. The ordinance passed by recorded majority vote with the written-reason and listener-hostility limitations intact. The Council directed the permitting office to publish a standardized application and fee worksheet before processing the next cycle of recurring-event requests.

## November 3 Administrative Implementation Note

The permitting office issued Form PS-1 and a fee worksheet on November 3, 2025. The form asks for event logistics, each paid speaker's name, a short topic or format description, and a proposed time. Instructions say that “author reading and audience questions” or a comparably brief phrase ordinarily suffices. They expressly state that no manuscript, summary of opinions, party affiliation, or endorsement of City policy is requested. Intake staff received a checklist separating completeness review from substantive risk review.

Training notes direct reviewers to cite a site measurement, conflicting reservation, prior-event record, departmental estimate, or other identified source before recommending a condition. They distinguish routine crowd presence from a documented problem and instruct staff to consider a narrower layout or timing measure before denial. The notes also state that substitutions after the deadline are not automatically accepted or rejected; the written response should explain whether completed planning can accommodate the change.

The administrative team did not revise the amounts or filing period selected by the Council. It identified the base assessment as $240 and the additional amount as $45 for each paid speaker. The implementation file contains objections that these charges may disproportionately affect small literary groups and staff responses that a fixed schedule promotes uniform treatment. This compilation preserves both positions. It records what the Council and staff considered through November 3, 2025, and does not purport to determine how the ordinance will operate in every later application.
