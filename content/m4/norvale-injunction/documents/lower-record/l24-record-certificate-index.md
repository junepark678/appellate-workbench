# United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit

## Piedmont Booksellers Guild v. City of Norvale

Appellate No. SYN-CA4-26-CV-4103
District Case No. SYN-DSC-26-CV-0107
Certificate of Record Completion and Record Index
Filed February 9, 2026

The district clerk certifies that the electronic record designated for this interlocutory appeal is complete as of February 9, 2026. The appeal concerns the January 23 preliminary injunction and the related January 28 denial of a district stay. The underlying civil action remains open. This certification neither converts the preliminary order into a final judgment nor transmits materials created outside the district proceedings.

The record begins with the verified complaint, the City's answer, and the municipal enactment materials. It includes Ordinance N-2025-18 with Form PS-1 and its fee schedule; the staff report, meeting minutes, and legislative history; and the parks department's pilot permit and enforcement history. These materials identify the forty-five-day filing rule, the $240 event charge, the $45 paid-speaker assessment, the requested names, topics, and times, the written-reasons requirement, and the documented-risk criteria that the parties dispute.

The supporting factual materials include declarations from the Guild's executive director, a member bookseller, and its fair logistics organizer; declarations from the City's parks and events director and police logistics officer; and the site photographs, map, calendar, reservation excerpts, and dispatch abstracts admitted for the preliminary-injunction record. The index reflects the documents as filed and does not adopt either party's characterization of peaceful event history, ordinary congestion, staffing needs, program flexibility, or economic burden.

The motion sequence consists of the Guild's January 5 preliminary-injunction motion and memorandum, the City's January 14 opposition, the Guild's January 16 reply, and the January 16 stipulation of facts and alternatives chart. The January 19 hearing transcript follows those submissions. The January 23 memorandum opinion and preliminary-injunction order completes the merits portion of the interlocutory record. The transcript and order are transmitted in their entirety, including the court's findings, discussion of the four preliminary-relief factors, operative restraint, and treatment of security.

The stay sequence consists of the City's January 26 motion for a district stay pending appeal, the Guild's January 27 opposition, the City's January 28 reply, and the January 28 order denying a stay. Those papers permit the appellate court to evaluate whether the City first sought relief below and how the originating court assessed the requested suspension. They do not include any appellate Rule 8 motion or later appellate order.

The initiation materials consist of the City's January 29 notice of appeal, the January 30 certified district docket, and this certificate and index. The notice invokes 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1). No party designated material beyond the twenty-four items listed in this index, and no sealed or ex parte material is included. The clerk has not excluded a designated item on confidentiality grounds.

No additional transcript is required for this appeal. The January 19 hearing is the only reported proceeding relevant to the preliminary injunction and has been filed in written form. Routine conferences not recorded on the docket did not generate a transcript. If the court of appeals requests a supplement, the district clerk will respond by separate certification rather than silently altering this completed index.
