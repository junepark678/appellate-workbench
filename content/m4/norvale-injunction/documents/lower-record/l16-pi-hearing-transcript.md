**CITY COUNSEL:** Does your office review ideas?

**VOSS:** No. A functional phrase is enough. We instruct staff not to seek a script, ideology, political affiliation, or position. A prediction that people may oppose lawful speech cannot justify a condition.

## Voss — Risk Review and Written Decisions

**CITY COUNSEL:** How does the documented-risk standard operate?

**VOSS:** A recommendation must point to an application fact, site condition, comparable-event record, conflict, departmental estimate, traffic assessment, or another identified source. The decision states the concern and explains how a condition addresses it. Staff should choose an adjustment rather than denial when reasonable.

**CITY COUNSEL:** Give examples without referring to the Guild's viewpoint.

**VOSS:** Preserve a measured emergency route, move chairs away from the service entrance, end unloading before a presentation, place signs near a bus approach, or coordinate a limited traffic post. A site may be unavailable because a complete earlier reservation controls. Those are operational grounds.

**CITY COUNSEL:** What has pilot administration shown?

**VOSS:** One craft market preserved a twelve-foot route after a fire measurement. A food fair moved its stage away from a bus boarding area. An incomplete application received a written omission notice. No pilot application was denied, and no decision discussed a presenter's opinions.

**THE COURT:** How many complete pilot files?

**VOSS:** Two complete applications under the form, plus the matter corrected after an omission and one guidance inquiry. It is a small sample.

**CITY COUNSEL:** How would you approach a late Guild substitution?

**VOSS:** I would compare the approved slot and plan. If time, duration, location, format, equipment, and audience remain stable, review should be limited. If those facts change, I may need input from another office. The answer must be written.

**THE COURT:** Is there a mandatory response period?

**VOSS:** No fixed period in the ordinance. We try to act promptly, but the text does not state a number.

## Voss — Fees and Administrative Limits

**CITY COUNSEL:** Explain the published amounts.

**VOSS:** The $240 base amount reflects average intake, completeness checking, site coordination, circulation when needed, organizer communication, and written-decision work. The $45 increment reflects review of a scheduled presentation and transitions. Financial staff used blended estimates because files vary.

**CITY COUNSEL:** Are the charges based on controversy?

**VOSS:** No. Employees cannot alter them according to subject, speaker, expected protest, or popularity. Receipts are credited to permitting administration. Requested officers, barricades, equipment, or extraordinary sanitation require another legal basis and cannot be duplicated.

**THE COURT:** Do you have time entries showing that each added paid speaker costs $45 to review?

**VOSS:** No. Our categories do not isolate each speaker. I cannot say the marginal work always equals exactly $45.

**THE COURT:** If speaker six replaces speaker five in the same slot, is there another $45?

**VOSS:** Not if the original withdraws and the plan is substantially unchanged. If it is an additional speaker, the schedule adds $45 even if operational changes are modest.

**CITY COUNSEL:** Why use averages rather than individual bills?

**VOSS:** Predictability and uniform treatment. Itemized time accounting would itself take resources and could make costs uncertain. Some applications use less staff time and some more.

**THE COURT:** Is the base amount imposed on the same event with no paid speakers?

**VOSS:** Not under this ordinance. Other general reservation or service charges may apply under separate rules. The $240 amount here is triggered by coverage under N-2025-18.

**CITY COUNSEL:** No further questions at this stage.

## Guild's Merits Argument

**GUILD COUNSEL:** Paid author readings are protected speech under *Billups*. Cedar Commons is public property used for expressive gatherings. The City can impose content-neutral time, place, and manner controls, but it bears the burden of showing appropriate tailoring. Its witnesses proved the importance of schedule and site facts; they also proved that the challenged proxies do not supply those facts.

Compensation is an objective line, but objectivity is not fit. It excludes an unpaid presentation that may draw hundreds and burdens a paid writer who draws twelve. Identity is a convenient label, not a risk variable. Literary topic is unnecessary when format states whether there will be questions, chairs, sound, or a demonstration. The $45 amount is not supported by speaker-specific time data, and the $240 amount turns on the same paid-speech coverage.

**THE COURT:** *Ward* says the City need not choose the least restrictive alternative. Why do your examples prove substantial overbreadth rather than merely another reasonable policy?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** Because the record identifies direct measures already available and contains no evidence that payment, identity, or subject materially advances the interest. In *Billups*, evidence and substantially less restrictive alternatives mattered. Here the City's own witnesses would plan from time, audience, format, and place if names changed.

**THE COURT:** Does the written-reason requirement narrow the discretion?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** It helps with a later operational condition. It does not undo the cost, original completeness rule, or lost opportunity while an amendment awaits review. The categories also do not create a prompt decision for an unchanged replacement.

**THE COURT:** You concede content neutrality?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** On this record, yes. Our likely-success argument is inadequate tailoring, not secret censorship.

**GUILD COUNSEL:** There is an averaged worksheet, but the same physical event would require ordinary review without paid authors. The City did not separate the incremental work caused by protected presentations from general site administration.

**THE COURT:** Address alternatives such as unpaid authors or another location.

**GUILD COUNSEL:** Unpaid speaking excludes writers unable to donate labor or travel. Fewer writers is less speech, not another channel. Another site lacks the established location and audience and remains subject to availability. None preserves the same timely literary exchange.

**THE COURT:** What authority supports irreparable harm before a denial?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** The present deadline, fee, and uncertain process govern invitations now. First Amendment loss occurs when the organizer forgoes or delays protected programming; it need not await an ideologically motivated rejection.

## City's Merits and Winter Argument

**CITY COUNSEL:** The ordinance regulates event structure, not message. A compensated scheduled appearance objectively identifies a program assembled by the organizer. Written decisions then turn on physical evidence. Pilot administration and the Guild's history show measured conditions, not censorship.

Presentations create known transition times. Forty-five days is an interdepartmental compromise. The averaged fee does not vary with controversy. Narrow tailoring permits imperfect classifications and does not demand every alternative counsel proposes.

## Court's Questions to the City and Guild Rebuttal

**THE COURT:** Counsel, *Billups* requires attention to evidence connecting the burden to the interest. If your own witnesses say direct logistical facts do the work, what is left beyond administrative convenience?

**CITY COUNSEL:** Administrability is part of a workable content-neutral system, but not our only answer. **THE COURT:** Could the City accept a provisional schedule identifier at forty-five days and require direct facts?

**THE COURT:** Does no final denial defeat as-applied relief if the automatic fee and deadline are already operating?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** Briefly, Your Honor. The City calls payment administrable because it is easy to observe, not because it advances traffic or access. **THE COURT:** If I preserve every direct site control, what irreparable municipal injury remains?

**GUILD COUNSEL:** The Guild accepts that specificity. It does not seek surprise changes or immunity from documented conditions.
