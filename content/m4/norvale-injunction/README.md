fictional exercise *Piedmont Booksellers Guild v. City of Norvale*
(`SYN-DSC-26-CV-0107`; `SYN-CA4-26-CV-4103`). It is synthetic training material,
not legal advice, a representation of a real dispute, an independently reviewed pack,
or a release-ready gold pack.

All people, associations, municipal actors, addresses, events, testimony, dockets, and
record facts are fictional. Real courts appear only as institutional procedural actors.

## Legal and modeling boundary

The exercise concerns a content-neutral permit triggered by paid speakers at the Guild's
recurring, free-to-attend Cedar Commons book fairs. The City advances real neutral
crowd-flow, traffic, emergency-access, sanitation, and staffing interests; the Guild
challenges the 45-day submission rule, names/topics/times disclosures, $240 event fee,
$45-per-speaker charge, and burdens on late author additions. Nonspeaking vendors are
exempt. The record preserves both the fairs' peaceful history and routine congestion,
including two minor medical calls and no violence, without treating either side's
characterization as mechanically established.

The actual authored disposition exercises 28 U.S.C. § 1292(a)(1) jurisdiction, rejects
the one-date mootness request, affirms entitlement to preliminary protection, and remands
only for a superseding Rule 65(c)/(d)-compliant order while interim protection remains.
The current structured-disposition schema has no separate field for non-vacatur or
continued protection; those limits therefore remain textually aligned in the opinion,
judgment, case plan, and evidence narrative. The adverse reversal is a counterfactual
plan only.

The workflow serializes district-first and appellate stay practice before
merits briefing. It does not claim concurrent emergency/merits clocks, infer
impracticability or mootness from arbitrary text, classify novel filings, or enforce a
clerk-versus-panel distinction beyond role authorization and exact authored bindings.
These are explicit limitations, not latent engine capabilities.

The three early Rule 8 counterfactuals are deliberately scoped endpoints. The threshold
denial (B02), impracticability denial (B05), and grant/dissolution sequence (B06–B07)
remain in the appellate-stay stage and do not ingest the actual notice, docketing,
record, briefing, or merits documents. The workflow abstracts the counterfactual notice
and docket setup needed to present those exercises; it does not claim a complete alternate
appellate docket for them.
